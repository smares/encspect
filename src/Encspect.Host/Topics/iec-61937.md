**The problem.** An IEC 60958 link (S/PDIF) carries two channels of PCM, but receivers also decode AC-3 or DTS themselves, so a player has to send the coded frames over a link built for PCM samples.

**How it works.** IEC 61937 packs the frames into data-bursts that fill the 16-bit samples of the link. A burst starts with a preamble of four 16-bit words. Pa and Pb are the sync words F872h and 4E1Fh, Pc is the burst-info and Pd gives the length of the payload. Pc holds the data type in bits 0 to 6, an error flag in bit 7 and the bitstream number in bits 13 to 15. The payload is one encoded audio frame, and stuffing fills the rest of the repetition period of the data type, which is counted in link frames: 1,536 for AC-3, 512, 1,024 or 2,048 for DTS types I to III, 6,144 for E-AC-3. Pd counts bits for AC-3 and DTS and bytes for E-AC-3.

Between the bursts of a stream the link may carry pause bursts (data type 3) and null data (data type 0), which hold no audio; the analyzer counts them and does not list them.

A WAVE file that records such a link holds the bursts as 16-bit stereo PCM samples, so the words are little-endian and the bytes of each payload come in swapped pairs. The link normally runs at the sampling rate of the coding, E-AC-3 excepted, which uses four times the rate.

Only a chain counts: a sync word alone is no burst, and the analyzer takes the samples for bursts when three in a row of one data type follow at the repetition period, after any silence or pause bursts.

**What you see.** The bursts with their preamble fields, the frames of the coding as the codec parser reads them, and findings for bursts that are missing, early, too long or of another type.
