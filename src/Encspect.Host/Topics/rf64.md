**The problem.** A RIFF size is 32 bits, so a WAV file cannot exceed 4 GiB, which a long recording of many channels at a high rate reaches in minutes.

**How it works.** RF64 is RIFF with the ID RF64 in place of RIFF and a ds64 chunk right after the header. The ds64 chunk holds the three sizes that overflow, each in 64 bits: the size of the whole RF64 chunk, of the data chunk and the number of samples that the fact chunk would state. Their 32-bit fields hold 0xFFFFFFFF, meaning that the value is in the ds64 chunk. A table in the ds64 chunk can give 64-bit sizes of other chunks the same way. Everything else is as in a WAV file, so the channel mask and the Broadcast Wave chunks work unchanged.

A recorder that does not know in advance whether a file will pass 4 GiB writes a JUNK chunk of the size of a ds64 chunk first. If the file grows past the limit, it renames the chunk to ds64, fills it in, changes RIFF to RF64 and sets the 32-bit sizes to 0xFFFFFFFF.

**What you see.** The RF64 chunk, the ds64 chunk with its sizes and table, and the sizes of the other chunks resolved through it. Findings appear where the ds64 chunk is missing or not first, or where its sizes differ from the ones in the chunks.
