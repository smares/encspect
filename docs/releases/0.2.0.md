The second release. It is marked as a pre-release. 0.1.0 read the structure of the lossy formats and WavPack and decoded nothing; 0.2.0 adds the other lossless formats and decodes most of them (not WMA Lossless, DTS-HD's lossless extension, TAK and OptimFROG), measures the decoded audio, and ships the command line tool as a NativeAOT executable.

## What is new since 0.1.0

- **Lossless formats, read and decoded:** FLAC (native, in Ogg and in Matroska), Monkey's Audio (versions 3.80 to 3.99 and 4.11 decoded), ALAC, TTA, and Dolby TrueHD and MLP are new in this release, read frame by frame and decoded. WAV (with RIFX, RF64 and BW64), Wave64 and AIFF (with AIFF-C) are new, with their PCM, floating-point, A-law and mu-law samples decoded. WMA Lossless is new, read frame by frame with its Stereo Coding tally and graph, and not decoded. WavPack, read since 0.1.0, is now decoded too. `--verify` compares the checksums that cover decoded audio for the codings that store one; `--measure` decodes the others.
  - **ALAC:** frame by frame in MP4 and Matroska; the decoder follows Apple's Apache-licensed reference decoder and is checked sample for sample against it ([alac.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/alac.md)).
  - **TTA:** the decoder follows a clean-room description of libtta-c, the TTA authors' own codec, and is checked sample for sample against the reference `tta` and FFmpeg ([tta.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/tta.md)).
  - **Not decoded:** WMA Lossless (planned for 0.5) and DTS-HD's lossless extension.
- **Measurements of the decoded audio** (`--measure`, and the Audio panels in the GUI): the average spectrum and the spectral cutoff, the spectrogram and waveform grids, the levels (sample peak, RMS level, DC offset, clipping runs counted at the bits in use, so that 16-bit audio padded to 24 bits is held to its own extremes, digital silence stretches of 10 ms or more, and bit-depth usage), loudness after ITU-R BS.1770-5 and EBU R 128, and the true peak. Every figure has a unit and a basis; none is a verdict.
  - **Deterministic:** the measurement code avoids fused multiply-add and the platform math library, so reports are byte-identical across platforms and in the NativeAOT build.
- **lossyWAV and lossyFLAC:** a WAV file whose `fact` chunk holds lossyWAV's text, and a FLAC or WavPack file that keeps that chunk, gets a "Processor" claim of lossyWAV with its version and settings as evidence. For FLAC and WavPack the wasted-bits statistic and the levels' "Low Bits Unused" figures show where low bits are zeroed, and a FLAC or WavPack stream whose wasted-bits pattern matches lossyWAV's blocks gets the same claim at low confidence, named without a version. No finding says that a file is not lossless ([wav.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/wav.md), [flac.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/flac.md)).
- **TAK and OptimFROG at header level:** the metadata objects and stream info of `.tak` with their CRC-24s, the encoder's preset and the stored WAV header; the blocks of `.ofr`, `.ofs` and `.ofc` with their CRC-32s, the encoder's mode and the stored WAV header. Neither has a decoder, and TAK's frames are not walked ([tak.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/tak.md), [optimfrog.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/optimfrog.md)).
- **Cross-checks:** every format's fixtures, the new lossless formats' included, are held in CI to other tools (FFmpeg and MediaInfo for all, and for OptimFROG, which neither reads, only that they find no audio; and each format's own tools: `flac` and `metaflac`, libwavpack and `wvunpack`, `mac`, `mpcdec`, `tta`, Apple's ALAC decoder, OptimFROG's library, TAK's `Takc`, `mkvinfo`, MP4Box, liba52, libdca and others; [development.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md)). New in 0.2.0:
  - **Decoded audio:** every decoder's PCM is compared sample for sample with a reference decoder: FLAC with `flac -d` and FFmpeg, WavPack with `wvunpack` and FFmpeg, Monkey's Audio with `mac` and FFmpeg, ALAC with Apple's reference decoder and FFmpeg, TTA with the reference `tta` and FFmpeg, TrueHD and MLP with FFmpeg, and the samples of WAV, Wave64 and AIFF with FFmpeg.
  - **Official decoders:** the analyzer's sample counts, trims and damaged-frame behavior are held to the committed records of Xiph's Opus and Vorbis decoders, the reference `tta`, FDK AAC, Core Audio and Media Foundation for AAC, and Core Audio for AC-3 and E-AC-3 (Windows runners have no AC-3 decoder) ([design](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/design/official-decoder-crosschecks.md)). DTS, DTS-HD, TrueHD and MLP have no freely available official decoder, so FFmpeg stays their reference.
- **Samples and lost samples:** one definition for every format ([samples-definition.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/design/samples-definition.md)). A stream's samples are the samples its units declare; units that are cut, refused or unreadable stay in the count and their samples are reported apart as lost samples (`lostSamples` in JSON, `Lost Samples` in CSV). A checksum failure alone does not make samples lost.
- **The same statistics in every format, and renamed ones:** statistics and series built for one format's concept now exist, under one name, in every format that has it: bitrate mode and distribution, stereo coding tallies, block usage, reservoir fill, stored, verified and failed checksums, coded bandwidth, and more. Several statistics were renamed to unify them; the old and new names are in [Names of statistics and series](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#names-of-statistics-and-series). For example, AAC's "Window Sequences" is now "Block Usage", Opus's "Pre-skip" statistic and Musepack's "Decoder Delay" are "Start Trimming", MPEG audio, Opus, Vorbis and Speex have "Bitrate Mode" in place of "IsVbr", and a stream's `isVbr` in JSON is true exactly where its Bitrate Mode is variable. A script that reads statistics by name needs the new names. Every rename is in "Renamed statistics" below.


- **Graphs of coding choices:** category series for the coding tools each frame uses (stereo coding, block usage and sizes, coded bandwidth, channel bit share), in MPEG audio, AAC, and AC-3 and E-AC-3, and in other formats where the parsers read them, grouped under the same headings in every format; the GUI adds a Coding Summary and a Frame Map that shows each frame as a square (see "In the GUI").
- **Container Samples:** "Container Samples" and "Container Duration" are the container's own stated length at the stream's output sample rate, with no codec trims, on one basis in MP4, Matroska, Ogg, ASF, FLAC (native files), WAV, Wave64 and AIFF.
- **Routes:** Matroska tracks of FLAC, WavPack, ALAC, TTA, TrueHD and MLP, and FLAC in Ogg, are analyzed (and decoded) as in their own files; WAV hands MPEG audio, AC-3, E-AC-3 and DTS to their parsers, and AC-3, E-AC-3, DTS and AAC carried as IEC 61937 bursts in the samples.
- **Output:** JSON gains `verification`, `measurements` and `lostSamples` where they apply (schema version 1, fields added only); the CSV gains the processor and lossy-part claims, `Lost Samples`, `Is Verified` and the measurement columns (among them `Clipping Runs` and `Digital Silence Stretches`), each next to the columns it belongs with ([cli.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/cli.md)).
- **Fixed since 0.1.0:** a Matroska file cut inside a laced block keeps the lace's whole frames and counts the cut frame's samples as lost, where 0.1.0 reported invalid lacing and read none of the block's frames.

## In the GUI

New since 0.1.0 ([gui.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/gui.md)):

- **Graphs tab:** the graphs are a list on the left and the chart on the right. The list has the same headings in every format ("Summary", "Stereo Coding", "Block Switching", "Coded Bandwidth", "Bit Distribution", "Other" and "Audio"), so files of different formats are compared the same way. 0.1.0 drew the bitrate and, for Layer III, the bit reservoir fill, per frame and per second, and the distribution of the bitrates.
- **View switcher:** an entry has one view switcher with its forms: "Over Time", "Smoothed" (only for a stream of more than 30 one-second intervals), "Frames" and "Summary" for a category series, and "Per Frame", "Over Time" and, for the bitrate, "Distribution" for a numeric quantity. The chosen form is remembered for the next entry that has it.
- **Coding choices:** a category series (stereo coding, block usage and sizes, coded bandwidth) is a 100% stacked area over time ("Over Time", or "Smoothed"), a strip with a cell for each frame ("Frames"), or a table of each category's count and share ("Summary"). The Coding Summary shows the tables of every category series of the stream at once.
- **Hover, click and selection in the new charts:** hovering an interval, a cell or a table row says its shares or category; clicking an interval selects its first frame, a cell its frame, and a table row or legend entry steps through the frames of that category; a frame selected in the tree is marked with a line across the over-time view and the strip.
- **Frame Map:** the last form of every entry that has a series per frame. It draws each frame as a square, in rows like text, so a whole file is seen at once. The squares reflow as the pane is resized and keep their place; a key under the map shows the colors; a frame with a finding has a ring; the selected frame has a ring of its own, and a lane beside the map shows the whole stream in proportion, with a mark for each finding.
- **Audio panels:** with Decode Audio, the heading "Audio" lists "Waveform", "Spectrogram", "Spectrum", "Loudness" and "Figures", drawn from the measurements of the decoded audio. Clicking the Waveform or the Spectrogram selects the frame that holds that time in the tree and hex view (for framed formats); the lookup works from the stream's frame offsets and its sample count and is exact only where the frames are equally long.
- **Verification:** the header shows "Verified: yes", "no" or "unknown" with the reason for each stream that stores checksums, and "Decode Audio" decodes, verifies and measures the file shown; the streams line gives a stream's lost samples.
- **Learn More:** under the chart of a category series and under each audio panel, a "Learn More" link opens the background article on what the graph shows; F1 opens the article of the chosen graph while the focus is in the graph area. The articles number 212 in this release against 127 in 0.1.0.
- **Keyboard and accessible names:** Tab reaches the stream choice, the entries, the view switcher and the chart; the arrow keys move a cursor over the points, cells or squares, Home and End go to the ends (Page Up and Page Down move the Frame Map by a screen), and Enter selects what the cursor shows. Each chart has a name and a help text that says in words what it shows and where the cursor is.

## The command line tool is a native executable

0.2.0 is the first release whose `encspect` is a NativeAOT executable (0.1.0's was a self-contained .NET single file with ReadyToRun code). The GUI is unchanged in kind: a single file that carries the .NET runtime.

- **Instruction sets on x64:** `encspect` needs a processor with SSSE3, SSE4.1, SSE4.2 and POPCNT (x86-64-v2) and refuses to start without them: it writes "The required instruction sets are not supported by the current CPU." and aborts.
- **Size and start:** the archive is about 30 MB smaller (the CLI file is 13.1 MB against 85.1 MB on linux-x64), and a tiny FLAC file takes 0.014 s from start to the end of the analysis, against 0.158 s for a ReadyToRun build of the same code, as 0.1.0 was packaged.
- **Linux:** the CLI needs glibc 2.27 or later and, unlike the GUI, neither libstdc++ nor libgcc_s (0.1.0's notes say both programs need them). The GUI's libraries are listed under "Linux libraries" below.
- **Two x64 builds:** the x64 downloads come twice.
  - `encspect-<version>-<platform>` is x86-64-v2: Intel Core since Nehalem, AMD since Bulldozer and Jaguar.
  - `encspect-<version>-<platform>-v3` is for processors with AVX2: Intel Core and Xeon since Haswell (2013), AMD Excavator (2015) and Zen; not the Pentium, Celeron and Atom models sold without AVX2. It holds the v3 CLI and the same GUI.
  - To choose, extract the `-v3` archive and run `encspect --version`; if it prints the version, use it.
- **Measured, linux-x64, v3 against v2** (Xeon at 2.8 GHz, minimum of 3 runs; Windows and macOS were not measured):
  - 493 MPEG fixtures: 2.371 s against 3.203 s (-26%).
  - DTS fixtures: 1.134 s against 1.623 s (-30%) for analysis, 1.207 s against 1.652 s (-27%) with `--verify`.
  - 456 FLAC fixtures with `--verify`: 1.043 s against 1.208 s (-14%).
  - Elsewhere the difference is within the noise: WavPack, Monkey's Audio, TrueHD and the single FLAC files change by 0 to 19% either way ([the table](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#measured-x86-64-v2-against-x86-64-v3)).
- **Against a ReadyToRun build of the same code, as 0.1.0 was packaged** (linux-x64): 456 FLAC fixtures with `--verify` -23%, WavPack default analysis -59%, Monkey's Audio `-c3000` analysis -73%; the 493 MPEG fixtures take the same time ([the table](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#measured-readytorun-against-nativeaot)).

## Running it

Unpack the archive, then:

```sh
./encspect song.flac --measure   # text tree with the measurements; --json, --ndjson and --csv for the other outputs
./encspect --verify song.flac    # also compares the checksums over decoded audio
./encspect --version
./encspect-gui                   # the GUI; it also takes files and folders as arguments
```

On Windows these are `encspect.exe` and `encspect-gui.exe`. Options are in the [command line](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/cli.md) and [GUI](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/gui.md) documentation.

## The builds are not signed

The programs have no Developer ID or Authenticode signature and are not notarized (the .NET SDK ad-hoc signs the macOS executables, which Apple's notarization does not count), so each system asks before it runs them.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Choose "More info", then "Run anyway". With Smart App Control on (Windows 11), Windows blocks programs it cannot judge, and unsigned ones among them, without a "Run anyway" option; Microsoft's "Smart App Control Frequently Asked Questions" says there is no way to bypass it for individual apps and that turning it off is the remedy.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses to start programs from a download that nobody it knows has signed. The GUI is a single executable, not an `.app` bundle. Follow Apple's route, described in the Mac User Guide page "Open a Mac app from an unknown developer" and the support article "Safely open apps on your Mac":
  1. Try to open the program once (double-click `encspect-gui`, or run `./encspect` in Terminal). macOS refuses.
  2. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click "Open Anyway". The button is there for about an hour after the attempt.
  3. Confirm with your login password, and open the program again.
  4. Repeat for the second program.

  The alternative in Terminal clears the quarantine attribute of the whole unpacked folder in one step. Use the folder's name for your download:

  ```sh
  xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine encspect-0.2.0-osx-arm64
  ```
- **Linux:** nothing is checked. The archive keeps the programs' executable bit; if a tool drops it, `chmod +x encspect encspect-gui` restores it.

## Linux libraries

Both programs need glibc 2.27 or later; the builds are not for musl-based systems such as Alpine. The command line tool needs nothing more. The GUI also needs libstdc++ and libgcc_s, as in 0.1.0; it draws with Skia and HarfBuzz, which are inside its file, but its Skia library links the system's libfontconfig, and its two windowing backends are managed code over the system's own libraries:

- **X11, the default** (also on a Wayland desktop, through XWayland): libfontconfig, libX11, libXrandr, libXi, libXext, libXfixes, libXcursor, libSM and libICE; libgtk-3 and libgdk-3 for the GTK file dialog when no desktop portal answers.
- **Wayland, experimental and opt-in** with `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND=wayland`: libfontconfig, libwayland-client, libwayland-cursor, libwayland-egl, libxkbcommon, libgbm and libdrm, with EGL for drawing.
- **File dialogs:** on X11 the system dialog comes from the XDG desktop portal, then GTK, then Avalonia's own dialog; on Wayland it is the portal, then Avalonia's own dialog.

Without its display, a forced backend ends with one line naming `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and `DISPLAY`, and exit code 1.

## Known limits

- **Not decoded:** the lossy formats; their decoders, and the coding graphs that need them, are planned for 0.3 to 0.5 (DTS's extensions later), so the GUI's audio panels appear only for the decoded lossless formats and PCM. WMA Lossless, DTS-HD's lossless extension, TAK and OptimFROG have no decoder.
- **Partly decoded:** Monkey's Audio versions 3.80 to 3.99 and 4.11 only, and TTA audio format 1 only; WavPack DSD, and WAV and AIFF streams that are not PCM, are not measured. FLAC and TrueHD in MP4 are not routed yet (planned for 0.4).
- **No playback and no tag editing:** by design.
- **Wayland:** native Wayland is experimental. A program cannot place its windows there, so the detail window's position is the compositor's choice.
- **Not run by a person:** the file dialogs, drag and drop and the clipboard on Wayland, and light and dark mode there, need a desktop session and were not exercised by the automated checks.
- **Not measured:** the speed of the NativeAOT build on Windows and macOS.
- **Unsigned builds** (above).
