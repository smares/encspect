The second release. It is marked as a pre-release. Where 0.1.0 read the structure of the files, 0.2.0 also decodes the lossless formats, measures the decoded audio, and ships the command line tool as a NativeAOT executable.

## What is new since 0.1.0

- **Lossless formats decoded and measured:** FLAC (native and in Ogg), WavPack, Monkey's Audio, Dolby TrueHD and MLP, WAV, Wave64 and AIFF have decoders; so do ALAC and TTA, new in this release. `--verify` compares the checksums that cover decoded audio for the codings that store one; `--measure` decodes the others.
  - **ALAC:** frame by frame in MP4 and Matroska; the decoder follows Apple's Apache-licensed reference decoder and is checked sample for sample against it ([alac.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/alac.md)).
  - **TTA:** the decoder follows a clean-room description of the format and is checked sample for sample against the reference `tta` and FFmpeg ([tta.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/tta.md)).
  - **Not decoded:** WMA Lossless (planned for 0.5) and DTS-HD's lossless extension.
- **Measurements of the decoded audio** (`--measure`, and the Audio panels in the GUI): the average spectrum and the spectral cutoff, the spectrogram and waveform grids, the levels (sample peak, RMS level, DC offset, clipping runs, digital silence stretches and bit-depth usage), loudness after ITU-R BS.1770-5 and EBU R 128, and the true peak. Every figure has a unit and a basis; none is a verdict.
  - **Two level figures added since the first measurements:** digital silence counts only stretches of 10 ms or more, and clipping is counted at the bits in use (16-bit audio padded to 24 bits is held to its own extremes). The CSV gains the columns `Clipping Runs` and `Digital Silence Stretches`.
  - **Deterministic:** the measurement code avoids fused multiply-add and the platform math library, so reports are byte-identical across platforms and in the NativeAOT build.
- **lossyWAV and lossyFLAC:** a WAV file whose `fact` chunk holds lossyWAV's text gets a "Processor" claim of lossyWAV with its version and settings as evidence. For FLAC and WavPack the wasted-bits statistic and the levels' "Low Bits Unused" figures show where low bits are zeroed, and a FLAC stream whose pattern matches lossyWAV's blocks gets the same claim at low confidence, named without a version. No finding says that a file is not lossless ([wav.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/wav.md), [flac.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/flac.md)).
- **TAK and OptimFROG at header level:** the metadata objects and stream info of `.tak` with their CRC-24s, the encoder's preset and the stored WAV header; the blocks of `.ofr`, `.ofs` and `.ofc` with their CRC-32s, the encoder's mode and the stored WAV header. Neither has a decoder, and TAK's frames are not walked ([tak.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/tak.md), [optimfrog.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/optimfrog.md)).
- **Cross-checks:** every format's fixtures, TAK's and OptimFROG's new ones included, are held in CI to other tools (FFmpeg and MediaInfo for all, and each format's own tools: `flac` and `metaflac`, libwavpack and `wvunpack`, `mac`, `mpcdec`, `tta`, Apple's ALAC decoder, OptimFROG's library, TAK's `Takc`, `mkvinfo`, MP4Box, liba52, libdca and others; [development.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md)). New in 0.2.0:
  - **Decoded audio:** every decoder's PCM is compared sample for sample with a reference decoder: FLAC with `flac -d` and FFmpeg, WavPack with `wvunpack`, Monkey's Audio with `mac`, TrueHD and MLP with FFmpeg, ALAC with Apple's reference decoder, TTA with the reference `tta` and FFmpeg.
  - **Official decoders of the lossy formats:** the analyzer's sample counts, trims and damaged-frame behavior are held to Xiph's Opus and Vorbis decoders, and, for AAC and AC-3, to FDK AAC, Core Audio and Media Foundation ([design](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/design/official-decoder-crosschecks.md)). DTS, DTS-HD, TrueHD and MLP have no freely available official decoder, so FFmpeg stays their reference.
- **Samples and lost samples:** one definition for every format ([samples-definition.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/design/samples-definition.md)). A stream's samples are the samples its units declare; units that are cut, refused or unreadable stay in the count and their samples are reported apart as lost samples (`lostSamples` in JSON, `Lost Samples` in CSV). A checksum failure alone does not make samples lost.
- **Gap-wave figures and renamed statistics:** statistics and series built for one format's concept now exist, under one name, in every format that has it: bitrate mode and distribution, stereo coding tallies, block usage, reservoir fill, stored, verified and failed checksums, coded bandwidth, and more. Several statistics were renamed to unify them; the old and new names are in [Names of statistics and series](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#names-of-statistics-and-series). For example, every format says "Sample Rate" and "Bits Per Sample", MPEG audio gains "Bitrate Mode" in place of its "IsVbr" statistic, and a stream's `isVbr` in JSON follows its stated bitrate mode in every format. A script that reads statistics by name needs the new names.
- **Container Samples:** "Container Samples" and "Container Duration" are the container's own stated length at the stream's output sample rate, with no codec trims, on one basis in MP4, Matroska, Ogg, FLAC (native files), WAV and AIFF.
- **Routes:** Matroska tracks of FLAC, WavPack, TrueHD and MLP are analyzed and decoded as in their own files.
- **Fixes from the milestone's reviews** (seven independent reviews, each followed by a review of its fixes; pull requests #254 to #260) fixed, before this release, among others:
  - a Matroska file cut inside a laced block keeps the lace's whole frames and counts the cut frame's samples as lost, where it reported invalid lacing and dropped them;
  - 16-bit FLAC padded to 24 bits whose encoder codes some frames as mid/side no longer gets a lossyWAV claim;
  - a headerless TTA track in Matroska with a crafted channel count no longer exhausts memory;
  - an ID3v1 tag after an OptimFROG file whose title has one or two characters is read as a tag again;
  - WMA Professional and WMA Lossless count the frames after a damaged frame or a cut, and OptimFROG counts the blocks it reads, as the samples definition says.

## What it reads

The formats and tags of 0.1.0, with these additions or changes ([details](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/formats/README.md)):

- FLAC, WAV, Wave64, BW64 and AIFF, Monkey's Audio, Dolby TrueHD and MLP: decoded and measured.
- ALAC in MP4 and Matroska, and TTA: parsed, decoded and measured.
- TAK and OptimFROG: header level.
- WMA Lossless: header level, as in 0.1.0.

## The command line tool is a native executable

0.2.0 is the first release whose `encspect` is a NativeAOT executable (0.1.0's was a self-contained .NET single file with ReadyToRun code). The GUI is unchanged in kind: a single file that carries the .NET runtime.

- **Instruction sets on x64:** `encspect` needs a processor with SSE4.2 and POPCNT (x86-64-v2) and refuses to start without them: it writes "The required instruction sets are not supported by the current CPU." and aborts. The .NET runtime inside it is the NativeAOT one.
- **Size and start:** the archive is about 30 MB smaller (the CLI file is 13.1 MB against 85.1 MB on linux-x64), and a tiny FLAC file takes 0.014 s from start to the end of the analysis against 0.158 s.
- **Linux:** the CLI needs glibc 2.27 or later and, unlike the GUI, neither libstdc++ nor libgcc_s (0.1.0's notes say both programs need them). The GUI's libraries are listed under "Linux libraries" below.
- **Two x64 builds:** the x64 downloads come twice.
  - `encspect-<version>-<platform>` is x86-64-v2: Intel Core since Nehalem, AMD since Bulldozer and Jaguar.
  - `encspect-<version>-<platform>-v3` is for processors with AVX2: Intel Haswell (2013) and later, AMD Excavator (2015) and Zen. It holds the v3 CLI and the same GUI.
  - To choose, extract the `-v3` archive and run `encspect --version`; if it prints the version, use it.
- **Measured, linux-x64, v3 against v2** (Xeon at 2.8 GHz, minimum of 3 runs; Windows and macOS were not measured):
  - 493 MPEG fixtures: 2.371 s against 3.203 s (-26%).
  - DTS fixtures: 1.134 s against 1.623 s (-30%) for analysis, 1.207 s against 1.652 s (-27%) with `--verify`.
  - 456 FLAC fixtures with `--verify`: 1.043 s against 1.208 s (-14%).
  - Elsewhere the difference is within the noise: WavPack, Monkey's Audio and TrueHD runs change by 0 to 19% either way ([the table](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#measured-x86-64-v2-against-x86-64-v3)).
- **Against 0.1.0's build** (ReadyToRun, linux-x64): 456 FLAC fixtures with `--verify` -23%, WavPack default analysis -59%, Monkey's Audio `-c3000` analysis -73%; the 493 MPEG fixtures take the same time ([the table](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/development.md#measured-readytorun-against-nativeaot)).

## Running it

Unpack the archive, then:

```sh
./encspect song.flac --measure   # text tree with the measurements; --json, --ndjson and --csv for the other outputs
./encspect --verify song.flac    # also compares the checksums over decoded audio
./encspect --version
./encspect-gui                   # the GUI; it also takes files and folders as arguments
```

On Windows these are `encspect.exe` and `encspect-gui.exe`. Options are in the [command line](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/cli.md) and [GUI](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.2.0/docs/gui.md) documentation.

## The builds are not signed

The programs have no Developer ID or Authenticode signature and are not notarized (the .NET SDK ad-hoc signs the macOS executables, which Apple's notarization does not count), so each system asks before it runs them.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Choose "More info", then "Run anyway". With Smart App Control on (Windows 11), Windows blocks programs it cannot judge, and unsigned ones among them, without a "Run anyway" option; Microsoft's "Smart App Control Frequently Asked Questions" says there is no way to bypass it for individual apps and that turning it off is the remedy.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses to start programs from a download that nobody it knows has signed. The GUI is a single executable, not an `.app` bundle. Follow Apple's route, described in the Mac User Guide page "Open a Mac app from an unknown developer" and the support article "Safely open apps on your Mac":
  1. Try to open the program once (double-click `encspect-gui`, or run `./encspect` in Terminal). macOS refuses.
  2. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click "Open Anyway". The button is there for about an hour after the attempt.
  3. Confirm with your login password, and open the program again.
  4. Repeat for the second program.

  The alternative in Terminal clears the quarantine attribute of the whole unpacked folder in one step. Use the folder's name for your download:

  ```sh
  xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine encspect-0.2.0-osx-arm64
  ```
- **Linux:** nothing is checked. The archive keeps the programs' executable bit; if a tool drops it, `chmod +x encspect encspect-gui` restores it.

## Linux libraries

Both programs need glibc 2.27 or later; the builds are not for musl-based systems such as Alpine. The command line tool needs nothing more. The GUI also needs libstdc++ and libgcc_s, as in 0.1.0; it draws with Skia and HarfBuzz, which are inside its file, but its Skia library links the system's libfontconfig, and its two windowing backends are managed code over the system's own libraries:

- **X11, the default** (also on a Wayland desktop, through XWayland): libfontconfig, libX11, libXrandr, libXi, libXext, libXfixes, libXcursor, libSM and libICE; libgtk-3 and libgdk-3 for the GTK file dialog when no desktop portal answers.
- **Wayland, experimental and opt-in** with `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND=wayland`: libfontconfig, libwayland-client, libwayland-cursor, libwayland-egl, libxkbcommon, libgbm and libdrm, with EGL for drawing.
- **File dialogs:** on X11 the system dialog comes from the XDG desktop portal, then GTK, then Avalonia's own dialog; on Wayland it is the portal, then Avalonia's own dialog.

Without its display, a forced backend ends with one line naming `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and `DISPLAY`, and exit code 1.

## Known limits

- **Not decoded:** WMA Lossless and DTS-HD's lossless extension; all lossy formats (their coding graphs and decoders are planned for 0.3 to 0.5, so the GUI's audio panels appear only for the decoded lossless formats). TAK and OptimFROG have no decoder.
- **No playback and no tag editing:** by design.
- **Wayland:** native Wayland is experimental. A program cannot place its windows there, so the detail window's position is the compositor's choice.
- **Not run by a person:** the file dialogs, drag and drop and the clipboard on Wayland, and light and dark mode there, need a desktop session and were not exercised by the automated checks.
- **Not measured:** the speed of the NativeAOT build on Windows and macOS.
- **Unsigned builds** (above).
