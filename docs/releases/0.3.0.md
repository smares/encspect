The third release. It is marked as a pre-release. 0.2.0 decoded and measured the lossless formats; 0.3.0 adds the first lossy decoders (MPEG audio Layers I to III with the MPEG-2 multichannel extension, Vorbis, Opus, AC-3 and E-AC-3), so files of these codings get the spectrum, cutoff, levels and loudness that lossless files have, and it reads their coded data in every analysis for graphs of coding choices. Five new figures describe the spectrum of decoded audio, and the finding codes of three formats are renamed.

## What is new since 0.2.0

- **Lossy decoders** (`--measure` and Decode Audio in the GUI; `--verify` alone decodes none of them, since none stores a checksum over decoded audio), for analysis only, with the output compared with reference decoders (below):
  - **MPEG audio:** Layers I, II and III of MPEG-1, MPEG-2 and MPEG-2.5, free format included. The decoder follows minimp3 (CC0), operation by operation. A Layer II stream that carries the MPEG-2 multichannel extension in its base frames is decoded to its channels (transmission channels, dynamic crosstalk, prediction, dematrixing and the LFE channel); a stream whose extension is a separate bitstream decodes its two base channels only, and its measurements say so ([mpeg-audio.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/formats/mpeg-audio.md#decoding)).
  - **Vorbis,** in Ogg and Matroska: floors 0 and 1 and residues 0 to 2, following libvorbis 1.3.7 where the specification leaves details open ([vorbis.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/formats/vorbis.md)).
  - **Opus,** in Ogg, Matroska and MP4 (the `Opus` sample entry with its `dOps` box is new): SILK, CELT and hybrid frames, redundant frames, multistream mappings and the output gain, following libopus 1.5.2 and RFC 8251. After a lost or empty frame the decoder starts over instead of concealing the loss as libopus does, and it withholds 80 ms ([opus.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/formats/opus.md)).
  - **AC-3 and E-AC-3,** written from A/52:2018: bit stream IDs up to 8 for AC-3; for E-AC-3 the independent substreams with the adaptive hybrid transform, spectral extension, enhanced coupling, transient pre-noise processing and the dependent substreams' channels, so a 7.1 program gives its eight channels. No dynamic range control and no downmix is applied. Frames at the reduced sample rates are not decoded ([ac3.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/formats/ac3.md)).
  - **Trims and gaps:** Vorbis and Opus start and end trimming (granule positions, the Opus pre-skip, and Matroska's DiscardPadding) reach the measurer as trimmed samples, and output that depends on a lost unit is withheld and reported as such. JSON gains `innerTrimmedSamples` where a trim lies inside the measured stream.
  - **Checksums:** the MPEG audio frame CRC-16 is a header checksum (it covers no sample), so an MPEG audio stream reads "unknown" with the CRC counts, or "no" where a CRC fails. AC-3 declares crc1 and crc2 and E-AC-3 its CRC; they cover coded data, and a stream whose frames all pass reads "verified (lossy stream)". `isLossy` is true in these verifications.
- **The coded data in every analysis:** the parsers read Layer III's scalefactors and Huffman data, Vorbis's floors and residues, every SILK and CELT frame of Opus (to its final range coder state) and every mantissa code of AC-3 and E-AC-3 (adaptive hybrid transform and enhanced coupling included), with or without a decoder session. A frame whose coded data are inconsistent is a finding (`ac-3.block-inconsistent`, `ac-3.mantissa-code-invalid`, `opus.frame-overrun`, `opus.redundancy-overrun`, `vorbis.walk-capped`, `mpeg-audio.huffman-overrun`, among others).
- **Coding graphs that the lossy decoders' coded data give,** under the names, units and axes the other formats use:
  - **Coded Bandwidth** for MPEG audio Layer III (0.2.0 had it for Layers I and II) and for Vorbis, from the residues only; Opus keeps the bandwidth its TOC bytes name.
  - **Block Sizes** and **Stereo Coding** for the CELT frames of Opus.
  - **Intensity Stereo Bound,** below.
- **Intensity Stereo Bound:** one numeric series in hertz (a quantity of its own, listed under "Stereo Coding" in the GUI), with a statistics node of the same name (lowest and highest hertz), in MPEG audio Layers I to III, AAC and Opus's CELT frames. It is the lowest frequency from which a frame codes intensity stereo ([coding-graphs.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/design/coding-graphs.md)).
- **Measurements of decoded audio, five new figures** (`--measure`, the Audio panels, JSON and CSV; each has a unit and a basis, none is a verdict; definitions and thresholds in [cli.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/cli.md)):
  - **Spectral Bandwidth:** the lowest frequency of a flat floor that reaches the top of the search, where the spectrum stands 20 dB or more above the floor, with the floor's level.
  - **Partial Cutoff:** the frequency at which the most frames show a ceiling of their own, from a per-frame count; printed in the text where the spectral cutoff is not found or lies more than 1 kHz away.
  - **Spectral Slope:** where the average spectrum is still falling at the top of the search, with the fall and the slope in dB per kHz; printed where the spectral cutoff is not found.
  - **Mid/Side:** a mid and a side spectrum of the pair of channels the layout names front left and front right (or of the two channels of a stream with no layout), each with its own cutoff; the GUI's Spectrum has a "Mid/Side" form for it.
  - **Spectral Holes:** the share of cells with signal whose power is at most a thousandth (30 dB) of the mean power of the 1 kHz of rows below it and of the 1 kHz above it, with the same share for the mid and the side spectrum.
  - **Encoder files:** the notes of [spectral-analysis.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/design/spectral-analysis.md) record what these figures read for stereo files from LAME 3.100 and other encoders, with the settings; they are observations of Encspect's test files and are not used as a rule by the analyzer.
- **JSON and CSV:** JSON (schema version 1, fields added only) gains `bandwidth`, `channelBandwidths`, `slope`, `channelSlopes`, `partialCutoff`, `holes` and `midSide` in `spectrum`, the `innerTrimmedSamples` field, and the verification of MPEG audio streams. The CSV gains `Spectral Bandwidth (Hz)`, `Spectral Slope (dB/kHz)`, `Partial Cutoff (Hz)`, `Side Cutoff (Hz)` and `Spectral Holes (%)` after `Spectral Cutoff (Hz)` ([cli.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/cli.md)).
- **Cross-checks against reference decoders:**
  - **PCM comparisons** (the output of the analyzer's decoders against another decoder's, with the bounds in [development.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/development.md)): MPEG audio against minimp3, FFmpeg and the reference output of ISO/IEC 13818-4 (14 of its 22 multichannel streams, within 1 LSB, with mctoolamed as a second column for all 22); Vorbis against libvorbis and FFmpeg; Opus against libopus 1.5.2 and the test vectors of RFC 6716 and RFC 8251, including the final range coder state of every packet; AC-3 against liba52 and FFmpeg; E-AC-3 against FFmpeg's decoder, whose output agrees with the analyzer's on the files without the adaptive hybrid transform and spectral extension and by band energy on the real files that use them, and hand-built files for each of those coding tools.
  - **Official decoders of AC-3 and E-AC-3:** Core Audio (the macOS job of the official-decoder workflow) and, run by hand on one Windows 10 notebook and never in CI, Microsoft's Dolby Digital Plus decoder (five runs). Where they differ from the analyzer and from each other, the differences and the decisions are in [development.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/development.md#e-ac-3-decoding): for example, Dolby's decoder reads enhanced coupling as amplitude-only as ETSI TS 102 366 V1.4.1 describes it, and the analyzer follows A/52:2018. The Vorbis reference is libvorbis, since FFmpeg's decoder disagrees with it on the one stream FFmpeg's own encoder wrote.
  - **Encoder-written fixtures,** new in 0.3.0: files from mctoolame and Philips' multichannel MP2 encoder, from LAME 3.20 to 3.89 and 3.95 to 3.98.4, and from libvorbis 1.0 to 1.2.3.
  - **The standards' text:** the clause numbers and tables that the MPEG audio code, docs and articles cite were checked against ISO/IEC 11172-3 and 13818-3, and the MPEG-2 multichannel extension's `dyn_cross_LR` cases follow 13818-3 2.5.2.15.
- **Background articles:** 225 articles in the GUI's "Learn More" and `--learn`, against 212 in 0.2.0.
- **Fixed since 0.2.0:**
  - A file whose only MPEG audio evidence is a chain of frames the analyzer distrusts (every CRC failing, for example) is no longer identified as MPEG audio. It is unrecognized, with the info finding `format.weak-candidate` naming the offset and frame count.
  - A file whose configuration changes has several streams, and each stream now gets the measurements of its own decoding session; before, the streams of one region shared the first stream's.
  - WavPack's audio before the block the decoding opens with, the WavPack sets that begin without the initial block flag, and the stretch of TrueHD after a stream parameters change are counted as lost samples; before, the measurements held neither these samples nor a lost-sample count for them, and no finding said so.
  - An Opus stream whose packets lie out of file order keeps its statistics inside its audio span (Speex and Vorbis start their span at the lowest packet offset too).

## Renamed finding codes

The finding codes of three formats now start with the format's name, written as in its other names. The code is the `code` of a finding in JSON; the structure of the report is unchanged and `schemaVersion` stays 1. A script that matches finding codes needs the new ones; the part after the dot is unchanged.

| Old prefix | New prefix | Codes renamed | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mpeg.` | `mpeg-audio.` | 61 | `mpeg.crc-mismatch` is `mpeg-audio.crc-mismatch` |
| `ac3.` (AC-3 and E-AC-3) | `ac-3.` | 47 | `ac3.block-invalid` is `ac-3.block-invalid` |
| `dtshd.` | `dts-hd.` | 14 | the codes of the `.dtshd` parser: `dtshd.stream-repeated` is `dts-hd.stream-repeated` |

- **Not renamed:** the names of nodes, statistics and codec IDs; `--only-format mpeg` and `--as mpeg` still work, since a format is named by the start of its name.
- **Removed, not renamed:** `ac3.aht-not-read`, `ac3.dependent-layout-changed` and `ac3.enhanced-coupling-not-read`, all three with the E-AC-3 decoder, which reads the adaptive hybrid transform and enhanced coupling and takes the dependent substreams' channels into the layout. New codes: `ac-3.dependent-format-differs`, `ac-3.mantissa-code-invalid`, `mpeg-audio.huffman-overrun`, `mpeg-audio.multichannel-reference-software-reading`, `mpeg-audio.multichannel-value-forbidden`, `aac.discard-padding-large`, `opus.discard-padding-large`, `opus.frame-overrun`, `opus.redundancy-overrun`, `mp4.opus-version`, `vorbis.discard-padding-large`, `vorbis.setup-too-large`, `vorbis.walk-capped` and `format.weak-candidate`.
- **Library users:** the MPEG audio project, assembly and namespace are `Encspect.Formats.MpegAudio` where they were `Encspect.Formats.Mpeg`. There are no NuGet packages yet ([library.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/library.md)).

## In the GUI

New since 0.2.0 ([gui.md](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/gui.md)):

- **Spectrum:** a "Mid/Side" form beside "Combined" and "Per Channel" for a stream with a pair of front channels, with a legend of "mid" and "side" and a dashed line for each one's cutoff.
- **Figures:** the lines for "Spectral Bandwidth", "Spectral Slope", "Partial Cutoff", "Mid/Side" and "Spectral Holes", each with its description as hover text.
- **Coding graphs:** the Graphs tab and the Frame Map list the new series under the headings the other formats use: "Coded Bandwidth" of Layer III and Vorbis, "Block Sizes", "Stereo Coding" and "Intensity Stereo Bound" of Opus, "Intensity Stereo Bound" of MPEG audio and AAC (under "Stereo Coding"), and the transmission channel series of the MPEG-2 multichannel extension.
- **Audio panels** appear for the files of the lossy formats above, with Decode Audio.

## What it reads

The formats and tags of 0.2.0, with these additions ([details](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/formats/README.md)):

- MPEG audio (Layers I to III, and the MPEG-2 multichannel extension where it rides in the base frames), Vorbis, Opus (also in MP4), AC-3 and E-AC-3: read, decoded and measured.
- Everything else as in 0.2.0: the lossless formats decoded and measured; AAC, Speex, Musepack, DTS, DTS-HD, the WMA codecs, TAK and OptimFROG not decoded.

## Running it

Unpack the archive, then:

```sh
./encspect song.mp3 --measure    # text tree with the measurements; --json, --ndjson and --csv for the other outputs
./encspect --verify song.flac    # also compares the checksums over decoded audio
./encspect --version
./encspect-gui                   # the GUI; it also takes files and folders as arguments
```

On Windows these are `encspect.exe` and `encspect-gui.exe`. Options are in the [command line](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/cli.md) and [GUI](https://github.com/smares/encspect/blob/v0.3.0/docs/gui.md) documentation.

## The builds are not signed

The programs have no Developer ID or Authenticode signature and are not notarized (the .NET SDK ad-hoc signs the macOS executables, which Apple's notarization does not count), so each system asks before it runs them.

- **Windows:** SmartScreen shows "Windows protected your PC". Choose "More info", then "Run anyway". With Smart App Control on (Windows 11), Windows blocks programs it cannot judge, and unsigned ones among them, without a "Run anyway" option; Microsoft's "Smart App Control Frequently Asked Questions" says there is no way to bypass it for individual apps and that turning it off is the remedy.
- **macOS:** Gatekeeper refuses to start programs from a download that nobody it knows has signed. The GUI is a single executable, not an `.app` bundle. Follow Apple's route, described in the Mac User Guide page "Open a Mac app from an unknown developer" and the support article "Safely open apps on your Mac":
  1. Try to open the program once (double-click `encspect-gui`, or run `./encspect` in Terminal). macOS refuses.
  2. Open System Settings, Privacy & Security, scroll to Security, and click "Open Anyway". The button is there for about an hour after the attempt.
  3. Confirm with your login password, and open the program again.
  4. Repeat for the second program.

  The alternative in Terminal clears the quarantine attribute of the whole unpacked folder in one step. Use the folder's name for your download:

  ```sh
  xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine encspect-0.3.0-osx-arm64
  ```
- **Linux:** nothing is checked. The archive keeps the programs' executable bit; if a tool drops it, `chmod +x encspect encspect-gui` restores it.

## Linux libraries

As in 0.2.0. Both programs need glibc 2.27 or later; the builds are not for musl-based systems such as Alpine. The command line tool needs nothing more. The GUI also needs libstdc++ and libgcc_s; it draws with Skia and HarfBuzz, which are inside its file, but its Skia library links the system's libfontconfig, and its two windowing backends are managed code over the system's own libraries:

- **X11, the default** (also on a Wayland desktop, through XWayland): libfontconfig, libX11, libXrandr, libXi, libXext, libXfixes, libXcursor, libSM and libICE; libgtk-3 and libgdk-3 for the GTK file dialog when no desktop portal answers.
- **Wayland, experimental and opt-in** with `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND=wayland`: libfontconfig, libwayland-client, libwayland-cursor, libwayland-egl, libxkbcommon, libgbm and libdrm, with EGL for drawing.
- **File dialogs:** on X11 the system dialog comes from the XDG desktop portal, then GTK, then Avalonia's own dialog; on Wayland it is the portal, then Avalonia's own dialog.

Without its display, a forced backend ends with one line naming `ENCSPECT_GUI_BACKEND`, `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` and `DISPLAY`, and exit code 1.

## Known limits

- **Not decoded:** AAC, Musepack, DTS and Speex (0.4 and 0.5), the WMA codecs (0.5), DTS-HD's lossless extension, TAK and OptimFROG; the GUI's audio panels appear only for the decoded codings.
- **Partly decoded:**
  - MPEG audio: the MPEG-2 multichannel extension's separate bitstream is never read (such a stream decodes its two base channels), and its multilingual channels are not decoded. The −90 degree phase shift and the dynamic expansion that 13818-3 allows after dematrixing are not applied.
  - AC-3 and E-AC-3: frames at the reduced sample rates (`bsid` 9 and 10, `fscod2`) are not decoded, no dynamic range control or downmix is applied, and the dependent substreams of two real 7.1 files have no second decoder to compare with.
  - Opus: mapping family 3 and unknown families are not decoded; lost frames are not concealed and in-band forward error correction is dropped; libopus differs from the analyzer for about 1,920 samples after a gap.
  - Vorbis: a setup header that states a very large codebook is not decoded.
  - Monkey's Audio versions 3.80 to 3.99 and 4.11 only, and TTA audio format 1 only; WavPack DSD, and WAV and AIFF streams that are not PCM, are not measured. FLAC and TrueHD in MP4 are not routed yet (planned for 0.4).
- **Not run in CI:** Dolby's decoder ran on one notebook, by hand, and its runs are records, not checks.
- **No playback and no tag editing:** by design.
- **Wayland:** native Wayland is experimental. A program cannot place its windows there, so the detail window's position is the compositor's choice.
- **Not run by a person:** the file dialogs, drag and drop and the clipboard on Wayland, and light and dark mode there, need a desktop session and were not exercised by the automated checks.
- **Unsigned builds** (above).
