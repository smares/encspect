# AC-3 and E-AC-3

Dolby Digital and Dolby Digital Plus, as raw files and in [MP4](mp4.md).

- **Parsers:** one format parser, "AC-3" (`src/Encspect.Formats.Ac3`), for raw files, and two codec parsers, "ac-3" and "e-ac-3", which MP4's `ac-3` and `ec-3` sample entries route their samples and their dac3 and dec3 boxes to.
- **Sources:** ATSC A/52:2018 with Annex D (the alternate bit stream syntax) and Annex E (E-AC-3), ETSI TS 102 366 V1.4.1 Annex F (MP4) and Annex H (EMDF), and ETSI TS 103 420 V1.2.1 (object audio). `scripts/fixtures/ac3_specs.py` downloads all three, pinned by their SHA-256, since the repository may not hold them.

## What is read

- **Frames:** sync on a chain of frames, with resync after damage.
  - A frame starts with the sync word 0x0B77. A header counts as sync only when two more frames follow it at the lengths their headers give, or its frames end exactly at the end of the data.
  - Then frames are followed by their lengths: a frame in which a new chain starts is cut short there, one the data ends inside is truncated, and anything else up to the next chain is a gap. Embedded tags split the data as they do for MPEG audio.
  - An E-AC-3 frame needs 6 words at the least for the fields every frame has (Annex E 2.3.1.3 says the smallest frmsiz values cannot hold a frame); shorter ones are no sync.
  - Bit stream IDs 0 to 8 are AC-3 (6 with Annex D's syntax), 11 to 16 E-AC-3. 9 and 10, the half- and quarter-rate AC-3 that ffmpeg and liba52 accept, are read up to the end of the bit stream information with an info finding, and so are E-AC-3's reduced sample rates (fscod 3).
- **Detection:** a chain at the start of the data (after zero bytes) is class 92, one after junk class 50.
  - The sniff also reports where the chain starts and how many of its first 16 frames pass their CRCs (crc1 and crc2 in AC-3).
  - The host ranks by class, then the earlier start; chains of one class that overlap are ranked by their verified frames first. A file with junk in front stays AC-3 or E-AC-3 where ADTS-looking or MPEG-looking bytes inside its frames chain too: its own chain starts first. Where the start of the file is cut off, so that the look-alike in the partial first frame starts before the first whole frame, its frames' CRCs hold and the look-alike's do not exist, and the AC-3 chain wins (`junk-adts-in-frames-cut.eac3`). See [architecture.md, "Format detection"](../architecture.md#format-detection).
- **Samples:** a program frame counts the audio blocks of its header at 256 samples each, a cut frame included. Lost Samples counts the program frames that are cut (by the end of the data or by the next sync word) or whose syntax breaks so that the rest is not read (`ac3.block-invalid`, `ac3.block-overrun`, `ac3.header-overrun`), whole: 1,536 for six blocks. A frame that fails a CRC is counted and not lost, and so are frames the analyzer does not read (bsid 9 and 10, a reduced sample rate, the adaptive hybrid transform, enhanced coupling).
- **CRCs:** crc1 and crc2, with "Failed Frame Checksums", the frames whose crc1 or crc2 fails (always listed, with no "Verified" companion: E-AC-3 frames have one CRC and AC-3 frames two).
- **Bit stream information:** every field.
  - AC-3's (A/52 5.3.2): bsid, bsmod, acmod, the mix levels, Dolby Surround mode, LFE, dialogue normalization, compression gain, language code, mixing level and room type, copyright, original, the time codes, additional information; Annex D's two extended information groups in their place.
  - E-AC-3's (Annex E 2.3.1): stream type, substream ID, frame size, sample rate codes, number of blocks, channel map, the mixing metadata with its program scales, external program scales, mix definition and the blocks' pan and mix data, the informational metadata, convsync and blkid, additional information.
  - audfrm (E-AC-3): exponent and snr offset strategies, the frame's exponent strategies (Table E2.10) or each block's, converter exponent strategies, AHT flags, transient pre-noise processing and spectral extension attenuation, and the block start information.
  - Mixing data that runs past its mixdeflen ends where mixdeflen says, with a finding at the group.
- **Audio blocks:** every block up to its mantissas. The parser steps over the mantissas by running the bit allocation itself; liba52 confirms where every block of the AC-3 fixtures ends, delta bit allocation sent in block 0 (and reused by the blocks after it) included (`derived-ffmpeg-stereo-192k-delta-block0`).
  - Every block: block switching, dither, dynamic range gain, spectral extension with its band structure and coordinates (E-AC-3), coupling (strategy, channels in coupling, band structure, phase flags, coordinates), rematrixing with each band's left/right or sum/difference, exponent strategies, bandwidth, the exponents (the groups are checked, not kept), the bit allocation parameters, snr offsets, coupling leak, delta bit allocation, the skip field, and the mantissas as one field (its codes are read as well, see "Decoding").
  - The bit allocation is A/52 7.2 (masking curve, delta bit allocation, the bap tables), and the mantissas are counted by their grouping (7.3.5); their codes are read in every analysis (the entropy pass), and where a session decodes they are stored for the decoder.
  - The checks of 7.10.2 are findings. A block that reuses what no earlier block of the frame set is read on with what it has (a warning); an undefined value, such as a bandwidth code above 60 or a reserved strategy, stops the reading of the frame's blocks there (also a warning).
  - Checks 1 to 28 are made; 18 to 20, a grouped mantissa code that no quantizer has (above 26 for the 3-level, 124 for the 5-level and 120 for the 11-level quantizer), are `ac3.block-inconsistent` at the block's mantissas, and the codes Tables 7.21 and 7.23 have no value for (7 in the 7-level and 15 in the 15-level quantizer, which 7.10.2 does not list) are `ac3.mantissa-code-invalid`, a warning that names the table. An E-AC-3 block 0 that sends no bit allocation parameters where bamode is 1 breaks check 12 as an AC-3 block does.
  - Fields that run past the frame's end are named by the part they are in: `ac3.block-overrun` for an audio block, `ac3.header-overrun` for the bit stream information or audfrm.
  - Auxiliary data (auxdatal, auxdatae) is read from the frame's end, and the error check holds crc2 (and crcrsv).
    - AC-3's crc1 covers the first 5/8 of the frame and crc2 the rest (7.10.1); E-AC-3's one CRC the whole frame after the sync word.
    - A mismatch is a warning, and the frame is still read.
  - A field that the end of the file cuts is `ac3.frame-truncated` alone when it fits its frame (`truncated-in-skip`); `ac3.block-overrun` is for a field or an audio block that does not fit the frame.
- **Streams and programs:** a program is an independent substream (AC-3 has one) with the dependent substreams after each of its frames; one stream is a run of a program's frames with one configuration.
  - A program frame is an independent frame with its dependent frames. It holds each dependent substream ID once (Annex E 2.3.1.2 allows at most 8); a dependent frame that repeats an ID ends the program frame, is left out of its figures and statistics (the Dependent Frames count too), and is counted in `ac3.dependent-excess`, as are the dependent frames after it up to the next independent frame.
  - A configuration that changes ends the stream and starts another one (a run of one independent substream with one configuration is one stream, as in the other formats): `ac3.stream-changed` is one Info finding with a count, and a configuration that returns starts a stream of its own. The analyzer keeps at most 64 streams, counting those of all independent substreams together. A frame that would start another is in no stream, and so are the dependent frames after it and every later frame of its independent substream; an independent substream that already has its stream and does not change keeps it. All of these are counted in one warning, `ac3.streams-limited`. The rule is the same for MPEG audio, ADTS and DTS ([architecture](../architecture.md#stream-changes), "Stream changes").
  - A program's statistics cover its own frames, from its first to its last in the first region it appears in (the whole region where the bytes are not contiguous).
  - The independent substreams of one raw file play at the same time, so their streams share one `playbackGroup` and a summary counts the longest once. Where a substream changes its configuration, no group is given: a group counts only its longest stream, so it cannot say that one substream's runs follow one another beside another substream; such a file stays ungrouped and the summary adds all its streams up (more than the playing time). In MP4 and Matroska the container replaces the number with its movie's or Segment's group when a track's streams all play together, so the substreams of a track add up no more than a raw file's.
- **MP4:** the dac3 and dec3 boxes, compared with the frames. A sample that ends inside its frame is `ac3.frame-truncated`, counted once for the file.
  - **Interpretation:** num_dep_sub is read as the number of dependent substreams of the independent substream, as the dec3 syntax (chan_loc follows when it is above 0) and ffmpeg's muxer (`movenc.c` counts them) use it. ETSI TS 102 366 F.6.2.12 words it as the substreamid of the last dependent substream before the next independent one, which is one less than the count; a box that follows the wording is reported as differing from the frames.
- **Object audio (E-AC-3):** the additional bit stream information's extension flag and complexity index, the EMDF containers with their payloads, and the header of the joint object coding payload. See "Object audio" below.

- **dmixmod 3:** A/52:2018 Annex D Table D2.2 reserves it (E-AC-3's mixing metadata has the same field and Annex E gives no table of its own), and the decoder should still reproduce audio. Dolby's own test files write it, and ffmpeg's `ac3defs.h` notes that encoders use it to indicate Dolby Pro Logic II. It is an info finding (`ac3.dmixmod-reserved`), not a warning. The other reserved values of the shared tables (the surround mix levels 0 to 2, dheadphonmod and roomtyp 3) stay warnings; no real file the project has uses them.

- **Why the sniff counts CRCs:** other formats' header-like bytes inside AC-3 frames.
  - FFmpeg's `csi_miami_stereo_128_spx.eac3` has 352 bytes of junk, then E-AC-3 whose frame headers hold bytes that read as an ADTS header, chaining three frames in several places. The AC-3 chain starts first, at the end of the junk, so it wins.
  - With the start of that file cut off (`junk-adts-in-frames-cut.eac3`), the look-alike in the partial first frame starts before the first whole frame and the two chains overlap: the AC-3 chain wins because its frames pass their CRCs.
  - `monsters_inc_2.0_192.ac3` with junk in front was taken for MPEG audio, because every 768-byte AC-3 frame holds a CRC-protected MPEG-2.5 Layer I header of 768 bytes; MPEG audio now ranks such a chain 30 when none of its CRCs holds.
  - A side effect: junk, then a real ADTS stream of fewer than eight frames or with data after it, then appended AC-3 frames, is AC-3, the one chain that holds up.
  - Junk passes a frame's CRC about once in 2^16 (E-AC-3) or 2^32 (AC-3), and a bit error or two in the first frames lowers the count only by those frames.
- **Memory:** each frame keeps a 16-byte record (its offset, where its kept bits lie in a shared store, their count and the CRC verdicts) and the bits it read, with the exponent groups, skip fields, mantissas and auxiliary data replaced by their lengths.
  - The nodes are built again from them 1,024 frames at a time when read.
  - A mono 96 kbps stream keeps 85 bytes per frame (peak 89 to 102), and the smallest frames, 12-byte E-AC-3 frames that overrun, 46 (peak 80).
  - On the 4-core test machine a 200 MB file of 3/2 + LFE at 448 kbps (111,616 frames) takes about 4 s and a peak of 191 MB as CSV with the published CLI, one of mono at 96 kbps (520,848 frames) about 4 s and 204 MB.
  - 12 MB of 12-byte frames (a million) peaks at 284 MB: that machine's processor reports a 260 MB cache, from which .NET sizes the collector's first generation, so the peak follows what the analysis allocates and drops, about 1.5 KB per such frame. With `DOTNET_GCgen0size` at 16 MB it is 172 MB.
- **Where the parser follows A/52 and ffmpeg 6.1 differs:**
  - ffmpeg decodes independent substream 0 only.
  - Where snroffststr is not 0, ffmpeg's E-AC-3 decoder reads a snroffste bit in block 0, which Table E1.4 leaves out there and takes as 1, and never reads it in blocks 1 to 5, where Table E1.4 has it; its encoder writes snroffststr 0 only.
  - Where frmfgaincode is 1 and a block sends no fast gain codes, Table E1.4 resets them to 4; ffmpeg keeps the previous block's.
  - ffmpeg counts the coupling channel's delta bit allocation bands from the coupling's first band (ac3.c, `ff_ac3_bit_alloc_calc_mask`), where A/52 7.2.2.6 and liba52 count from band 0; it rejects the derived AC-3 fixture's coupling segments.
  - ffmpeg 6.1's muxer writes dec3 with five reserved bits before num_dep_sub where F.6 has three, so its MP4 E-AC-3 with dependent substreams would read as disagreeing; its encoder writes none, so no fixture shows it.

## What is graphed

- **Bitrate Mode** (the mode the stream or its container states where it states one (the nominal bitrates of AC-3 and of DTS without extension substreams, 0x7FF in ADTS, the variable flag of ASF), else variable or constant by the units' own bitrates, unknown for one unit) and **Bitrate Distribution** (the units' bitrates counted in ranges of 32 kbps, with each range's share), beside the minimum and maximum bitrate.
A program frame is the unit of every series: an AC-3 syncframe, or an E-AC-3 independent frame with the dependent frames of its program frame. The series are recorded while the audio blocks are read, so each equals its statistics node, and they share the stream's time index and the x values of "Bitrate".

- **Flag series**, one value for each audio block read, in the order of the blocks (the independent frame's, then each dependent frame's). A stream has the series whose statistics node it has:
  - "Blocks With Block Switching" (concept Block Switching): whether any full bandwidth channel of the block has blksw set (A/52 5.4.3.1); in E-AC-3 a block of a frame whose blkswe is 0 sends no flags, and the audblk syntax of Annex E 2.2.4 sets blksw to 0, so it counts as without.
  - "Blocks With Coupling" (Stereo Coding): cplinu, which the last cplstre sets and a block keeps (5.4.3.7, 5.4.3.8); not for a stream whose independent frame has an audio coding mode below 2 (1/0 and 1+1), where the tally node does not exist either.
  - "Blocks With Rematrixing" (Stereo Coding): whether the flags in force have a band coded as sum and difference, mid/side (sum/difference) coding of the band (5.4.3.19, 5.4.3.20); 2/0 streams only, and a block of another audio coding mode in a program frame of a 2/0 program (a dependent frame) counts as without.
  - "Blocks With Spectral Extension" (Coded Bandwidth, since it states where coding stops): spxinu (Annex E 2.3.3.2), E-AC-3 only.
  - Each has two categories, "With ..." (the count of its statistics node) and "Without ..." (the other blocks read). The layout follows the units: an AC-3 stream whose program frames have six blocks or fewer has six values in each (a fixed layout, a block the frame has no value for is empty); an E-AC-3 stream, and an AC-3 core with E-AC-3 dependent frames (ETSI TS 102 366 J.1.5.2.2, the form of a program of more than 5.1 channels, which the stream is then reported as), have as many as their blocks make, up to 54 (a variable layout). The layout is chosen when the stream's figures are built, so a series never has fewer values than its tally counts.
- **Coded Bandwidth** (Hz, per program frame and per second, with the node "Coded Bandwidth": lowest, most common and highest in whole hertz): the highest end bin of any block read, over its full bandwidth channels and the coupling channel, times the sample rate over 512, rounded half to even. A channel's end is 37 + 3 × (chbwcod + 12) where it codes its own exponents, 37 + 12 × cplbegf where it is coupled, 25 + 12 × its begin sub-band (spx_begin_subbnd) in spectral extension (A/52 7.2.2.1; Annex E 3.3.3); sub-band ranges are written as Annex E counts them, spx_begin_subbnd to spx_end_subbnd with the end exclusive ("sub-bands 5 to 13" are 5 to 12), and the coupling channel's 37 + 12 × (cplendf + 3). The LFE channel is left out. It is a bound the stream states, not the encoder's lowpass.
- **The block tallies count blocks read to their end.** A block that breaks (an invalid field, mantissas past the frame, the adaptive hybrid transform or enhanced coupling) is in "Audio Blocks" but in none of the "Blocks With" counts (block switching, dynamic range gain, coupling, rematrixing, spectral extension, delta bit allocation), nor in "Bands Coded as Sum/Difference" or "Exponent Strategies", nor in a series. Earlier reports counted what the block that broke had stated until it broke.
- **Not graphed:** per-channel block switching, exponent strategies, dynamic range gain and delta bit allocation (design: later).
- **Spectral extension's band structure** is the default (A/52:2018 Annex E, Table E2.11; TS 102 366 E.1.10) at the first block of a frame that uses spectral extension without sending one (TS 102 366 E.1.3.3.7), as the analyzer reads it. FFmpeg 6.1.1 loads it only in block 0 (`ac3dec.c`, `decode_band_structure`, 812-813), so it misreads a frame whose spectral extension starts later without a structure of its own; `coding-eac3-spx-default` holds such frames.

## Decoding

The decoder (`src/Encspect.Formats.Ac3/Decoding`) is written from ATSC A/52:2018 alone (sections 5 to 7), with no other source; the design is [lossy-decoders.md](../design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-ac-3). It decodes AC-3 (bsid up to 8, Annex D's syntax included); E-AC-3 is the next step.

- **Which frames:** the independent frames of an AC-3 program, whether in a raw file or handed over by a container (MP4, Matroska, ASF, WAV). A program is one run of frames with one configuration, as for the report's streams; it gets one session, started at its first frame, and a change of configuration starts another. A frame of bsid 9 or 10, E-AC-3 frames and the dependent substreams after an AC-3 core are not decoded: a program that starts with a bsid above 8 has no session, and a core with dependent frames outputs the core's channels only.
- **What the parser hands over:** the walk of every audio block (`FrameSyntax`) reads the mantissa codes with the same code in every analysis and, where a session decodes, writes each block's side information (block switching and dither flags, coupling with its band structure and coordinates, the rematrixing flags) and the exponent, bit allocation pointer and mantissa code of every bin into the buffers the session lends. The decoder reads no coded bit.
- **Reconstruction** (A/52 7.3 to 7.5, 7.9):
  - the mantissas are dequantized (the symmetric quantizers of Tables 7.19 to 7.23 and the two's complement fractions of the asymmetric ones) and shifted by their exponents;
  - a zero-bit mantissa of a channel whose dither flag is set becomes noise of a generator with a fixed seed (a 32-bit linear congruential generator, uniform between -0.707 and 0.707, A/52 7.3.4), reset at the start of the stream and after each gap; a coupled channel dithers its own copy of the coupling channel's zero-bit bins after the coordinate has scaled them;
  - coupling is expanded with each channel's coordinates (7.4.3: the mantissa and exponent with the master coordinate, times 8) and the phase flags of the 2/0 mode;
  - rematrixing turns the bands that set their flag from sum and difference into left and right (7.5.4, with the band edges of Tables 7.25 to 7.28 for the coupling state);
  - each channel goes through the inverse transform of 512 points, or the two of 256 for a block switched channel (7.9.4), with the window of Table 7.33 (a Kaiser-Bessel derived window with the parameter 5, computed with `DetMath` and compared with every value of the table by a test), and the overlap with the block before (a factor of 2 undoes the encoder's headroom scaling).
- **What it leaves out:** dynamic range control (`dynrng` and `compr`), dialogue normalization and every downmix (decision 9 of the design): the samples are the coded channels at full scale, 256 for each block, unclipped and in the coded order (left, center, right, surround channels, then the LFE), the order of the stream's channel layout.
- **Checksums:** crc1 (the first 5/8 of the frame) and crc2 (the whole frame) are declared as the stream's checksums, compared in every analysis, and read "verified (lossy stream)" where every frame passes both and none is missing (decision 2 of the design). They cover coded data, so `--verify` alone decodes nothing for AC-3 and `--measure` and the front ends' output do.
- **Damage:** a frame the parser counts as lost (cut by the end of the data or the next sync word, or one whose syntax breaks so that its blocks are not read) gets no output; the host counts its 1,536 samples as lost. A frame after one, or after junk between frames, resets the overlap and the dither generator and withholds its first block, 256 samples ("after a gap"): its first half-transform's overlap is the lost frame's. A frame whose CRC fails is still decoded, because the parser counts it as read and not lost: its audio is whatever its bytes say, and the failed CRC is the finding. A mantissa code that no quantizer has is limited to the quantizer's highest value, and is reported (`ac3.block-inconsistent` for the grouped codes, `ac3.mantissa-code-invalid` for the others).
- **Trims:** none. The syntax states no priming or padding (decision 8), and the edit lists of MP4 are not applied, so the measurements keep every sample, which equals the stream's Samples less the lost and withheld ones (`Ac3SamplesTests`).
- **Accuracy** ([development.md](../development.md#ac-3-decoding)): every fixture liba52 decodes agrees with it to 2.4 x 10^-7 of full scale once the dither flags are cleared, and with FFmpeg's float decoder to 2 x 10^-5 except in one block of `external-aften-stereo-192k-blocksw.ac3`, where FFmpeg differs from liba52 and the analyzer. The dithered files agree by the energy of bands.
- **Cost:** the decoder charges 44 units for each sample of each channel (the floor of 22 and 22 more for the dequantization, the transform and the window), which `DecoderWorkTests` holds to at most 1.02 times the FLAC reference's time per unit; the declared allowance is 2,048 units a byte.

## Channel layout

From the audio coding mode and the LFE flag. The decoder declares the layout of the channels it outputs (the coded channels, the LFE last, dual mono as two discrete channels), so loudness weights them as MPEG's dual channel is. E-AC-3's dependent substreams add their channels to their program's layout.

## Object audio

Dolby Atmos in Dolby Digital Plus adds object audio to an ordinary E-AC-3 stream: ETSI TS 103 420 carries it as joint object coding (JOC) side information and object audio metadata (OAMD) in an EMDF container, announced by an extension flag.

- **Extension flag (frames):**
  - An E-AC-3 frame whose additional bit stream information (addbsi) is exactly two bytes shows `HasExtensionTypeA` (flag_ec3_extension_type_a, the last bit of the first byte), `Complexity Index Type A` (complexity_index_type_a, the second byte) and the seven reserved bits before the flag, with a finding if they are not 0 (TS 103 420 8.3.1).
  - Other lengths show the bytes only. ffmpeg's `eac3dec.c` reads the flag from the first byte of an addbsi of any length, even one byte, and sets the profile "Dolby Digital Plus + Dolby Atmos" from it; MediaInfo needs two bytes or more, and Encspect requires two, as 8.3.1 lists. A two-byte addbsi that carries something else cannot be told from the extension.
- **Extension flag (dec3):** after the last independent substream's entry, the dec3 box has seven reserved bits, the flag and the complexity index (TS 103 420 C.3.1); a box that ends there shows none. ETSI TS 102 366 V1.4.1 F.6 only reserves those bytes, and no later version exists. The box is compared with the frames' addbsi fields, which are read in every frame, and with the payloads.
- **EMDF containers (TS 102 366 Annex H):**
  - Found by their sync word 0x5838 in the skip fields, in the bits after the last audio block read (the unused bits and the auxiliary data field) and, for a frame whose audio blocks are not read to their end (adaptive hybrid transform, enhanced coupling, a reduced sample rate), in all the bits after where reading stopped. A skip field is read before its block's mantissas, so the skip fields of the blocks read are never part of the unread bits.
  - A candidate must pass a dry run of the structure, as MediaInfo's `Ac3_EMDF_Test` makes it: the length fits, the version is 0, the payloads reach the end marker inside the length, protection_length_primary is not 0, and the fields end exactly where emdf_container_length says, after zero padding to the byte boundary. Random bytes that contain the sync word are no container.
  - A frame whose addbsi sets the flag announces a container, so one that starts a skip field or the bits after the last block read, or follows another, is read on its sync word alone and its damage is reported; the bits after where reading stopped mid-block start nowhere, so a candidate there always has to pass the test.
  - Per container: length, version, key ID, each payload with its ID, configuration and size, the end marker, the protection lengths and the padding. Payload 11 is OAMD and 14 is JOC (TS 103 420 Table 55); 1 to 7 are named after TS 102 366 Table H.2.3.
- **JOC header:** the downmix configuration, the number of objects (joc_num_objects), the extension configuration, the clip gain and the sequence count (TS 103 420 6.2.2, 6.2.3). **OAMD:** the version and the object count (5.5.2), which is not the JOC count: Dolby's own file has 16 objects in the OAMD payload, one of them the LFE channel, and 15 in the JOC header.
- **Stream:** `hasObjectAudio` and `jocObjectCount` (joc_num_objects) in the JSON stream, the GUI's stream line, the summary row, the CSV (`Has Object Audio` and `JOC Object Count`) and the GUI's summary cache are set where a frame holds a JOC payload. The JSON leaves them out otherwise. The statistics add the containers, the frames with object audio and with the flag, the JOC object count and the complexity index.
- **Findings:**
  - `ac3.emdf-truncated`, `ac3.emdf-length`, `ac3.emdf-payload-size`: a container longer than its field, fields that end before or after emdf_container_length, a payload that runs past its container.
  - `ac3.emdf-reserved`, `ac3.emdf-version-not-read`, `ac3.emdf-limit`: a reserved protection length, padding that is not 0, a duration with more than the two groups H.2.2.3.4 allows; a version Annex H does not define (its payloads are not read); a container beyond what the analyzer reads (a fifth variable_bits group or more than 32 payloads, limits of the analyzer, not of the specification).
  - `ac3.object-payload-short`, `ac3.joc-reserved`, `ac3.object-audio-incomplete`: a payload too short for its first fields, a reserved or out-of-range JOC header value, one of the two payloads missing.
  - `ac3.extension-without-joc`, `ac3.joc-without-extension`: judged over an access unit (an independent frame and its dependent frames), since TS 103 420 8.3.1 puts the flag in the substream that holds the container and ffmpeg reads it from either; the first only when every frame of the unit was read to its end.
  - `ac3.config-differs`: a dec3 box whose flag or complexity index disagrees with the frames, or that has no extension where the frames set the flag or carry JOC.
- **Verified against a real file:** Dolby's DD+ Online Delivery Kit v1.4.1 serves JOC test files (`check_eac3_samples.py` downloads three of them in CI, with three files from the FFmpeg archive (two AHT files and one with junk in front), and checks the analyzer, ffprobe and MediaInfo against `tests/conformance/eac3-samples.json`). They show:
  - the container at the start of a skip field, in block 2 of each frame, filling it exactly;
  - emdf_container_length counting the bytes after the length field (389 in a 393-byte skip field);
  - the payload list ending with a bare 5-bit ID 0;
  - codecdatae 0, protection lengths 2 and 1, JOC with the downmix configuration 3, the complexity index 16, and a program of dynamic objects with an LFE channel.
  - On the first file the analyzer reports 5,669 containers, 15 objects and complexity index 16, in agreement with ffprobe and MediaInfo.
  - `joc-atmos` is laid out like them. The other placements (after the last block, in the auxiliary data field) follow H.1 and are hand-built; MediaInfo 24.01 and 26.01 parse a container before the auxiliary data field only.
- **Interpretations:**
  - The syntax tables end the payloads at an ID of 0 without a configuration or size, although H.2.2.2.4 says an ID of 0 comes with zero fields; Dolby's file follows the tables.
  - TS 103 420 Table 56 lists codecdatae as 1; H.2.2.3.7, MediaInfo and Dolby's file take 0. Both are read.
- **ffmpeg 6.1** reads only the flag in the frames; its mov demuxer and muxer read and write neither dec3's extension nor the payloads.

## Not yet covered

- E-AC-3 frames that use the adaptive hybrid transform or enhanced coupling are read only up to that point (planned: entropy pass). No E-AC-3 frame is decoded (the next step of the lossy decoders, [plan](../plan.md#03-lossy-formats-measured)).
- E-AC-3 frames at a reduced sample rate (fscod2) and AC-3 frames with bsid 9 or 10 (half and quarter rate): only their headers are read, not their audio blocks (planned: entropy pass).
- Object audio: the payloads after the JOC and OAMD fields named above (the per-object parameters, the program assignment, the object properties), the protection bits (their calculation is the implementation's, TS 102 366 H.2.2.4.3), TS 103 420's requirement that the container is in the last dependent substream, and Dolby Atmos in TrueHD.

## Encoder detection

No encoder is named from the frames, since no fixture confirms anything that identifies one. An ID3v2 TSSE frame names the encoder with low confidence (`ac3.encoder-claim` for a second one). The writer is resolved apart from claims of the writer role: an APEv2 Encoder item, ffmpeg's muxer version, and a container's tool atom; two that name different software are `ac3.writer-claim-differs`, and software known to write another format is `ac3.writer-other-format`.

## Reference for sample counts

- **Apple's decoder in Core Audio is the reference** for the stream's `samples` at the coded stage (rollout step 5 of [official-decoder-crosschecks.md](../design/official-decoder-crosschecks.md), run 41463609030; macOS 15.8.2 build 24H232, AudioCodecs 7.0, AudioToolbox 1.14). It is the only official decoder a runner image has: Windows has no PCM decoder for AC-3 or E-AC-3 on any image the probe looked at (see "Probe findings" in the design). It is a test tool run in CI and never linked into Encspect; whose code Apple's decoders are is not documented.
- **How it is fed.** `AudioConverter` gets one unit at a time, the units of `scripts/fixtures/unit_list.py` (a syncframe of AC-3, an independent frame of E-AC-3 with its dependent frames, an MP4 sample), with the stream description `AudioFile` reads from the file, or built from the first syncframe for a file `AudioFile` cannot open; a cut unit is fed up to its last byte. `ExtAudioFile` reads the file to the end as a witness.
- **Core Audio's converter outputs 288 frames fewer than the units code, on every file** it decodes (72 of 72 in the probe, 72 of 72 here): the first output of a file with 1,536-sample frames is 1,248. The comparison names the constant (`referenceOffset` -288 in the record) and holds the analyzer's `samples` plus it to the converter's total; the harness adds and subtracts nothing.
- **Agreement** (78 files): the converter has a count for 72 files with one stream and the analyzer less 288 equals it in all 72. No count: `bsid-9.ac3` and `bsid-10.ac3` (`AudioFile` refuses both files, `'typ?'`, and no converter can be built, `'fmt?'`, for the description the harness made from the first syncframe, at 24,000 and 12,000 Hz; the nominal rate was not tried; the extractor reads them now), `reduced-rate.eac3` and `dependent-repeated.eac3` (the converter refuses every unit, `'!dat'`; ffmpeg and liba52 stay their reference). `stream-changes.ac3` and `substreams.eac3` hold several streams (formats) and are not compared with a converter that has one.
- **The witnesses.** `ExtAudioFile` equals the converter in 65 of 68 files; it outputs nothing for a frame that is cut and the converter outputs more than the coded stage less the lost samples (`cut-frame.ac3`, `truncated.ac3`, `truncated-in-skip.eac3`: 7,392, 7,392 and 4,320 against 8,928, 8,928 and 5,856): the converter is `noted` with both figures and no cause, and the reader is `explained` (the analyzer less 288 equals the converter, and the reader's total is the coded stage less the lost samples and the same 288). ffmpeg 8.0.1 equals the analyzer in 65 of 68 and counts fewer in `cut-frame.ac3`, `sample-short.mp4` and `junk-around.ac3` (6,144 each). The statuses follow the cross-check rules of [samples-definition.md](../design/samples-definition.md); no `samples` changes.
- **The edit list** of the two MP4 files with one (`external-ffmpeg-mp4-5.1-448k.mp4`, `external-ffmpeg-mp4-eac3-stereo-96k.mp4`: 23,712 against `samples` 24,576) has no reference: `ExtAudioFile` applies no edit list to AC-3 and E-AC-3 in MP4 (its packet table says 24,576 valid frames, no priming and no padding), so the trimmed stage is not compared.
- The figures, the run and every difference are in `tests/fixtures/ac3/official-decoders.json`, which `Ac3OfficialDecodersTests` holds the analyzer to, together with the `ac3` findings (warnings and errors) at the listed byte ranges. Nothing is `pending`; the analyzer has not been changed.
