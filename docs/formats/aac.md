# AAC

AAC in [MP4](mp4.md), and raw AAC files as ADTS or ADIF. A container that hands over ADTS frames one by one is served by the `aac-adts` codec parser (see below).

## What is read

- **AudioSpecificConfig:** including PCE, ELD and xHE-AAC's (USAC) configuration with its elements, MPEG Surround 2-1-2 settings and configuration extensions, and explicit SBR and PS signaling (HE-AAC and HE-AAC v2).
- **Raw data blocks:** every block of AAC Main, LC, SSR and LTP, element by element, without decoding the audio:
  - window sequences and shapes, section data and scalefactors;
  - mid/side and intensity stereo, noise substitution, pulses, TNS, prediction and gain control;
  - the Huffman-coded spectral data;
  - fill elements with SBR headers, parametric stereo, dynamic range control, fill and ancillary data.
- **AudioSpecificConfig in detail** (ISO/IEC 14496-3 1.6.2.1):
  - the object type with its escape, the sampling frequency index or explicit rate, the channel configuration;
  - GASpecificConfig, with a program config element (4.4.1.1) where the channel configuration is 0;
  - ELDSpecificConfig with its low-delay SBR headers and extensions;
  - of UsacConfig: its rate, coreSbrFrameLengthIndex, channel configuration, the elements with their core and SBR defaults, and the configuration extensions, with a channel pair element's Mps212Config, read as ffmpeg and fdk-aac read it;
  - a fill or audio pre-roll element reads no configuration, as Table 17 says and ffmpeg and libxaac read it; a configuration length it declares anyway is a warning (fdk-aac skips the bytes).
- **Explicit SBR and PS:**
  - hierarchical (object type 5 or 29 first) and backward compatible (the sync extensions 0x2B7 and 0x548);
  - with SBR the output rate is the extension rate and an access unit's samples double; with SBR a mono core gives two channels, with parametric stereo from the blocks that hold its data and the one coded channel on both where they hold none (14496-3 1.6.6.3);
  - a configuration that signals SBR absent (ffmpeg writes 0x2B7 with the flag clear) is plain "AAC LC"; SBR data in such a stream is a warning;
  - for the object types whose blocks are not read (scalable, TwinVQ), a GA core that signals nothing about SBR stays "AAC LC (no SBR signaled explicitly)", and an SBR stream over a mono core that signals nothing about PS "HE-AAC (no PS signaled explicitly)", each with an info finding.
- **Each access unit** keeps a record of its offset, length and first syntax element (4.4.2.1) with its instance tag; for a fill or data stream element its count, extension type and any printable text in its payload; USAC's usacIndependencyFlag.
  - The error resilient object types hold no element IDs, so nothing is kept for them.
  - The nodes are built 1,024 access units at a time when read.
- **Elements read in a raw data block** (4.4.2), at frame lengths of 1024 and 960:
  - single channel, channel pair (common_window, ms_mask_present, ms_used per group and band), coupling channel (its targets, domain, gain element sign and scale, the gain element lists), LFE, data stream (byte alignment, the escape count, printable text), program config, fill (the count with its escape, and its extension payloads), and END with the byte alignment after it;
  - individual_channel_stream: global_gain; ics_info (window sequence and shape, max_sfb, scale_factor_grouping, AAC Main's prediction data with the reset group and prediction_used per band, LTP data for each channel of a pair); section_data with codebook escapes; scale_factor_data through the scalefactor codebook, with intensity stereo positions and noise energies, the first a 9-bit value; pulse_data; tns_data, whose coefficient resolution is the window's; gain_control_data (SSR, whose location fields differ in width per window sequence);
  - spectral_data through codebooks 1 to 11 with their sign bits and escapes of at most 8 leading ones. Codebooks 13 to 15 code no spectral data.
- **Fill payloads:**
  - EXT_SBR_DATA and EXT_SBR_DATA_CRC with sbr_header and, where an element's header has been read, sbr_data far enough to find the parametric stereo extension (EXT_ID_PS), with the frequency band tables derived from the header (4.6.18.3);
  - EXT_FILL_DATA (the fill nibble and the 0xA5 bytes checked), EXT_DATA_ELEMENT (ancillary data), EXT_DYNAMIC_RANGE (dynamic_range_info with its PCE tag, excluded channels, bands and reference level) and EXT_FILL's other bits;
  - a payload is read only within its fill element's count; one that needs more is a finding, and the next element is read where the count ends, as decoders do.
- **Intensity stereo under ms_mask_present 2:** the references differ on whether every band's sign is inverted. ffmpeg and fdk-aac invert it, since that mask marks every band; faad2's invert_intensity inverts only under ms_mask_present 1.
  - The report follows ffmpeg and fdk-aac for the bands it shows out of phase.
- **ADTS:** sync on a chain of frames, resync after damage, every header field, and the CRC over the elements it covers.
  - A header counts as sync when two more frames with the same fixed header (but the private bit) follow at the offsets its length gives, or its frames end exactly at the end of the data. Then frames are followed by their lengths; a frame in which a new chain starts is cut short there as damaged, and anything else is a gap.
  - ADTS's layer bits are 00, which no MPEG audio header has, so neither format takes the other's headers, and ADTS-looking bytes inside an MP3 stay a gap.
- **ADTS detection:** a chain at the start of the data (after zero bytes) of three frames is class 91. A chain further in is class 50 only with eight consistent frames or frames to the end of the data, since other formats' bytes (E-AC-3's frame headers) can chain three.
- **ADIF:** the header, and its raw data blocks, which the parser delimits.
- **Implicit signaling:** SBR and PS found only inside the blocks make the stream HE-AAC or HE-AAC v2, with a finding that says so. The rule for what counts:
  - an SBR payload counts as evidence where it follows a single channel, channel pair or LFE element, whether or not its data can be read (before its first SBR header, with tables that cannot be built, past its fill element, or after an LFE element, as FAAC 2.0 and 2.1 write theirs in 5.1). Decoders enable SBR from the payload's presence there:
    - ffmpeg (`aacdec_template.c`, `decode_extension_payload`) sets `m4ac.sbr = 1` before the data is read; ffprobe 6.1.1 reports `faac-2.1-he-51-adts` as HE-AAC at 44,100 Hz, with "cannot apply SBR to element type 3";
    - faad2 (`syntax.c`, `fill_element`) sets `sbr_present_flag = 1` before `sbr_extension_data`, for a fill element right after such an element; `sbr_data` skips the data for an LFE element;
    - fdk-aac (`aacdecoder.cpp`): `IS_CHANNEL_ELEMENT` names the single channel, channel pair and LFE elements and its SBR payload is parsed after them, but its SBR parse returns right after initializing an LFE element (`sbrdecoder.cpp`, `ID_LFE`), and FDK AAC 2.0.2 outputs `faac-2.1-he-51-adts` at the core rate, 22,050 Hz, without SBR (see "Reference for sample counts"): it is not one of the decoders that apply SBR after an LFE element;
    - a coupling channel element is none of these: fdk-aac excludes it, faad2 rejects it (error 6 without COUPLING_DEC, and error 24 for a fill element with SBR data met elsewhere), and only ffmpeg keeps it as the previous element, where a program config element has given it a channel. A payload after one is reported (`aac.sbr-without-channel-element`) and counts for nothing (`sbr-after-coupling-element.aac`);
    - a payload before any channel element does not count either;
  - blocks of frames the analyzer flags as damaged (cut, truncated or failing their CRC) and blocks that cannot be read to their end are no evidence;
  - the first block that counts decides, as in ffmpeg 6.1.1 (`sbr-in-all-but-first-block.aac` decodes at the core's rate and `sbr-in-first-block-only.aac` at twice it; no other decoder has been run on them): if it holds such a payload the stream is HE-AAC (and doubles its output rate and samples), and if it holds none the stream stays the core, whatever the later blocks hold; the decoder decides on the same block;
  - the decision is taken from the first 32 blocks read (`DecodeFeed.MaxUndecided`): where none of them counts (a stream whose frames all fail their CRC), the stream stays as its configuration says and no later block changes it; the decoder decodes every block either way, and a block that fails its CRC is decoded and withheld, never lost;
  - the blocks that count are the same for the report and the decoder: blocks read to their end that are not in a damaged frame or one that fails its CRC; the decoder holds the blocks before the first one that counts until it is known;
  - blocks that disagree with the first one are reported: `aac.sbr-inconsistent` (warning) where most blocks disagree, and `aac.sbr-data-sparse` where the stream stays the core although some blocks hold SBR data;
  - parametric stereo is not decided by the first block: where the configuration does not signal PS absent (psPresentFlag 0 in the backward-compatible 0x548 extension), a single channel with SBR decodes to two output channels, and the blocks that hold PS data apply it from whatever block it starts in, while the others map the one coded channel to both (14496-3 1.6.6.3 and 1.6.6.4; `sbr-ps` conformance streams `al_sbr_ps_04_new` and `al_sbr_ps_06_new` start it at block 53 of 106 and 106 of 212, which FFmpeg decodes the same way). The codec says HE-AAC v2 where PS data is signaled or appears in any block read, and HE-AAC otherwise; the channel mode of a stream with SBR and no PS data reads "Mono, output on both channels", so that identical channels are not read as stereo content. Explicit PS absent keeps the one channel;
  - parametric stereo also needs a core of one channel: decoders apply it to nothing else (fdk-aac, ffmpeg), and the clause speaks of a single channel element, so a stream of more channels (a program config element with several elements as well) keeps its channels, and the PS ID in it, or object type 29 over a channel configuration above 1, is reported (`aac.ps-without-mono-core`) and not applied;
  - the hand-built fixtures `sbr-in-first-block-only.aac`, `sbr-in-first-three-blocks.aac`, `sbr-in-all-but-first-block.aac` and `sbr-after-first-frame-failing-crc.aac` cover the rule, with `sbr-in-one-block.aac` and `sbr-in-three-blocks.aac`; every other committed HE-AAC fixture with implicit signaling has SBR in all of its blocks.
  - the finding of an implicit HE-AAC stream says where decoders differ: its output rate and samples double, as fdk-aac ("assume dual rate SBR") and ffmpeg decode it, while faad2 doubles only a core rate of up to 24 kHz, which the finding says for a higher one;
  - SBR data before its first header is an info finding (`aac.sbr-before-header`), since a stream cut mid-way starts so.
- **Storage:** a report keeps each block's bits without the Huffman-coded scalefactors and spectral data, the SBR data and long payloads (UnitStore), with the length of each part it leaves out, and rebuilds the block's nodes from that when they are read.
  - On ffmpeg's frames at 96 kbit/s the report keeps 116 bytes per frame.
  - A 195 MB M4A of 533,066 access units at 128 kbit/s analyzes in 4.3 seconds (1.3 seconds before the raw data blocks were read) with a peak of 221 MB (194 MB before).
  - Its JSON report with every node is 13.4 GB; without the side information (`--hide side-info`) 2.1 GB.
- **Limits:** the frame records of an ADTS file (frames and gaps of all its streams together) and the block records of an ADIF stream are capped at 2,097,152 each (about 13.5 hours of AAC LC at 44.1 kHz), since a frame or block can be a few bytes and each costs a record. Past the cap the frames that follow one another with the stream's header (ADTS), or the blocks that can be read to their end (ADIF), are only counted: they join the stream's frames, bytes, samples, duration, average, smallest and largest sizes and bitrate extremes, but get no node (`Counted Frames`, `Counted Blocks`, in records of at most 2 GiB), no raw data block is read, and the graphs and coding statistics cover the first 2,097,152. ADTS counting resyncs after a sync loss on the next chain of the same stream, as before the cap (the bytes between are a `Gap`, up to 4,096 times); it stops at another stream, and ADIF at the first block that cannot be read to its end, and the bytes left are not analyzed (`Unread Bytes`). The finding is `aac.frames-limit` or `aac.blocks-limit`. The graph of bitrate over time keeps at most 131,072 entries, merging neighbors where a stream is longer or its sample rate lower. An explicit sample rate below 1,000 Hz is reported (`aac.implausible-sample-rate`; 0 is `aac.reserved-sample-rate`).
- **ADTS streams:** a change of the fixed header ends the stream and starts another, also where an earlier header returns; the stream rule is the same for MPEG audio, AC-3 and DTS ([architecture](../architecture.md#stream-changes), "Stream changes").
  - `aac.stream-changed` is one Info finding with the number of changes.
  - A file has at most 64 streams. The frames from the change that would start stream 65 on are in no stream and are counted in one warning, `aac.streams-limited`; the frames counted only from their headers past the record cap (see Limits) are counted by the frames they hold. Their raw data blocks are not read, and no log of blocks is kept for their headers, so a file that changes its header at every frame keeps a few kilobytes per stream, 64 at most, not a log for each change.
- **ADTS frames as packets (`aac-adts`):** the IEC 61937 bursts of AAC in a WAV file carry ADTS frames ([wav.md](wav.md)). The codec parser takes one frame per packet:
  - the configuration is the first valid frame's header (object type, sampling frequency index and channel configuration, as in a raw ADTS file; SBR and PS are found from the fill elements of the raw data blocks, as there);
  - each frame's raw data block follows its header and error check and is read as an access unit of the `aac` codec parser, with the same statistics, series and encoder detection; a payload handed over in one piece is read as a raw AAC file;
  - a packet that does not start with a valid header of the stream (the fixed header fields of the first frame) is skipped, `aac.adts-packet-invalid`; with none valid there is no configuration (`aac.no-configuration`);
  - a frame of several raw data blocks is skipped, `aac.adts-multiple-blocks` (info), since the positions of its blocks are not read in this mode;
  - the frames are not nodes of their own: the stream's nodes are the access units, at the bytes of the file the frame's raw data block is in.
- **ADTS frames of several blocks:** an unprotected frame that holds fewer raw data blocks than its header declares is reported (`aac.blocks-missing`) and counted by the blocks it holds; bytes after its last declared block are reported like those of a single block.

## Coding class

AAC in every object type is lossy coding, MPEG-4 ALS and DST are lossless coding, and SLS has no class: its enhancement layer can be truncated short of lossless, so the stream alone does not say ([the class table](../cli.md#the-coding-class-of-a-stream)).

## Statistics

- **Bitrate Mode** (the mode the stream or its container states where it states one (the nominal bitrates of AC-3 and of DTS without extension substreams, 0x7FF in ADTS (any other value, 0 included, states a constant bitrate), the variable flag of ASF), else variable or constant by the units' own bitrates, unknown for one unit) and **Bitrate Distribution** (the units' bitrates counted in ranges of 32 kbps, with each range's share), beside the minimum and maximum bitrate.
- **Buffer Fullness** (ADTS): the adts_buffer_fullness of the frames that state one, as "Minimum Buffer Fullness", "Maximum Buffer Fullness" and "Average Buffer Fullness" in 32-bit words per channel (ISO/IEC 14496-3 1.A.3.2.1; the unit shows as "words per channel"; with the DiscardPadding of a Matroska track's last block there is also "End Trimming", and Samples and Duration are less by it), the series "Buffer Fullness" per frame and "Buffer Fullness Over Time" (the mean of each second), and "Frames Signaling Variable Bitrate" for 0x7FF, which states no fullness and is no fill. The name is its own: a buffer fullness the encoder states is not the "Reservoir Fill" that mp3 and WMA compute, and its unit is words, not bytes. LATM is not parsed here, so its bufferFullness is not covered.

**Units counted:** each raw data block (an ADTS frame holds one to four, an MP4 sample or ADIF block one) counts the samples of the stream's frame length: 1,024, or 2,048 at the output rate of SBR, 960 for the 960 transform. A cut ADTS frame counts them too, and so does a packet the container cut (read as no access unit, and in no unit count, size or bitrate), and a block whose syntax breaks (`aac.section-overrun`, `aac.element-past-end`, `aac.codebook-reserved`, `aac.prediction-not-allowed`, `aac.max-sfb-too-large`, `aac.escape-too-long`, `aac.store-mismatch`) stops being read there; both are reported as Lost Samples (and the stream's `lostSamples`). Findings the reading survives (a CRC mismatch, `aac.ics-reserved-bit`, `aac.window-sequence-invalid`, a scalefactor out of range, fill data) lose nothing, and an access unit with no byte counts nothing. A header destroyed by damage is a gap and counts nothing.

Each stream whose raw data blocks are read (AAC Main, LC, SSR and LTP) reports, counted while the blocks are read:

- window sequences and shapes per channel stream (the tally and series **Block Usage**, named as MPEG audio names the block types of Layer III), and **Block Sizes**, the window sequences grouped as long (only long, long start and long stop) and short (eight short);
- channel pairs with a common window, mid/side masks and bands, intensity stereo, and **Stereo Coding**, how every channel pair codes its channels (below);
- noise substitution, TNS per window sequence, pulse data, codebook use, max_sfb and global gain per channel;
- **Coded Bandwidth**: the lowest, most common and highest coded bandwidth of the stream's access units or frames, in whole hertz (below);
- **Last Non-Zero Coefficient**, **Non-Zero Coefficients** and **Escape-Coded Coefficients**, from the quantized values of the spectral data (below);
- SBR's frequencies and the fill payloads;
- **Verified Frame Checksums** and, above 0, **Failed Frame Checksums** for ADTS: the frames whose CRC-16 equals the one computed over the header and the elements it covers (a frame whose CRC could not be verified, because a raw data block was unreadable, or that lies in no stream, is in neither count).

Not covered yet: a "Reservoir Fill" series (as in MPEG audio and WMA) from the buffer fullness field of the ADTS and LATM headers; the field is shown in the ADTS header node but not graphed.

The classification of a channel pair, per pair and independent of its window:

- **Intensity stereo:** some band of the second channel uses codebook 14 or 15, in any channel pair. A pair without a common window can code it as FFmpeg 6.1.1 (`aacdec_template.c`, `decode_cpe`, which calls `apply_intensity_stereo` outside its common window case) and faad2 decode it, and the hand-built fixtures `stereo-coding` and `stereo-coding-reserved` hold the rule. fdk-aac applies intensity stereo only with a common window (`channel.cpp` lines 228-231) and its `stereo.cpp` comment quotes ISO/IEC 14496-3 4.6.8.2.3 as allowing the codebooks only there, so such a pair gets the warning `aac.intensity-without-common-window` (worded as what fdk-aac's source says, the specification text not having been read) and is still counted.
- **Mid/side stereo:** at least one band has its ms_used flag set, ms_mask_present 2 counting as all bands set, and is neither an intensity stereo band nor a band where either channel uses noise substitution (codebook 13, 14 or 15): FFmpeg's `apply_mid_side_stereo` skips such a band, which is the rule counted. faad2's `ms_decode` skips the noise bands of the first channel and the intensity stereo bands of the second only, and fdk-aac's `MapMidSideMaskToPnsCorrelation` skips only bands where both channels use noise substitution. It needs a common window. In an intensity stereo band ms_used inverts the sign (ISO/IEC 14496-3 4.6.8.2.3), so it is no mid/side coding there.
- **Both** where both hold, **left/right stereo** where neither does, and **Reserved** for ms_mask_present 3, which the specification reserves and which takes precedence over intensity stereo. FFmpeg rejects ms_mask_present 3 as an error.
- "Channel Pairs" keeps its entries. Its "Intensity Stereo" is the share of all channel pairs with an intensity stereo band, and its "Intensity Stereo Bands" the share of the second channels' bands in intensity stereo, so a pair without a common window counts; its mid/side entries keep the pairs with a common window as their denominator. "Intensity Stereo" is listed for every stream with channel pairs, "Intensity Stereo Bands" and "Out of Phase" only where a band exists.
- **Out of Phase** counts an intensity stereo band as out of phase when its codebook is 14, and with ms_mask_present 1 also when it is 15 and its ms_used flag is set, as faad2 and fdk-aac decode it (`stereo.cpp` lines 1154-1160 clear the mid/side flags before intensity stereo is applied, so mask 2 inverts nothing). FFmpeg inverts with ms_mask_present 2 too; the specification text was not available to settle it.

**Coded Bandwidth** (renamed from "Bandwidth", which was the most common max_sfb of long windows as text):

- The value of an access unit or ADTS frame is the top of the highest band that any of its channel streams codes: band max_sfb of its window's table, long (1,024 or 960 lines) or short, at the core's sample rate, in whole hertz rounded half to even.
- It is a bound the stream states. Bands below it may be coded as zero, as noise substitution or as intensity stereo, SBR adds content above the core, and it is not the encoder's lowpass. A channel stream with max_sfb 0 gives 0 Hz; a unit without a channel stream has no value.

**Window sequences that cannot follow one another** (`aac.window-sequence-invalid`, information):

- The four window sequences have a left and a right slope: only long has long slopes on both sides, long start a long left slope and a short right one, eight short short slopes on both sides, and long stop a short left slope and a long right one (ISO/IEC 14496-3 4.5.2 and 4.6.11 define the sequences, the sections the other descriptions of this project cite; the specification's text is not available to the project, so the rule is stated from the windows the decoder builds).
- Two windows overlap by the slopes they share, so a window may follow another only where its left slope equals the other's right one: only long or long stop before only long or long start, long start or eight short before eight short or long stop. A long window followed directly by eight short ones, or a long stop followed by a long stop, is not a pair the windows define.
- The check compares each channel stream with the stream of the same number in the block before (the channel streams of a block in element order, coupling channel elements left out). It forgets the block before after a block that was not read to its end and after a gap (lost sync, a cut), so the first block after those is never flagged; a frame whose checksum fails is compared like any other, as its verdict comes after its blocks are read. A common window pair is flagged once.
- One finding for the stream, counting the raw data blocks that hold such a window sequence and pointing at the first one's Window Sequence field. It is information, not a warning: it states what the stream does, and the decoders' records (`official-decoders.json`) list no unit of the files that have it that a decoder refuses or conceals. The decoder windows such a pair by each block's own sequence (see Decoding).
- In Encspect's test files the finding appears in the FDK AAC encoder's files (`fdkaac-lc`, `fdkaac-lc-vbr`, `fdkaac-he`, `fdkaac-he-adts`, `fdkaac-he-hierarchical`, `fdkaac-he-implicit` and `fdkaac-crc-mono`, in their first frames), in the hand-built files that mix long and short windows between blocks for other reasons and in the Matroska files made from their frames (`aac-legacy-lc`, `aac-legacy-explicit-rate`, `aac-legacy-stray-codec-private`, `aac-discard-padding`, `aac-discard-padding-large`, `writer-lavc-id` and `writer-tag-and-apps`), and in `window-sequences-invalid.aac`, which holds each kind of pair on purpose.

**The quantized values** (the walk of every analysis, whether or not the stream is decoded; names as WMA's "Non-Zero Coefficients" and "Escape-Coded Coefficients"):

- **Last Non-Zero Coefficient:** the lowest, most common and highest, over the stream's units, of the upper edge of the highest nonzero quantized coefficient of the unit's channel streams, in whole hertz at the core's sample rate (a short window's line at an eighth of a long window's lines; of eight short windows, the highest line of any). It is read from the Huffman-coded values, so it lies at or below the Coded Bandwidth, which is what max_sfb states; pulse data (ISO/IEC 14496-3 4.6.3), noise substitution and intensity stereo add values this does not count. A unit whose coefficients are all zero has none.
- **Non-Zero Coefficients** counts the nonzero quantized coefficients, and **Escape-Coded Coefficients** those coded with an escape sequence (a value of 16 or more in codebook 11, 4.6.3.3), with their share of the non-zero ones.
- The walk takes the nonzero positions from a table of each codebook (`Codebooks.NonZero`: which values of a symbol are nonzero), not from decoded values, so the cost is a bit mask per codeword ([development.md](../development.md#analysis-speed-of-the-lossy-formats)); when a session decodes, the walk that stores the values counts the same figures, and a test holds the two to each other.

## Graphs

Each stream whose blocks are read reports category series and numeric series for the Graphs tab:

- **Recorded while reading,** not derived at the end as MPEG audio's are: the parser keeps the bits of each block and rebuilds its nodes by walking the syntax again, so a field has no fixed place to read back, and recording it costs a byte in the pass that counts it already.
- **A unit** is what the bitrate series has a point for: an access unit (MP4, Matroska), an ADTS frame or an ADIF block. It holds as many values as it has (a variable layout, with a count byte per unit); a unit codes at most 254 values and counts the rest without a code, so a series still equals its tally.
- **Category series,** each anchored to its Statistics node, with the names of the node's entries and a time index of the seconds "Bitrate Over Time" uses:
  - "Block Usage" (concept `blockSwitching`): the sequence of every channel stream of every raw data block of the unit, as the tally counts them (coupling channel elements have none); and its grouping "Block Sizes";
  - "Window Shapes" (sine, KBD; no concept): the window shape of every channel stream, as the tally of Window Shapes counts them;
  - flag series per channel stream (no concept), each named as the tally node it is held equal to: "TNS", "Pulse Data" and "Noise Substitution";
  - flag series per raw data block, as their tallies count blocks: "SBR" and "Parametric Stereo", named as their tally nodes; a block that could not be read has neither;
  - category series of the SBR and parametric stereo data read to their end, named as their tally nodes under "SBR" and "Parametric Stereo": "SBR Frame Classes" (FIXFIX, FIXVAR, VARFIX, VARVAR) and "SBR Envelopes" (1 to 5 envelopes), with a value per SBR channel, and "Parametric Stereo Envelopes" (no envelopes to 4) and "Parametric Stereo Bands" (10, 20 or 34 bands, the finer of the intensity difference and coherence modes, or none where neither is enabled), with a value per raw data block that has parametric stereo data; the parametric stereo data is read in every analysis, with or without decoding;
  - "Stereo Coding" (concept `stereoCoding`): the coding of every channel pair of the unit, with the category "Reserved" for ms_mask_present 3.
- **Numeric series** "Mid/Side Bands" (%, concept `stereoCoding`, a quantity of its own, per unit): the share of the scalefactor bands of the unit's channel pairs with a common window that are coded as mid/side, leaving out intensity stereo bands; a unit without such a pair has no point.
- **Numeric series** "Intensity Stereo Bound" (Hz, concept `stereoCoding`, a quantity of its own, per unit only): the lowest frequency from which the unit's channel pairs code intensity stereo, the lower edge of the lowest scalefactor band of any pair's second channel that uses codebook 14 or 15 (a short window's band at an eighth of a long window's lines), at the core's sample rate; a unit without such a band has no point. Its Statistics node of the same name holds the lowest and highest. It is what the stream states, not a measurement.
  - The host reads the series by the quantity `SeriesQuantities.IntensityStereoBound` and, with `--measure`, sets its median beside the start of the inter-channel coherence of the decoded audio as figures ([cli.md](../cli.md)).
- **Replicated bands:** with `--measure`, an HE-AAC stream's decoded audio reads a stretch from within 130 Hz of the start its SBR header states (neroAacEnc 1.5.4.0: about 1.4 kHz above it) up to the top of the content, at offsets that are multiples of 689 Hz at 44.1 kHz, in 50 to 100 % of the frames with signal (qaac 3.07: 9,388 to 20,284 Hz at 6,202 and 11,025 Hz); AAC LC reads none (best band 26.2 %). `aac/fdkaac-he` and `aac/faac-2.2-he` hold no signal above 1 kHz and read none. The figure describes the decoded audio and names no encoder ([the design](../design/spectral-analysis.md#as-built-step-9)).
- **Numeric series** "Coded Bandwidth" (Hz, concept `codedBandwidth`) per unit, and "Coded Bandwidth Over Time", each second's highest, anchored to the Statistics node. A unit without a channel stream has no point, and the series then has its own x values; "Bitrate" and "Bitrate Over Time" get the concept `bitDistribution` and the quantity "Bitrate", as MPEG audio's.
- **Numeric series** "Last Non-Zero Coefficient" (Hz, concept `codedBandwidth`, a quantity of its own) per unit and "Last Non-Zero Coefficient Over Time", each second's highest, anchored to the Statistics node of the same name, beside "Coded Bandwidth": the highest over the unit's channel streams, as that one; a unit without a nonzero coefficient has no point and the series then has its own x values.
- **Numeric series** "Escape-Coded Coefficients" (%, a quantity of its own, per unit only): the share of the unit's nonzero coefficients that are escape-coded, in tenths of a percent; a unit without a nonzero coefficient has no point.
- A stream without a channel pair has no "Stereo Coding" (a mono stream keeps nothing for it), and the series stop at the unit where `aac.frames-limit` or `aac.blocks-limit` stops the statistics.
- An ADIF stream whose last block cannot be read to its end has that block as a unit of the series (its channel streams are in the tally) though it is no point of the bitrate graph, and the series then have the blocks' own offsets for x.
- Memory: a stereo unit adds 5 bytes of codes and count bytes (the two channel streams' window sequences and the pair's coding, with a count byte for each series), and the coded bandwidth's 8 bytes, with 6 more for the last nonzero coefficient and the escape share. Measured on linux-x64, an ADTS frame of ffmpeg's encoder keeps 10 more bytes (126 against 116) and an MP4 sample 14 more (74 against 60), against bounds that stay as they were (`MemoryTests`, which also bounds the growth per further unit between two counts).

Cross-checks: `crosscheck_mp4_fixtures.py` holds the series of every golden report to faad2's trace per unit (window sequences, the pair classification computed from its flags and codebooks, and the bandwidth from its max_sfb), and `AacCodingSeriesTests` holds them to the nodes of the units' own raw data blocks.

## Channel layout

The channel configuration, a program config element, or parametric stereo.

- **Channel configurations 1 to 7 and 11 to 14** of Table 1.19 (amendment 4 of the 2009 edition adds 11 to 14) give their speakers in the order of their elements, as ffmpeg's decoder maps them.
- **A program config element** gives its front, side, back and LFE lists in its own order:
  - the front from the center outward: a single channel element first is the center, and two or more pairs start beside the center;
  - the back from the sides to the center: a last single channel element is the back center, and two back pairs without side elements are the side and back pairs.
- **USAC:** channelConfigurationIndex is ISO/IEC 23091-3's, and its output channel positions are mapped as MP4's chnl box maps them.
- **Parametric stereo** gives left and right from a mono core. Without it, a mono core with SBR is still output on two channels (the same signal in both) unless the configuration signals PS absent.

## Encoder and writer detection

The encoder and the writer are two fields of a stream, each with its own confidence and evidence (the "Encoder detection and confidence" and "Writer detection" topics of the GUI and `--explain` explain both); a writer that differs from the encoder is no conflict.

- **Encoder:** the stream's own evidence and the claims of the encoder role (an ID3v2 TSSE frame in a stream's file).
- **Version:** a writer claim of the same name that goes on from the encoder's name completes a version-less encoder (`ClaimRoles.CompleteVersion`, shared by every format parser); it never creates an encoder or raises a confidence.
- **Writer:** QuickTime's ©swr ("name and version number of the software that generated this movie"); the MP4 tool atom (©too), which no Apple specification defines; an APEv2 Encoder item; ffmpeg's muxer version ("Lavf60.16.100"); and the layouts of Apple's file writer. Two claims that name different software are an information finding (`aac.writer-claim-differs`), and software known to write another format (exhale or FAAC on the wrong codec, LAME) is `aac.writer-other-format`.

Encoder:

- ffmpeg's encoder and FAAC 1.30 to 1.50, from the version they write into a fill element, with high confidence:

## What Apple's encoder writes

From `afconvert` on macOS 15.7.9 (AudioToolbox framework bundle version 1.14, AudioCodecs.component bundle version 7.0, `afconvert` 2.0), arm64 and x86-64, 0.5 s of a test signal (1 s for HE-AAC), as the committed `apple-*` fixtures show:

## Reference for sample counts

- **Fraunhofer's FDK AAC decoder is the reference** for the stream's `samples` at the coded stage, the count before any priming or padding is removed: FDK AAC 2.0.2 (commit 801f67f), built on a Linux runner by `.github/workflows/official-decode.yml` and fed through `scripts/fixtures/fdk_decode.c` (see "AAC decoding by FDK AAC" in [development.md](../development.md), which lists the differences found). It is a test tool built in CI and never linked into Encspect; its license grants no patent license (the design's license reading).
- **How it is fed.** One unit at a time, the units of `scripts/fixtures/unit_list.py`, which is not the analyzer:
  - an ADTS frame per `aacDecoder_Fill`, with the bytes the extractor skipped (junk, a gap after damage) in front of the next frame, so that FDK's own resync is exercised;
  - an MP4 sample per fill, with the track's AudioSpecificConfig given to `aacDecoder_ConfigRaw`;
  - ADIF through FDK's own transport (`TT_MP4_ADIF`), which has no frame lengths, so its record has no unit offsets;
  - a cut unit up to its last byte and never padded.
- **Counting.**
  - A unit counts as output when FDK reports a valid output (`IS_OUTPUT_VALID`), and a frame it conceals counts. Concealment method 1 (noise substitution) is set for every object type (for ADIF after the first decode call, as its header is read inside the library), because the library's default depends on the object type and method 2 adds a frame of delay.
  - Samples are per channel, in runs of constant output format (rate and channels). Totals are compared in the same duration, never as sums across output rates: FDK's 1,024 samples at 22,050 Hz are 2,048 at 44,100 Hz.
  - The frames `AACDEC_FLUSH` drains at the end (`ceil(outputDelay / frameSize)`, one for AAC-LC and HE-AAC) are counted apart as `flushedSamples` and never in the coded total.
  - For a frame of several raw data blocks FDK keeps the last blocks until more bits arrive and the drain discards them, so its total is short by those blocks; such a total is no reference (`decoder-holds-blocks`).
- **Concealed frames.** An excess of FDK's total over the analyzer's that is exactly the samples of the frames FDK conceals is `explained`: a future Encspect decoder leaves a frame it cannot decode as a gap, never as concealed audio (the project lead, 2026-11-01). In some damaged files the analyzer counts the frame FDK conceals too, and then the totals agree.
- **Flagged units.** A unit the analyzer flags (for example `aac.frame-truncated`) counts by the positions its header declares, and FDK outputs nothing for it: the shortfall is `explained` (decided 2026-11-02 for AAC: `samples` means the positions the stream's units declare; the stream's `lostSamples` report the lost ones apart).
- **Core Audio is the second reference and the reference of the trimmed stage** (rollout step 5; macOS 15.8.2, run 41463609030): FDK has no file reader, so the analyzer's "Samples (iTunSMPB)" and "Samples (Edit List)" are held to the total of `ExtAudioFile`, which applies them. ## Decoding

AAC LC (object type 2, frames of 1,024 samples), with SBR (HE-AAC) and parametric stereo (HE-AAC v2) where the stream has them, is decoded for `--measure` and the front ends' output, from the access units of MP4 and Matroska, the frames of ADTS and the blocks of ADIF.

- **What is decoded:** the spectral values the walk stores (`BlockRecord`, one slot per channel stream), with inverse quantization and the scalefactors, mid/side and intensity stereo, noise substitution, temporal noise shaping, pulse data and the filter bank (the inverse MDCT with the sine and Kaiser-Bessel derived windows of the long, start, eight short and stop sequences, and the overlap-add). The output is planar 32-bit floats at full scale 1.0, unclipped and undithered, at the core's rate and in the order the elements follow one another (the stream's channel layout), with the positions of the coded stage: the first block's output is the first 1,024 samples.
- **Source:** the code follows libxaac (Ittiam, Apache 2.0, `scripts/fixtures/libxaac_tool.py` pins its commit) where the specification leaves details to the decoder, and its notice is in [THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md](../../THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md).
  - The order of the tools is libxaac's: mid/side stereo, then intensity stereo (on any channel pair, with or without a common window; a mid/side flag inverts the sign of an intensity band, for ms_mask_present 1 and 2, as libxaac and FFmpeg do and FDK AAC and faad2 do for 1 only), then noise substitution, then TNS. Noise substitution is generated after the stereo tools, so an intensity band that copies a noise band copies zeros, which FFmpeg's decoder (it generates noise first) does not.
  - Noise substitution draws `seed = 1664525 * seed + 1013904223` (seed 0 at the start of the stream and after a gap, one generator for all channels), takes `seed >> 3` as each value and scales the band to the energy 2^(noise energy / 2). A band that mid/side stereo flags in a pair, where both channels code noise, gets the same noise in both and no mid/side processing. libxaac keeps the first channel's seed per band, not per window of a group; the decoder keeps it per window.
  - Each block is windowed by its own sequence and shape (the left half by the shape of the previous block), which is the per-block definition of the filter bank and what libxaac's switch on the previous sequence amounts to. A transition the specification does not allow (a long window followed directly by eight short ones, as in the first two frames of four FDK AAC fixtures) overlaps a long slope with a short one the same way in libxaac and the analyzer; FFmpeg's decoder differs there.
- **HE-AAC:**
  - SBR runs on the core's output per channel element (`SbrElement`, `SbrChannel`, `SbrQmf`, `SbrLayout`): the 32-band analysis, the generation of the high band by patching the low band, the adjustment of the envelopes with noise and sinusoids, and the 64-band synthesis to twice the core's rate; coupled channel pairs dequantize with the balance of the pair. A frame without usable SBR data (none, damaged, or no header known yet) only upsamples the low band.
  - Parametric stereo (`PsDecoder`) turns the QMF domain of a single channel element into two: a hybrid filter bank for the lowest bands (20 or 34 subbands), a decorrelator of all-pass links and delays, and mixing matrices from the intensity differences and coherences, interpolated between the envelope borders. The hybrid analysis looks 6 QMF slots ahead, into the low band's slots the SBR buffer already holds, so the output is aligned with that of an SBR stream without parametric stereo (on `apple-hev2-adts-cbr-st44.aac` the RMS of the difference to FFmpeg is under 10^-6 at a shift of 0, and FFmpeg and libxaac differ by 1.6 x 10^-4 there; across the 61 files of the cross-check the difference stays under 3.2 % of FFmpeg's RMS). No inter-channel phase differences are decoded (the baseline skips them). The 34-band resolution (an intensity difference or coherence mode of 34 bands) is used by no encoder's file; the hand-built `mp4/ps-34-bands.m4a` exercises it and agrees with FFmpeg's decoder to under 5 x 10^-5 of its RMS (libxaac itself computes in 20 bands).
  - The tables (the QMF prototype, noise phases, the hybrid filters, the phase factors and band groupings) are libxaac's format data (`SbrTableData.cs`, `PsTableData.cs`; `scripts/fixtures/make_ps_tables.py` writes the second from a libxaac checkout).
  - Implicit SBR is decided at the first block that counts, as in the report (see "What is read"): the blocks before it wait for the decision, so the parser and the decoder count the same blocks. A single channel with SBR decodes to two channels from its first block on, whether or not PS data comes later.
  - Agreement with FFmpeg 6.1.1 (`crosscheck_he_aac_pcm.py`, 60 HE-AAC files compared, 0 failures at an RMS of the difference of at most 6 % of FFmpeg's): 0.0 to 1.3 % for SBR, 0.2 to 4 % for parametric stereo (the lower for streams of few envelopes); with libxaac's test decoder on ADTS (by hand): 0.2 % (`fdkaac-he-v2-adts.aac`) and 3 % (`apple-hev2-adts-cbr-st44.aac`). A mono core with SBR whose blocks hold no PS data decodes to two equal channels (FFmpeg does the same). `al_sbr_ps_06_new.mp4`, whose PS starts at its second half, agrees with FFmpeg's reference output to 1 % before and 1.6 to 2 % after PS starts; `al_sbr_ps_04_new.mp4` carries inter-channel phase differences in its PS extension (in 53 of its blocks), which the baseline decoder skips, so its second half differs from FFmpeg's (a listed difference, `DIFFERENCES` of the script), its first half agreeing.
  - The work weight of the stage is measured (`DecoderWorkTests`, the HE-AAC cases): 220 units per output sample.
- **What is not decoded** (the session starts and the decoder says so, `decode.not-possible`; also a stream whose samples are encrypted): downsampled SBR (the extension rate equals the core's), AAC Main, LTP and SSR, ER AAC, USAC, and frames of 960 samples. A block that holds more channel streams than the stream's first block, or a long-window TNS filter of an order above 12 (as libxaac refuses it), is `decode.frame-invalid`. Coupling channel elements are read and not applied (their channels are not output, and `decode.coupling-not-applied` says so); gain control data is read and not applied.
- **Gaps and trims:** a block lost to a cut or a broken syntax is a gap and is not decoded; the next block decodes for its overlap and is withheld ("after a gap", 1,024 samples), and the one after it decodes as in an undamaged stream, but for the bands of noise substitution, whose generator restarted at the gap. ADTS sync loss is a gap too. A block of a frame whose CRC fails is written and left out of the measurements, with the block after it (its overlap). Matroska's DiscardPadding is the end trimming of the track's last block: the parser holds the last blocks of the track until it states it (`DecodeFeed`), so the trim reaches the decoder before the frames it takes samples from. MP4 edit lists and iTunSMPB do not trim the measured range (the lead, 2026-11-26: decision 8 of [lossy-decoders.md](../design/lossy-decoders.md#decisions) takes the stream's own range, option (a), not the container's and the encoder's figures); they stay figures of their own ("Gapless" node), as [samples-definition.md](../design/samples-definition.md) keeps them: the measurements cover the stream's own range, and the priming (1,024 samples of the first block, 2,112 samples by iTunSMPB) is measured.
- **Verification:** the stream stores no checksum over its audio, so `--verify` decodes nothing; the ADTS CRC-16 (over the header and the elements it covers) is declared as a checksum of coded data that covers not all audio, and a stream that stores it reads "unknown" with the tallies (decision 2 of [lossy-decoders.md](../design/lossy-decoders.md#decisions)).
- **Where it keeps the standard's arithmetic instead of libxaac's:** Where libxaac and the standard differ, the decoder keeps the standard's arithmetic, each measured against libxaac's test decoder ([development.md](../development.md#aac-decoding)): libxaac's 16-bit path for TNS filters in one or two channels amplifies the rounding of the coefficients (up to 9,739 LSB, 30 % of full scale, on `apple-win-lc-m4a-q127-cbr64-clicks.m4a`; 57 to 232 LSB on three other files; a build with 32-bit TNS agrees within 22 LSB); its linearly interpolated table of q^(4/3) is off by at most about 12 LSB at loud peaks (the decoder computes the power exactly); its mid/side flags are read for a common window only and never cleared, so they invert intensity bands of a later pair without one (8,111 LSB in `stereo-coding.aac`); and it reuses the first channel's seed for the second channel's noise in grouped short windows (4,862 LSB in `loud-noise-pair.aac`; the decoder gives both channels the same noise in each window). Everything else it follows: the order of the tools, the generator, the TNS ranges and the limit of order 12. FFmpeg's decoder agrees with the decoder to about 10^-8 on the music files in each of the four cases.
- **Accuracy:** the output agrees with FFmpeg 6.1.1's to 1.5 x 10^-8 of full scale (RMS) on the encoder files without noise substitution, and with libxaac's test decoder to an RMS of 0.6 LSB of 16 bits, up to 21 LSB at loud peaks, but for the four cases above ([development.md](../development.md#aac-decoding)).
