# Matroska and WebM

Matroska (`.mka`, `.mkv`) and its subset WebM (`.webm`), built on EBML. One parser, "Matroska", reads both; the file is reported as WebM when the EBML header's DocType is `webm`. Written from RFC 9559 (Matroska) and RFC 8794 (EBML), with the codec IDs of draft-ietf-cellar-codec-20 and the WebM Container Guidelines (webmproject.org/docs/container, cited by address, since a web page cannot be pinned). `matroska_specs.py` downloads the RFCs, the draft and the Matroska element registry (`ebml_matroska.xml`, pinned by commit and SHA-256), all pinned by their SHA-256; `matroska_tables.py` generates the element table (`Elements.cs`, 273 elements with their IDs, names, types, paths and default values, and descriptions) from them and checks it in CI. Ten elements muxers write are in the registry and not in RFC 9559 (EditionFlagHidden, ChapterFlagEnabled, ChapterTrack, ChapterTrackUID, ChapterSkipType, EditionDisplay, EditionString, EditionLanguageIETF, TagBlockAddIDValue and Emphasis); their descriptions cite "the Matroska element registry".

## What is read

- **EBML:** the variable-size integers of IDs and sizes (RFC 8794, section 4), the header (EBMLVersion, EBMLReadVersion, EBMLMaxIDLength, EBMLMaxSizeLength, DocType and its versions; section 11.2), unknown sizes, Void and CRC-32 elements (section 11.3). A CRC-32 is verified (IEEE, little endian, over the siblings that follow it in the parent).
- **Segment:** SeekHead (each position checked against the element it points to), Info (timestamp scale, duration, titles, MuxingApp, WritingApp, UIDs), Tracks with every TrackEntry (audio fields in full; video, subtitle and other tracks at container level only), Cues (each point's track positions checked), Chapters, Attachments and Tags, all as nodes with values and descriptions. The Segment's Tags element is the file's own tags (`NodeKind.Tag`, as MP4's item list is), so the summary's tags, the CSV and GUI Tags columns list it and `--hide tags` hides it.
- **Sampling frequencies:** a SamplingFrequency or OutputSamplingFrequency that is not above 0 (NaN, infinite and negative values too; RFC 9559, sections 5.1.4.1.29.1 and 5.1.4.1.29.2) is `matroska.audio-fields` and is not used: a stream whose SamplingFrequency is invalid has a rate of 0, and a legacy `A_AAC/*/SBR` ID with an invalid OutputSamplingFrequency gets no AudioSpecificConfig worked out, so the AAC parser reports `aac.no-configuration`.
- **ContentEncodings:** the order, scope, type, compression and encryption elements. Header stripping is undone before a frame is routed; encryption (type 1) is reported and never decoded.
- **Clusters:** kept as compact records (about 120 bytes per block), with nodes built when read. Each block's track, timestamp (the Cluster's plus the block's own, which is in Track Ticks and so scaled by the track's TrackTimestampScale, RFC 9559 section 11.2; for a track whose Tracks element comes after its blocks, which section 6.1 allows behind a SeekHead, the times are worked out again once the Segment is read), flags and frames come from SimpleBlock and BlockGroup (Block, BlockDuration, ReferenceBlock, DiscardPadding, BlockAdditions), with all four lacing modes: none, Xiph, fixed-size and EBML (RFC 9559, section 10.3).
- **Unknown sizes:** a Segment or Cluster of unknown size ends where the next element that cannot be its child starts, as a live stream needs. Any other element with an unknown size is a finding.
- **Unknown and misplaced elements:** an element the table does not list, one with a reserved ID or one under the wrong parent is a node and a finding; the walk goes on (RFC 9559, section 7: a reader skips what it does not know). The value of an element the schema does not place where it stands, or under one that is misplaced, is shown but not used, as mkvinfo ("Known element, but invalid at this position") and FFmpeg's per-master tables do: a TimestampScale inside Tracks does not replace the Info's.
- **Strings:** a String or UTF-8 value ends at the first null octet; it and the data after it are ignored (RFC 8794, section 13), so a CodecID of `A_OPUS` padded with nulls and more bytes is `A_OPUS`.
- **EBML limits:** the Body is read with IDs of at least four octets whatever EBMLMaxIDLength says (RFC 9559, section 4.4), and `matroska.header-limits` states the values as the header gives them (an EBMLMaxSizeLength above 8 is reported, not clamped).

## What is routed

- **Audio frames** go to the codec parsers by codec ID, through the host's route `ParsePackets`, one packet per frame as mapped bytes:
  - `A_AAC` to AAC (CodecPrivate is the AudioSpecificConfig); the legacy `A_AAC/MPEG2/*` and `A_AAC/MPEG4/*` IDs have no CodecPrivate, so the AudioSpecificConfig is worked out from the ID (object type MAIN 1, LC 2, SSR 3, LTP 4), the SamplingFrequency, the Channels and, for `/SBR`, the OutputSamplingFrequency (explicit SBR signaling), following ISO/IEC 14496-3, as FFmpeg's `matroskadec.c` does for the common rates, and shown under the CodecID that gives its values;
  - `A_AC3` and `A_EAC3` to AC-3 and E-AC-3, `A_DTS` and its extensions to DTS;
  - `A_VORBIS` to Vorbis (CodecPrivate is the three headers in Xiph lacing, which are passed as the first packets) and `A_OPUS` to Opus (CodecPrivate is the OpusHead; CodecDelay is compared with the pre-skip, `opus.container-delay-differs`);
  - `A_MPEG/L1`, `A_MPEG/L2` and `A_MPEG/L3` are counted at container level (see below).
- **The codec's stream is merged** into the file's streams as MP4's is (codec, sample rate, channels, duration, bitrate, encoder), and the Matroska track's own fields are compared with it: `matroska.sample-rate-differs`, `matroska.channels-differ`.
- **Encryption** (RFC 9559, section 5.1.4.1.31.8 and following) is read from the ContentEncodings of audio tracks, from the highest ContentEncodingOrder down, and the finding and the stream's protection come from one reading of them. The rules, which `matroska_encodings_model.py` states independently and the random check holds the analyzer to:
  - **Defaults:** order 0, scope 1, type 0, ContentCompAlgo 0 (zlib, also for a type 0 without a ContentCompression) and ContentEncAlgo 0 (not encrypted).
  - **Which encodings encrypt:** a ContentEncryption counts for type 1 only; under type 0 it is ignored (`matroska.encryption-ignored`). ContentEncAlgo 0 states that the data are not encrypted (Table 24, also the default): nothing is encrypted and `matroska.encryption-none` says so. A type 1 without a ContentEncryption is encrypted by an unknown algorithm (`matroska.encryption-missing`). Types of 2 or more are not read and not protected. ContentEncAESSettings under another algorithm than AES is ignored (`matroska.aes-settings-not-aes`, Table 25).
  - **Scope** (ContentEncodingScope: bit 1 the frames, bit 2 the CodecPrivate, bit 4 the next ContentEncoding, which players do not support) is a set of bits: other bits are ignored, and scope 0 covers nothing. The CodecPrivate is covered only if the track has one. An encryption that covers nothing (`matroska.encryption-covers-nothing`) protects nothing.
  - **Protection** is reported when some part of the track ends up encrypted: the frames (the stream's samples are encrypted) or the CodecPrivate (they are not). The scheme is "Matroska ContentEncryption" with the algorithm of the first such encryption from the highest order down, and for AES the cipher mode: "Matroska ContentEncryption, AES-CTR" (mode 1), "AES-CBC" (mode 2), "AES, mode N" for another mode, "AES" without one, "DES", "3DES", "Twofish", "Blowfish" and "algorithm N" for the rest. No algorithm is named where none can be trusted. The DRM systems are none (ContentEncKeyID names a key). Nothing is decrypted.
  - **Hiding:** bit 4 of any encoding makes the settings of the next lower-order encoding unreadable (`matroska.encoding-hidden`), and so on down a run of bit 4 encodings; the run is told from its start and a running count of encryptions, so the work is linear in the number of encodings. The settings are the algorithm, the compression settings and a claim of "not encrypted", so a hidden encoding is not read whatever it states, and its type and scope still count. The parts it applies to are encrypted by an unknown algorithm (scheme "Matroska ContentEncryption") when an encryption is hidden further along the run, since which algorithm encrypts them cannot be read, or when the outermost encoding of the run is not an encryption and the hidden encoding is of type 1 (also with ContentEncAlgo 0 or without a ContentEncryption). Otherwise, when the outermost encoding of the run is an encryption (ContentEncAlgo not 0), they are encrypted by it and the scheme names it: its ciphertext hides the settings needed to read the frames. A hidden compression, and a hidden encoding of type 2 or more, under an outermost encoding that does not encrypt is not read and not encrypted.
  - **Equal ContentEncodingOrders** (`matroska.encoding-order`, which RFC 9559 forbids) leave the encodings without an order, so none is trusted: neither the frames nor the CodecPrivate are read (`matroska.track-encoding-not-read`), and the stream is encrypted by an unknown algorithm if any of them is of type 1, else not protected. Both report the container's timing for their length and samples (for Opus less the TrackEntry's CodecDelay, since the CodecPrivate is not trusted; the Clusters' Timestamps are in Segment Ticks and the blocks' in Track Ticks, so only the blocks' are scaled by the TrackTimestampScale); the per-encoding findings are not reported for such a track. The result does not depend on the order of the elements in the file.
  - **Encrypted frames** are not read (`matroska.track-encrypted`, which also names a compression the analyzer does not undo that lies over or under the encryption). When the CodecPrivate is clear, the codec parser is handed it with `AreSamplesEncrypted` and no frame, as the MP4 route does, so the stream has the codec, sample rate and channels the configuration gives, and the length, samples and bitrate come from the blocks' timestamps and sizes (for Opus less its pre-skip, as the clear track's samples are; the other codecs ignore the CodecDelay); otherwise the stream is built from the TrackEntry.
  - **An encrypted or not-undone CodecPrivate with clear frames** is not handed to a codec parser (`matroska.codec-private-encrypted`, `matroska.codec-private-not-read`). Vorbis and Opus need it for their setup, so their frames are not analyzed either and the stream is built from the TrackEntry; the other codecs are routed without a configuration.
  - **A track with no block** still gets its stream, with the protection (`matroska.track-empty`).
- **Header stripping and zlib** are undone for any codec, with the encodings that do not. The AAC, AC-3, DTS and MPEG audio parsers place their nodes at the offsets of contiguous file bytes, which frames put together from stripped or inflated bytes do not have, so a track of those codecs whose frames are zlib-compressed or header-stripped is not routed: `matroska.track-encoding-not-read` says so. bzlib, lzo and the other compressions are not undone. A CodecPrivate that is zlib-compressed or header-stripped is inflated, or has its bytes put back, and handed to the codec parser as its configuration, as the codec parsers of every codec get it (it is read from the CodecPrivate, not from file bytes, so the AAC parser takes it as it takes the AudioSpecificConfig worked out from a legacy codec ID). At most 16 encodings of a part of a track are undone in turn, and a frame inflates to at most 1 MiB (a codec packet is far smaller; a larger one gives the same finding as damaged data). An inflated CodecPrivate may be as large as a CodecPrivate is read (64 MiB), since a Vorbis comment header with pictures can be large. A frame that is no zlib data gives `matroska.frame-encoding-invalid` (the frames before it are analyzed) and a CodecPrivate that is none `matroska.codec-private-invalid`, which stops the analysis of the frames, for every codec.
- **The CodecPrivate** is handed to a codec parser only for the codec IDs whose mapping defines one: A_AAC, A_VORBIS, A_OPUS, A_ALAC and A_TTA1 (A_OPUS/EXPERIMENTAL, which the mapping does not list, is read as A_OPUS). For the others (AC-3, E-AC-3, DTS, MPEG audio, and the legacy A_AAC/MPEG2/* and A_AAC/MPEG4/* IDs: "Initialization: none") a CodecPrivate is `matroska.codec-private-unexpected` and is not read, however it is coded; a legacy AAC ID gets its AudioSpecificConfig from the ID, whatever the CodecPrivate says. FFmpeg uses a CodecPrivate when there is one; Encspect follows the codec mapping (draft-ietf-cellar-codec, section 3.4).
- **Codec IDs without a parser** (FLAC, TrueHD, WavPack, PCM, MLP, RealAudio, QuickTime and the rest) give an Info finding, `matroska.codec-not-analyzed`: their frames are counted and shown as blocks, and their parsers are not written yet (phase 3).
- **Several audio tracks** play together, so their streams share a `PlaybackGroup` (one per Segment): the summary and the CSV count the longest track's duration and samples once instead of adding them up, and two Segments add up. JSON streams carry `"playbackGroup"`. A track whose codec parser reports several streams that play together (E-AC-3 independent substreams) gives all of them the Segment's group; several streams of a track that follow one another (a configuration change) are not grouped.

## What is reported

- A node tree: the EBML Header, the Segment with its elements, every Cluster with its blocks and frames, and a "Track N Frames" node for each audio track whose frames are routed. Cluster nodes are streams (`NodeKind.Stream`) and blocks are frames.
- Streams for the audio tracks and, for a track whose parser cannot read it, a container-level stream with the codec ID, channels, sample rate, bits per sample, duration and the frames counted.
- Findings, all with the prefix `matroska.`:
  - **Structure:** `no-ebml-header`, `header-field`, `header-limits`, `header-version`, `doctype-unknown`, `segment-header-missing`, `segment-size-unknown`, `cluster-size-unknown`, `unknown-size-not-allowed`, `element-header-invalid`, `element-id-long`, `size-too-long`, `element-overruns-parent`, `element-truncated`, `misplaced-element`, `unknown-element`, `deprecated-element`, `too-deep`, `too-many-elements`, `value-size`, `invalid-text`, `uid-length`.
  - **Top level:** `missing-info`, `missing-tracks`, `no-clusters`, `seek-position`, `cue-position`, `crc-mismatch`, `crc-position`, `crc-size`.
  - **Tracks:** `track-field`, `audio-fields`, `track-number-duplicate`, `track-empty`, `track-not-analyzed`, `track-encoding-not-read`, `track-encrypted`, `encoding-order`, `encryption-ignored`, `encryption-none`, `encryption-covers-nothing`, `encoding-hidden`, `encryption-missing`, `aes-settings-not-aes`, `frame-encoding-invalid`, `codec-private-missing`, `codec-private-unexpected`, `codec-private-invalid`, `codec-private-encrypted`, `codec-private-not-read`, `codec-not-analyzed`, `sample-rate-differs`, `channels-differ`.
  - **Clusters and blocks:** `cluster-timestamp-missing`, `cluster-timestamp-position`, `timestamp-scale`, `timestamps-backwards`, `block-short`, `block-too-large`, `block-track-unknown`, `lacing-invalid`, `lacing-not-allowed`, `lacing-single`, `group-no-block`, `group-multiple-blocks`.
  - **Writer:** `writer-claim-differs`, `writer-other-format`.
  - **WebM:** `webm-codec`, `webm-timestamp-scale` (WebM allows VP8, VP9, AV1, Vorbis, Opus and WebVTT, and wants a timestamp scale of 1,000,000).
- A finding points to a node of the tree; those of a block point to its SimpleBlock or Block.

## Channel layout

- For a routed track the layout is the codec parser's (AAC, AC-3, DTS, Vorbis and Opus each name their speakers in their own order); the Matroska track's `Channels` is compared with it.
- A track counted at container level has only its `Channels` count and no layout: Matroska's ChannelPositions element is not in RFC 9559, so no speaker is named.

## Encoder and writer

- **The encoder** is decided by the codec parsers, from the frames they read. The Matroska parser names none.
- **The writer** is the software that put the audio into the file. The Segment's WritingApp and MuxingApp (RFC 9559, sections 5.1.2.14 and 5.1.2.13: "Writing application", "Muxing application or library") are claims of the writer role at low confidence, the evidence pointing at the element. Each Segment has its own Info, so its claims reach the tracks of that Segment only.
  - The WritingApp is weighed first, so the application that wrote the file outweighs the library that muxed it. ffmpeg's muxer version ("Lavf60.16.100", in both elements) names FFmpeg. A libavcodec encoder ID ("Lavc60.31.102 aac") in either element is a claim of the encoder role instead, as in the tags; the codec parsers decide whether it names an encoder.
  - mkvmerge writes its libraries in the MuxingApp ("libebml v1.4.5 + libmatroska v1.7.1") and the program in the WritingApp. The library never conflicts with the program that uses it. ffmpeg's muxer is such a library too: matroskaenc.c always writes its version ("Lavf60.16.100") as the MuxingApp and writes the `encoding_tool` metadata, when there is any, as the WritingApp, so a "Lavf" MuxingApp under the WritingApp of other software (HandBrake, say) is the library under it and no conflict (`external-ffmpeg-encoding-tool`, `writer-lavf-under-application`); a tag that holds the same text is not. The codec parsers get the Matroska libraries only when there is no WritingApp (then it names the writer, as "libebml v1.4.5 + libmatroska v1.7.1"); otherwise the Writer node the Matroska parser makes holds it as evidence.
  - A MuxingApp and a WritingApp that name different software are one `matroska.writer-claim-differs` (Info) for the Segment, however many tracks it has. Software known to write another kind of stream (LAME on a PCM or FLAC track) is `matroska.writer-other-format` (Info) for each such stream.
  - AAC, AC-3 and DTS weigh the Segment's claims with the file's, the tags' first (the order the router gives every codec parser). MPEG audio is routed too, but its parser reads only a contiguous payload and analyzes no packets, so its stream is a container-level one, like the unrouted codings below. A contained Vorbis stream weighs them first, then its own comment; a comment naming other software than the container is `vorbis.writer-claim-differs`, and the container's writer is the stream's.
  - Streams whose parser names none (MPEG audio, PCM, FLAC and the other codings no parser reads, Opus, which has no comment packet in a track) get the Writer from the Matroska parser: a Writer node in the Segment's Info element, whose elements are its evidence (under the stream's node when the Segment has no Info). The claims are weighed in the same order, and a conflict is one finding for the Segment.
  - A codec parser's claims stay with its stream: the comment of one track is never weighed for another.

## Detection

- An EBML header at the start with a DocType of `matroska` or `webm`, followed by a Segment: class 100. A header with no Segment after it: 90. A header that does not hold (an ID or size that is not an EBML header's, or no DocType): 70. Any other DocType: not Matroska.
- A Segment at the start with a consistent child, as in a file whose header was cut off: 80. A run of Top-Level Elements (or a Cluster with a Timestamp) from the start: 60.
- `DeclaredEnd` is the end of the Segment when its size is known. For a Segment of unknown size (a live recording) it is the end of the Top-Level Elements and, in a Cluster of unknown size, of the children the schema places in a Cluster: a trailing ID3v1 or APE tag after them is the file's, not an element's. An element the schema does not list is skipped by its size (RFC 9559, section 7) and belongs to the elements, with the Cluster children after it, only once a Top-Level Element follows it or the elements run to the end of the data without a break; a Void, a CRC-32 or a Cluster child after it does not vouch for it. A known element that runs past the end of the data (a recording cut short) owns the rest of the data, whatever comes before it. Tags are told apart from elements with these tests, weighed against the elements' own evidence (CLAUDE.md: a signature alone is never enough, the strongest evidence wins):
  - An unknown element that starts with the signature of a tag (`APETAGEX`, `ID3`, `3DI`, `TAG`, `LYRICSBEGIN`) is vouched for only by a Top-Level Element after it, not by the end of the data or by an element that runs past it (an APEv2 header reads as the ID 0x4150 and a size of 1,364 bytes, and its data may look like any element).
  - An APEv2 footer at the end of the data (or 128 bytes before it, ahead of an ID3v1 tag) places a tag: it has the signature, a tag size that fits the data and, where its flags announce a header, `APETAGEX` there (otherwise the tag starts at its items, as the APE parser has it). The walk over the elements ends at the tag's start only if it lands exactly on it, at an element's start. A cut element ahead of the tag, one that runs past the end of the data or into the tag, wins: the walk returns at it and the tag is lost (for a recording cut short in its last block). The items are not examined: flags and keys are the APE parser's to report.
  - The elements win against the footer when they vouch for their bytes from the tag's start: a Top-Level Element (a Segment child with a four-octet ID) starts there, or the elements from there run to the end of the data, following Clusters of unknown size into their children, through known elements and unknown ones, the last of them ending exactly at the end of the data. An element that runs past the end counts only after one whole element: the first element's header may be the first item's length bytes of a tag, as in a tag whose first value size reads as a Cluster child. Otherwise the tag wins, damaged or not, and the APE parser reports its damage (`ape.items-invalid` and the like).
  - A Top-Level Element that follows an unknown element still vouches for what comes before it.
  - A tag with a damaged footer, one the walk does not reach at an element's start, or one after a cut element may still be taken for elements; the tag parsers then report a near miss.
- `--as matroska` (also `mkv`, `mka`, `webm`) reads any bytes as Matroska.

## Limits

- Elements outside Clusters become nodes up to 250,000 in all (`matroska.too-many-elements`); nesting is read to 40 levels (`matroska.too-deep`). Past the budget the other Top-Level Elements of a Segment are skipped by their sizes (one Unparsed Data node for each run of them, which the finding names by where it starts) and the Clusters are still read, so what the skipped elements may hold (Info, Tracks) is not reported missing.
- The elements at the root (EBML Headers, Segments) count against the node budget, each Segment as 16 elements and each audio track as 32 for the stream, the codec parser's nodes and the series it is routed to (about 30 KB), so memory stays bounded: a live stream of concatenated documents (RFC 8794, section 11.1: each has its EBML Header; RFC 9559, section 23.2) with an Info element and one audio track each is read in full up to about 3,600 Segments, and fewer when each holds more elements; past the budget the rest is one Unparsed Data node. Every Cluster counts as a record.
- Findings that stand for one element each (a track without a TrackNumber, a Segment without Info) are listed up to 100 of a kind, then counted in one more finding of the same code; a valid file reaches this only with more than 100 tracks that each raise one (`matroska.codec-not-analyzed`, say).
- A Cluster's blocks and elements are kept up to 6,000,000 records in all, and the finding points at the Unparsed Data that covers the rest of the Cluster; a block of more than 64 MiB is not read (`matroska.block-too-large`).
- The frames of laced blocks that go to a codec parser (or whose track is not known yet) are counted against a budget of 4,000,000 in all (`matroska.too-many-elements`), since a block of seven bytes can state 256 frames of no bytes; fixed-size lacing keeps no sizes. The finding points at the first block that is not read.
- Memory is bounded by the compact records: `MemoryTests` hold the blocks to about 120 bytes each, the ContentEncodings of a track and the inflated frames; `ComplexityTests` hold the routing of thousands of tracks over millions of blocks (each track reads its own blocks, found in one pass) and thousands of hiding ContentEncodings to linear time.

## Not covered

- MPEG audio frames are counted at container level only: the MPEG parser reads a contiguous payload, and Matroska frames are packets.
- FLAC, TrueHD, WavPack, PCM, MLP and the RealAudio and QuickTime IDs have no parser here (phase 3).
- bzlib and lzo compression and all encryption are reported, not undone; zlib and header stripping are undone for any codec, but only Opus and Vorbis take the frames put together from other bytes (the other codec parsers need contiguous file bytes).
- Protection of tracks that are not audio is not reported: Matroska creates no stream that is not audio, so the encryption of a video or subtitle track is not reported (the track gets `matroska.track-not-analyzed` and its elements are in the tree). ASF does report the protection of its video streams.
- `A_MS/ACM` (a WAVEFORMATEX) is not routed.
- Video, subtitles and buttons are read at container level only; Chapters, Attachments and Tags are shown, not interpreted (a Tags value is not matched against a stream).
- DiscardPadding is read and shown but not applied: the Opus sample count of a track includes the padding at the end that DiscardPadding says a player drops.
- Only the WebM restrictions on codecs and the timestamp scale are checked, not the rest of the guidelines.
