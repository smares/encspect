# Samples: what a stream's units declare, and lost samples

Status: draft, revised 2026-11-02 after an independent review, for the project lead; nothing in it is built. It designs the lead's decision of 2026-11-02 that "samples" means one thing across formats. Claims about the repository were checked at `main` 465988f and on the branches `fix/wma-pro-trim` (a5035f1), `feat/wma-container-duration` (e5cb5b2) and `ci/official-decode-aac` (b03bdfc; also read: `ci/official-decode-ogg` 76a16c4 and `ci/official-decode-tta` ba7bbc0). A claim no code, golden or document confirms is marked **unverified**. Figures below were computed by scripts over the committed goldens (`tests/fixtures/**/*.report.json`) and the branches' `official-decoders.json`; a figure the goldens cannot give is marked **for the pull request**.

- **The decision:** a stream's `samples` are the samples its units declare, at the sample rate the analyzer determines. Units that are damaged or cannot be decoded stay in `samples`, and their declared samples are reported apart as lost samples.
- **Why:** the lead's gaps-not-concealment rule ([PLAN.md](../PLAN.md), "Later", lossy decoders; [official-decoder-crosschecks.md](official-decoder-crosschecks.md)) says that a frame a decoder cannot decode is a gap whose samples count as lost. Today each format counts such a unit its own way, so the cross-checks against the official decoders reach a different status for the same situation in each format (see [Today](#today)).
- **What this note settles:** the definition and how trimming composes with it ([Definition](#definition)), lost samples ([Lost samples](#lost-samples)), what a container must tell a codec parser ([What containers hand over](#what-containers-hand-over)), what changes per format ([Changes per format](#changes-per-format)), how the cross-check statuses follow ([Cross-check statuses](#cross-check-statuses)), the rollout ([Rollout](#rollout)) and the questions for the lead ([Open questions](#open-questions)).
- **Out of scope:** which sample rate a stream has (SBR doubling, a DTS extension's rate, the rate iTunSMPB counts at); the definition takes the rate as the analyzer determines it. The AAC items that are rate questions (`sbr-in-frames-failing-crc.aac`, `sbr-signaled-absent.m4a`, `faac-2.1-he-51-adts.aac`) are not touched.

## Today

The examples the lead named, with the code that makes them:

- **TTA** leaves a frame cut by the end of the data out (`tta.frame-cut`; "a frame the data cuts short is not counted", `Explain.StatSamples`). The lead kept that on 2026-11-01 for the four cut-last-frame variants (`keep`); these variants are derived files that are not committed. The committed `built-cut-*` files have no reference at all (`tta -d` stops on them).
- **MP4** leaves out a sample that does not lie wholly inside the file (`Mp4Parser.Tracks.cs`, `InFile`, `SampleRanges` and `Packets`): `truncated.m4a` counts 9,216, without the sample of which the file holds 1 byte.
- **Raw ADTS** counts a cut frame by its header: `truncated.aac` counts 12,288 with a 16-of-21-byte last frame.
- **Opus** counts packets that libopus refuses by their TOC bytes (`opus-packets-corrupt.opus`, `explained`).
- **Ogg** streams whose headers the decoder refuses still have their packets counted (`keep`).
- **WMA Professional** follows Microsoft's decoder on undamaged streams with the length prefix (`fix/wma-pro-trim`). Standard WMA is to follow the same overlap-tail rule (the lead, 2026-11-02); `feat/wma-container-duration` is being reworked to carry it.

The full inventory is in [Changes per format](#changes-per-format).

## Words

- **Unit:** what a format codes one at a time and states the length of in samples: a frame, a block, a packet, an access unit, a superframe, a raw data block; for PCM, a sample frame.
- **Declared samples** of a unit: the samples per channel that the format's decoding process assigns to the unit, read from the unit's own syntax (a frame header, a TOC byte, a mode number) or, where the unit has none, from the stream's fixed parameters (a TTA or Monkey's Audio frame length, an ALAC cookie's frame length). The lead's "positions the units declare" and this term are the same; the note, the descriptions and the docs use "declared samples".
- **`samples`** is the sum, over the counted units, of their declared samples (less the trims of the next section). A repeated or overlapping unit counts again, as it does today (the decoy frame of `flac/built-damaged-frame-after-frame.flac`, `flac/built-frame-repeated.flac`, an Ogg page the analyzer counts and `opusdec` skips); samples here are not a set of distinct positions.
- **Coded stage:** the sum of the declared samples of the counted units. **Trimmed stage:** the coded stage less the trims the stream states. Both terms are those of [official-decoder-crosschecks.md](official-decoder-crosschecks.md). The parser's figure for the coded stage is **Coded Samples** (a statistics node; [question 7](#open-questions) for the names it has today).
- **Cut unit:** a counted unit whose declared length in bytes is more than the bytes present (the data ends first, or the next accepted unit starts first). The declared length is the unit's own (an MPEG audio, AC-3, DTS or ADTS frame length), the container's (an MP4 sample size, a Matroska block size, an ASF media object size), or, for a frame with no length field (FLAC, Monkey's Audio, ALAC), the frame the data ends inside.
- **Lost samples:** the declared samples, at the coded stage, of counted units that are cut or that the parser cannot read (its syntax breaks, or a check that guards the syntax fails and the parser stops reading the unit). It is what the parser can show, not a statement that the audio cannot be rebuilt: a cut unit may still hold all its coded audio (see [question 14](#open-questions)).
- **Missing samples:** positions that no unit in the file declares but that the stream's numbering or a header total shows (a FLAC sample number that jumps, a TTA header count above the frames present). They are neither samples nor lost samples here ([question 5](#open-questions)).
- **Stated samples:** a count the stream or container states without units the analyzer reads (an encrypted track's timing, a WAV fact chunk, the ASF header's play duration). It is not declared samples.

## Definition

### The rule

1. **A unit counts** when the file holds the syntax that declares its samples: its header, TOC byte or mode bits for a unit that describes itself; its first byte, for a unit whose length in samples comes from the stream's parameters (TTA, Monkey's Audio, ALAC) or from an index (an MP4 sample table entry); all of its bytes for a PCM sample frame, which has no declaration but its bytes. A unit with no byte in the file is not counted ([question 2](#open-questions)).
2. **Its samples** are those it declares, whether or not it can be decoded: a cut, refused or syntactically broken unit counts in full. Whether a unit is cut is decided by its declared length against the bytes present, not by what ends it: a unit that the next unit's sync cuts before its declared length is cut, also where the declaration is what was damaged (the three cases in [question 14](#open-questions)).
3. **A unit that declares nothing counts nothing:** bytes skipped while searching for sync (`aac.sync-lost`, `flac.data-skipped`), a Vorbis packet that codes no block (empty, not audio, an undefined mode: the decoder skips it, Vorbis I 4.3.1), an Opus part whose frames would last more than 120 ms (`PartRecord.Samples` gives 0), a Matroska block whose frames are not read (`matroska.block-short`, `matroska.lacing-invalid`), a WavPack block whose header is cut (`built-truncated-header.wv`).
4. **A unit the analyzer cannot place** for lack of the stream's parameters counts nothing: TrueHD units before the first major sync (`truehd.units-before-major-sync`), whose sampling rate is not known yet. TrueHD units that need a major sync the stream lost are a different case (see [TrueHD](#changes-per-format)).
5. **A stream whose units are not read** (a track whose samples are encrypted, a coding without a parser) keeps its stated samples and has no lost samples. Encryption alone does not make units unread: the frames of the encrypted TTA files (`tta.audio-format-encrypted`; `built-audio-format-2.tta`, `external-libtta-16-stereo-password.tta`) are read, their frame CRC-32s checked, and their lost samples computed like any other TTA's.
6. **Samples a limit skips** (records caps, `aac.streams-limited`, `mp4.samples-overlap`) are outside the definition. Where a parser counts units past a cap from their headers, as the ADTS parser does (`Counted Frames` past 2,097,152 records), they count, and they are lost where the header pass sees a cut, so a cut last frame is lost in a short stream and in a long one. The samples past `mp4.samples-overlap` have bytes in the file (they lie over other samples); they are not analyzed because of the byte budget, so they fall under this rule and not under rule 1.

### Per format family

- **Frames of a fixed size per header:** MPEG audio (384, 1,152 or 576 per frame by layer and version), AC-3 and E-AC-3 (256 per audio block, the blocks from the header), DTS (the core's blocks of 32, at the rate the stream delivers), ADTS (1,024 per raw data block, at the output rate, the blocks from `number_of_raw_data_blocks_in_frame`; [question 4](#open-questions)), ADIF (1,024 per block read), Musepack (1,152 per frame), TrueHD and MLP (a fixed number per access unit at the rate of the last major sync: 40 per unit at 48 kHz in the committed fixtures, 640 per 16 units, [truehd-format.md](truehd-format.md)).
- **Packets with their own duration:** Opus (the frames of the TOC and the frame count byte times the frame duration, RFC 6716 section 3.1), Vorbis (each block returns a quarter of the previous block and a quarter of its own, the first nothing, Vorbis I 4.3.8), Speex (frames per packet times the frame size from the header; see [Ogg Speex](#changes-per-format)).
- **Units placed by a container's index:** MP4 (each sample of the sample table, counted by its codec parser's declaration; the `stts` durations are the container's statement), Matroska and WebM (each frame of a block, counted by its codec parser), ASF (the media objects' frames, counted by the WMA parsers; the header's play duration is the stated `Container Samples` of `feat/wma-container-duration`, not declared samples).
- **Lossless formats with checksums:** FLAC (the frame header's block size), WavPack (`block_samples` of the first block of a set), Monkey's Audio (the header's blocks per frame, the final frame's own count), TTA (the frame length from the rate, 256/245 of a second; the last frame the remainder of the header's count), TrueHD (above), ALAC (the cookie's frame length, or the count an element states), WMA Lossless (each frame's samples), PCM (one sample per sample frame of `nBlockAlign` bytes).

### Trimming

- **`samples` is the trimmed stage where the codec's own syntax or its normative mapping states a trim,** as today, and the coded stage elsewhere. The trims applied today, kept:
  - Opus: the pre-skip of the identification header (RFC 7845 sections 4.2 and 5.1) and the end trimming of the last granule position (section 4.4);
  - Vorbis and Speex: the start and end trimming of the granule positions;
  - Musepack: the synthesis filter's delay of 481 and the end of the last frame that is not audio (libmpcdec r495), capped by the header's count;
  - WMA Professional and Lossless: the frames' trims; WMA Professional on `fix/wma-pro-trim` follows Microsoft's decoder (next section);
  - TrueHD: the samples an end of stream marker drops.
- **Container presentation and encoder metadata stay figures of their own** and are not applied, as today: MP4 edit lists ("Samples (Edit List)") and iTunSMPB ("Samples (iTunSMPB)"), the LAME and Xing delay and padding ("Gapless" "Sample Count"), Matroska's DiscardPadding ([matroska.md](../formats/matroska.md): read and shown, not applied). Opus in Matroska takes the pre-skip from the OpusHead, which the parser compares with CodecDelay.
- **A coded-stage figure stands next to every trimmed `samples`,** named **Coded Samples**. Today it exists for Opus and Vorbis ("Decoded Samples") and for Speex and Musepack ("Coded Samples"). It does not exist for WMA Professional, WMA Lossless and TrueHD: "Trimmed Samples" on the WMA branches is the sum of the trims the frames state (it does not follow the cuts of Microsoft's rule), not a coded-stage figure, and TrueHD's end-of-stream trim has no figure. For these three formats the pull request adds a Coded Samples node where it differs from `samples` (frames times the frame size for WMA, the units' samples for TrueHD), so that a coded-stage Lost Samples has a figure to compare with ([question 7](#open-questions)).
- **Lost samples are counted at the coded stage,** inside or outside the trimmed range: a lost first Opus packet inside the pre-skip is lost too. Reason: the measurements read untrimmed positions ([spectral-analysis.md](spectral-analysis.md), "Lossy formats later"), and a coded-stage figure compares with them without a second trimming rule.

### The decoder-rule figures of WMA

- **For WMA, "declared samples" on an undamaged stream means the owner's decoder rule, computed from the syntax.** WMA has no public specification. Microsoft's start cut when no trim is stated (F / 2 + L_first / 2) and the tail (F − L_last) / 2 are decoder behavior that black-box runs found, not a trim the codec's syntax states. On `fix/wma-pro-trim` the rule is computed from the frames' own syntax: the number of frames N, the frame size F, the first and last subframe sizes and the first and last frames' trims (E = N × F + (F − L_last) / 2, less the start and end cuts; [wma-pro-format.md](wma-pro-format.md), "What Microsoft's decoder outputs"). It is declared samples in the sense that it is computed from the frames and not sized from a header.
- **The overlap-tail rule for standard WMA is the same kind of figure,** carried by `feat/wma-container-duration` (the lead, 2026-11-02): no separate pull request. Microsoft's output for the Windows-written standard WMA files equals the play duration less the preroll rounded up to 512 samples (256 at 22.05 kHz), a fit on those files only; the analyzer does not take it as `samples` and keeps it visible as `Container Samples`.
- **Damaged WMA streams:** each invalid frame counts F and is lost, and so are the frames the superframe's frame count declares after an invalid frame that ends its superframe (standard WMA, `StdText.StatInvalidFrames`). How Microsoft's tail and cuts apply to a damaged stream is not known until black-box runs on damage exist ([asf-wma.md](../formats/asf-wma.md), "Reference for sample counts"); until then the cuts of the undamaged rule are applied to the frames counted, **unverified** against Microsoft.
- **A frame started and not finished** (`wma.frame-unfinished`: the last packet ends with bits after a frame whose trailer bit says another follows) is a unit whose first bits are present, so it counts F and is lost. That changes the two `keep` entries of `fix/wma-pro-trim`: `pro-stereo-128-cut-open-3` (Windows 7,104, the analyzer 6,144) and `cut-open-27` (Windows 55,808, the analyzer 55,296). They are not untouched: their `samples` gain one frame and their `keep` stays with a larger difference ([question 9](#open-questions)).

### Duration, frames and bitrate

- **`duration` is `samples` divided by the rate,** so it includes lost samples.
- **Frame counts and average bitrates differ by format today, and keep their basis.** The frame count counts the cut unit in some formats and not in others; the average bitrate is the bytes the parser counts over the time its samples last. Measured on the goldens:

  | Fixture | Frames | Samples | Average bitrate | Basis today |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | `ac3/truncated.ac3` | 6 | 9,216 | 179.5 kbps | cut frame in both, so it lowers the bitrate: (5 × 768 + 468) bytes over 9,216 samples |
  | `dts/truncated.dts` | 6 | 3,072 | 357.5 kbps | cut frame in both |
  | `adts/truncated.aac` | 12 | 12,288 | 11.8 kbps | cut frame in both |
  | `wavpack/built-truncated-last-block.wv` | 3 blocks | 24,000 | 685.1 kbps | cut block in both |
  | `mpeg/truncated-last-frame.mp3` | 5 | 5,760 | 128.0 kbps | cut frame in neither: 5 whole frames over their time |
  | `flac/built-cut-mid-frame.flac` | 4 (cut one in) | 12,288 | 265.8 kbps | frame count includes it, samples do not |
  | `tta/built-cut-mid-frame.tta` | 1 (cut one out) | 46,080 | 1.56 kbps | cut frame in neither |
  | `truehd/built-unit-cut.thd` | 80 access units (cut one in) | 3,160 | 1,059.1 kbps | frame count includes it, samples do not |

- **No frame count changes.** The formats that count a cut unit's samples for the first time (MPEG audio, FLAC, TTA, TrueHD, Monkey's Audio, ALAC, Musepack, MP4 and Matroska codecs) keep dividing their bytes by the time of the units whose bytes are all present, so their `averageBitrate` does not change; the formats that already count the cut unit in both keep their formula, and the cut unit lowers their bitrate as it does today. The pull request holds this with a test: `averageBitrate` and every frame-count node are equal before and after in every golden of the changed formats, and the `changed` lists of the golden-change files name only `samples`, `duration`, the statistics text that repeats them and, above 0, Lost Samples. Each parser's "Samples" description says which units it counts.

## Lost samples

### Name and meaning

- **Name:** `LostSamples` on `StreamInfo`, `lostSamples` in the stream's JSON, "Lost Samples" as a statistics node and in the text report. The wording is uniform: "lost samples" in prose, "Lost Samples" for the node, nothing else ("lost figure", "damaged samples", "missing samples" are not used for it).
- **The measurements already use the name** (`StreamMeasurements.LostSamples`, JSON `measurements.lostSamples`, the text "N gaps skipped M samples"): the positions the decoder's output skipped. The two figures are not the same by definition, and the note says so instead of hiding it:
  - **The stream's `lostSamples`** is the declared samples of the lost units, from the parser, with no decoding. It is a subset of `samples` and of the coded stage.
  - **The measured `lostSamples`** is the positions the output lacks between its first and last block: the lost units the decoder dropped, plus the missing samples at jumps. Today it leaves out a lost first or last unit ([question 6](#open-questions)).
  - **Choice made here (the lead decides, [question 8](#open-questions)):** one name, two documented bases, with different JSON positions (`streams[].lostSamples` and `streams[].measurements.lostSamples`) and a description that states the basis on each. No schema change follows. The alternatives are splitting the measured figure into lost units and missing samples (a change of `measurements`), or a distinct name for the stream's figure.
- **A figure, never a verdict:** a count in samples with its basis in the description ("the declared samples of units cut by the end of the data, units that break the syntax, and units that cannot be read until the stream's next sync point"). It raises no finding; the findings on the units say what happened to them. The text never says the file is broken or incomplete because of it.

### What counts

- **Lost:** counted units that are
  - cut: their declared length is more than the bytes present (`mpeg.frame-cut`, `mpeg.frame-truncated`, `ac3.frame-damaged`, `ac3.frame-truncated`, `aac.frame-damaged`, `aac.frame-truncated`, `dts.frame-damaged`, `dts.frame-truncated`, `flac.frame-cut`, `tta.frame-cut`, `truehd.unit-cut`, `wavpack.block-truncated`, `monkeys-audio.frame-cut`, `musepack.frame-truncated`, and the packets that the containers flag, see [What containers hand over](#what-containers-hand-over));
  - unreadable because their syntax breaks: the parser's unreadable units (`CodedFrame.IsUnreadable`: `flac.subframe-invalid`, WavPack's refused blocks, ALAC's unreadable frames, `alac.element-invalid`, `alac.residual-invalid`, `alac.frame-overrun`), Opus parts that break RFC 6716 section 3.4 (`opus.packet-r1` to `-r7`, `opus.self-delimiting`), AAC raw data blocks whose syntax breaks (the codes are [question 10](#open-questions)), WMA invalid frames;
  - unreadable because the stream's state was lost: TrueHD units after damage until the next major sync (`truehd.units-after-damage`), WMA frames after an invalid frame in the same superframe.
- **Checksums** (the rule behind [question 1](#open-questions)): a failing checksum alone does not make a unit lost. A unit is lost when the parser refuses to read it. The TrueHD major sync CRC-16 and the unit parity nibble guard syntax the parser needs, and the parser stops reading after a failure (`built-sync-crc-mismatch.thd`, `built-parity-mismatch.thd`), so those units are lost. A checksum over coded data whose syntax reads (FLAC CRC-16, MPEG audio and AC-3 CRCs, the TTA frame CRC-32, the WavPack block checksum, TrueHD substream CRC-8 and parity, the Ogg page CRC) leaves the unit counted and not lost. Encspect's FLAC decoder decodes such frames (`IsDamaged`), so the measurements show no loss there either.
- **Not lost:** units a decoder refuses for a reason the parser does not check (they appear only in the measurements, as `decode.frame-invalid` and a position jump); a stream whose headers the decoder refuses as a whole (that is `decode.not-possible` for a decoder, and the 2026-11-01 `keep` for the Ogg streams stands).
- **Not known:** a stream whose units are not read (rule 5). There is no null: `LostSamples` is a plain `long`, 0 for these streams and for streams whose units all read. "Not read" is told by `Protection` (an encrypted track) or a `*.codec-not-analyzed` finding; the invariant test uses those two signals, as `DecoderSessionInvariantTests` does.

### Where it lives and how it is computed

- **A `StreamInfo` field** (`long`), since front ends read kinds and streams, never node names, and a "Lost Samples" statistics node with its `Description`, **both only where the figure is above 0**. Reason: the JSON writer writes stream fields unconditionally unless a condition holds (`ReportJsonWriter.cs` writes `multilingualChannels` and `bitsPerSample` that way), and the 2,602 `.report.json` goldens would otherwise all change. With the conditional write, goldens change only where lost samples are above 0 or where `samples` changes: at most 87 goldens in 16 folders now ([Rollout](#rollout)). An absent `lostSamples` reads as 0.
- **Without decoding,** from what each parser already records per unit: the cut flag (`Entry.IsTruncated` in MPEG audio, `Frame.Cut` and the truncated state in AC-3, `walk.IsCut` in FLAC), the unreadable status, the Opus `PartStatus`, the TrueHD units after damage, the WMA invalid frames, the container's flag for a packet ([What containers hand over](#what-containers-hand-over)). The figure is the sum of the declared samples of the flagged units, added in the same pass that adds them to `samples`, with no extra memory per unit. A set of WavPack blocks with one refused block is lost once by the set's `block_samples`, not by each block's.
- **In `CodedFrame`,** `IsDamaged` covers both a failed checksum and a cut today. No new flag is needed for the figure, since it comes from the parser's records. What the decoded formats need is that every counted unit, lost ones included, reaches the session with its `FirstSample` and `Samples`: FLAC hands the cut frame over (`IsDamaged`; its decoder fails with `EndOfStreamException`), Monkey's Audio hands it over too (its decoder reports that the coded data ends), WavPack marks it unreadable, and **TrueHD hands unreadable units with `Samples` 0 and does not advance `FirstSample`** (below). With every counted unit handed over, the host computes leading and trailing loss for [question 6](#open-questions) from the `CodedFrame`s, with no new parser API.
- **Output:**
  - JSON `lostSamples` after `samples` in the stream, above 0 only (a field is added and none changes meaning, so `schemaVersion` stays 1);
  - the text report's stream line and the GUI's stream summary, above 0 only;
  - a CSV column `Lost Samples` appended at the end, as the measured columns were ([cli.md](../cli.md)): the sum of `lostSamples` over the same streams the `Samples` column adds, that is, the audio streams, with the streams of one playback group represented by the one of the longest duration (`ReportSummary.cs`); 0 where none.
  - [cli.md](../cli.md) states that `lostSamples` is left out where 0.

### The relation to the measurements

- **The stage.** The invariant holds at the coded stage, against the coded stage figure and not `samples`. For Opus, Vorbis, Speex, Musepack, WMA and TrueHD `samples` is trimmed and the measurements read untrimmed positions.
- **The statement.** For a stream whose decoder produces no audio for exactly the units the parser flags, with no unit overlapping or repeating another, and with [question 6](#open-questions) (a): measured `Samples` equals the coded stage less the stream's `LostSamples`, and the measured `lostSamples` is the stream's plus the missing samples. Without the question 6 (a) change a lost first or last unit is in no measured figure.
- **Where it fails, and what the pull request changes:**
  - **TrueHD positions do not advance over lost units.** `FirstSample` and `Samples` grow only for readable units, and unreadable units go to the session with `Samples` 0 (`TrueHd/Analysis.cs`: the `IsUnreadable` frame). Under the new count the stream's samples grow by the lost units, so the parser must advance `FirstSample` by them and hand them over with their declared samples (40 per unit at 48 kHz, the rate of the last major sync). That puts a gap in the middle of the decoded stream, so the FFT frames, gating blocks and runs of the measurer reset there, and the measurement goldens of the damaged TrueHD fixtures change. This is a listed change of the TrueHD commit.
  - **Overlapping units.** In `flac/built-damaged-frame-after-frame.flac` the unreadable decoy frame claims sample 4,096 while the stream is at 8,192. Its 4,096 are counted (28,096) and would be lost, but the measurer sees no jump. The statement excludes overlapping and repeated units, and these fixtures are exceptions of the test with their reason.
  - **Trailing and leading lost units** are in the measured figure only with [question 6](#open-questions) (a).
- **The test.** `Verification.DecodedSamples` (`StreamVerification.DecodedSamples`, JSON `decodedSamples`, the sum of what the decoder returned) is available under `--verify` and is far cheaper than `--measure` over every decoded fixture. The invariant test compares `decodedSamples` with the coded stage less `LostSamples` for the fixtures of `flac`, `wavpack`, `monkeys-audio`, `truehd`, `wav` and `aiff` (the formats that have a decoder), with a short exception list. The `--measure` comparison runs on the fixtures of the damaged and cut cases only.
- **The GUI.** `SampleLocator` places frames from `Total` = `StreamInfo.Samples` and the end of what was measured, `FirstPosition + Samples + LostSamples` (`Gui/ViewModels/SampleLocator.cs`; spectral-analysis.md, "Finding a frame by time", which says it "holds only where the stream's sample count includes the samples of the frames the decoder lost"). Once `samples` includes a cut last frame that the decoder does not output, the end of the measuring is earlier than the stream's end by the lost tail and every frame's mark would shift early. That is why [question 6](#open-questions) (a) is part of the decision. The tests that hold the bounds are `SampleLocatorTests`, `FrameMapGraphTests` and `AudioPanelTests` in `Encspect.Gui.Tests` (and `AudioGraphs.SamplesPerColumn`, which adds the measured lost samples), with a fixture of a cut last frame added; `TrueHdRunsTests` for the TrueHD gap.

## What containers hand over

**The gap.** A codec parser learns that a handed packet is cut only if the packet says so. `MappedBytes` carries `EndPosition`, `EndPositionField`, `IsEndOfStream` and `FollowsGap`, and no cut flag and no declared length (`MappedBytes.cs`). A frame that states its own length (ADTS, AC-3, DTS, MPEG audio, TrueHD access units) shows its cut itself. A raw AAC access unit, an ALAC frame, an Opus, Vorbis or Speex packet, a FLAC or TTA frame inside a container, and a WMA media object do not: a cut Opus code-0 packet reads as a valid shorter packet, and a cut raw AAC block reads as a syntax error or as nothing.

**The change in Core.Abstractions** (the lead decides it: [question 16](#open-questions)).
- `MappedBytes` gets two members for a routed packet, next to the existing four: `IsCut` (the container knows the bytes are not the whole packet) and `DeclaredLength` (`long?`: the length the container states, where it states one and it is more than `Length`; null otherwise, for an Ogg packet that continues for an unknown length). Both default to "not cut". `ParseStream` needs no member: a payload handed in one piece is a `ByteReader` whose end the parser sees.
- A codec parser that cannot see the cut itself counts a flagged packet by its declaration (TOC, mode bits, configuration: 1,024 per raw AAC block, the cookie's frame length for ALAC) and records it as cut and lost. A parser that sees the cut itself ignores the flag.
- The host's `ParserRouter.ParsePackets` passes the packets through unchanged; the flag lives on the packet.

**What each container supplies:**
- **MP4.** `Packets` today yields only samples wholly inside the file (`InFile`). It yields also a sample whose first byte is in the file, with the bytes present, `IsCut` and `DeclaredLength` = the `stsz` size (`truncated.m4a`: 1 byte of the last sample, then 10 samples with no byte at all, which are not handed over and fall under [question 2](#open-questions)). `SampleRanges` includes the bytes present of that sample, so the node's ranges and the packets agree. MPEG audio in one stretch goes through `ParseStream` and needs nothing.
- **Matroska and WebM.** The block's declared end is known (`ChildEnd`: `matroska.element-truncated` when it runs past the file). The block record gets a cut bit (`BlockStatus`). - **ASF.** `MediaObjects` drops an incomplete object today (`asf.object-incomplete`: "They are dropped"), except that Windows Media Audio Lossless gets the object the file ends inside (`isKeepingPartial`). **Order of the commits.** Codec parsers first (they accept the flag and count a flagged packet as cut; no container sets it yet, so nothing changes), then the containers, each with its goldens. The container commits change goldens in the folders of the codecs that sit in containers (`mp4`, `matroska`, `ogg`, `asf`, and `alac` and `ac3` for their MP4 and Matroska fixtures) wherever a packet is cut; `ac3/sample-short.mp4` is detected by the AC-3 parser itself and needs nothing.

## Cross-check statuses

The statuses of [official-decoder-crosschecks.md](official-decoder-crosschecks.md) ("Differences and decisions") follow from the definition by rule, so that steps 2 to 6 reach the same status for the same situation. The rules decided on 2026-11-02 on `ci/official-decode-aac` (b03bdfc) already say that a unit the analyzer flags and counts by its declared samples, which the reference refuses, outputs nothing for or conceals, is `explained`, and that references that disagree are `keep` unless a reading of the specification decides. This note supplies the figure those rules compare with. The comparison works at the coded stage: the reference's total is compared with the coded-stage figure less `LostSamples`. For Opus and Vorbis the harness records it as `decodedSamples` (the key of `official-decoders.json`, which [question 7](#open-questions) renames); the reader paths (`opusdec`, `oggdec`) give the trimmed stage, which the rules do not cover, since lost samples sit at the coded stage. A trimmed-stage reader total is compared with `samples` less the lost samples that lie inside the trimmed range; where that cannot be told, it is `noted`.

1. **A unit the analyzer counts by its declared samples and flags as lost, which the reference refuses or outputs nothing for:** `explained`. The comparison script marks it with no review per file when two things hold: the reference's total equals the coded stage less `LostSamples`, and the units the reference refuses are the lost units, matched by offset and length from the per-unit records (the equality can otherwise hold by coincidence). A merged finding is rule 3.
2. **The same unit, which the reference conceals:** the totals are equal, and the unit's verdict (concealed against lost) is `explained`, as the 2026-11-01 rule on concealment says. A reader that writes audio for a cut unit (`tta -d` writing a header's count over a cut) is this rule too.
3. **A finding that merges several units points to the first,** so later flagged units have no finding at their bytes: `noted`, as decided for the Ogg step. The lost samples still count them, since they come from the unit records and not from the findings.
4. **The official decoders of a format disagree among themselves:** the existing rule applies, `keep` unless a reading of the specification decides. The status stays the lead's: the comparison script marks the disagreement and picks the reference as it does today, and no rule here assigns `keep` by itself.
5. **The reference outputs fewer samples than the coded stage less `LostSamples`, or refuses a unit the analyzer does not flag:** `pending`, as today. The definition does not explain a decoder's own resync or a check the parser lacks.
6. **A reference that drops a unit whose checksum fails** (ffmpeg's `-err_detect crccheck` on AC-3, libogg dropping every page whose CRC fails, which all Ogg references do, FDK refusing a block): `noted`, with the reason "the reference drops a unit whose checksum fails; the analyzer counts it and reports the checksum". It is not rule 1, since a unit with a failing checksum whose syntax reads is not lost ([question 1](#open-questions)).

## Decisions

1. **One definition, declared samples, for every format.** Reason: the gaps rule already counts an undecodable frame's samples as lost, and a figure that means the same thing in every format lets the cross-check statuses follow by rule. Alternative: count only decodable units, which makes `samples` depend on a decoder the analyzer does not have for most lossy formats.
2. **Lost samples as a subset of `samples`, at the coded stage.** Reason: the invariant with the decoded samples, and trims stay one rule per format. Alternative: lost samples at the trimmed stage, which needs every trim rule twice.
3. **Trims kept where they are today, and made explicit.** Reason: the codec-defined trims are part of the decoding process, the container's and encoder's are presentation; changing either is a separate decision with its own goldens. Alternative: always report the coded stage as `samples`, which changes every Opus, Vorbis, Speex, Musepack and WMA golden.
4. **Frame counts and bitrates keep their basis in each format, and the pull request holds it with a test.** Reason: they describe the bytes read, and the change stays in `samples`, `duration` and the new figure.
5. **The name `LostSamples` for the stream and for the measurements, with two documented bases and distinct JSON positions.** Reason: the same concept at two stages, and no schema change. Alternatives in [question 8](#open-questions).
6. **`lostSamples` is written only above 0.** Reason: it leaves the goldens of undamaged streams untouched. Alternative: write it always, with a change entry for every folder.
7. **A packet's cut is a flag on `MappedBytes`, set by the container.** Reason: a codec parser cannot see the cut of a packet that carries no length, and the container is the only one that knows its stated size.

## Open questions

1. **Does a unit whose checksum fails but whose syntax reads count as lost?**
   - (a) No: it counts; the checksum tallies of `StreamVerification` and the findings report it. A checksum that guards syntax the parser needs, after whose failure the parser stops reading the unit (TrueHD major sync CRC and parity), makes the unit unreadable and lost. The rule is "the parser refuses to read it", which also fits the TrueHD figures above.
   - (b) Yes: the AC-3 cross-check holds FFmpeg to refusing exactly the damaged frames, those failing a CRC among them, and the analyzer's own `ac3.crc1-mismatch` message says that decoders that check crc1 drop the frame.
   - (c) Per format, by what the owner's decoder does.
   - Recommendation: (a). A failed CRC says the audio may differ, which the verification already says; and a reference that drops such a unit gets `noted` by status rule 6 (the Ogg page CRC included: libogg drops the page and every Ogg reference follows), a rule written for exactly this.
2. **Do units with no byte in the file count?** MP4 samples wholly outside the file (`chunk-outside-file.m4a`) or of 0 bytes, Monkey's Audio and TTA frames past a cut. (The samples past `mp4.samples-overlap` have bytes and are rule 6.)
   - (a) No: a unit with no byte in the file is not in the stream. The findings and the header nodes carry the claim (`mp4.sample-outside-file`, `tta.frames-missing`, `monkeys-audio.data-cut`). Monkey's Audio changes from the header's total to the frames present, as FLAC and WavPack count today.
   - (b) Yes, as lost: MP4 by the `stts` durations, Monkey's Audio and TTA by the header, so a cut file reports its original length.
   - (c) Only MP4, whose sample table lists each unit, while the totals of TTA and Monkey's Audio stay claims.
   - Recommendation: (a). It needs no rule for which totals to trust, and the claim stays visible next to the count.
3. **Are MP4 samples outside every mdat declared samples?**
   - (a) Yes, counted as today, lost only where their codec parser flags them (`sample-outside-mdat.m4a`: the 10 samples with AAC syntax findings).
   - (b) Lost as a whole, since their bytes are not media data.
   - Recommendation: (a). The table declares them and the codec parser reads what they hold.
4. **How does a declaration the bytes contradict count?** Two cases: a frame whose bytes are complete (its length is satisfied) but whose header declares more ADTS raw data blocks than it holds (`aac.blocks-missing`), and a unit cut by the end of the data whose declared length runs past it (`aac.frame-truncated`).
   - (a) Trust the declaration in both: `blocks-missing.aac` becomes 40,960 with 30,720 lost, 75 % of a file whose frames are whole, and a future AAC decoder would output 1,024 and leave a gap of 3,072 per frame. FDK equals the analyzer's 10,240 today, decoding the one block each frame holds.
   - (b) Separate them: a declaration contradicted by complete bytes counts what the bytes hold and is not lost (`aac.blocks-missing` as today); a unit cut by the end counts its declared samples and is lost.
   - (c) Never count a cut unit.
   - Recommendation: (b). FDK's behavior is evidence for it, and the line between a wrong declaration and missing data is the bytes present.
5. **Do missing samples between units count as lost?** Positions shown by FLAC sample numbers, WavPack block indexes or granule positions that jump, and packets lost with a damaged Ogg page.
   - (a) No: they are neither samples nor lost; the findings carry their size, and the measurements count them as their gaps.
   - (b) Yes: `LostSamples` adds them, so it equals the measurements' figure on every decoded stream, but it is then no subset of `samples`.
   - Recommendation: (a). It keeps the invariant `0 ≤ LostSamples ≤ coded stage`; (b) would make lost samples depend on which formats number their units.
6. **Measuring lost units at the start and the end (required by the decision).** The measurer counts a gap only when a block's position jumps (`StreamMeasurer`), so a lost first or last unit is in no measured figure, and `SampleLocator` shifts.
    - (a) The host gives the measurer the stream's coded-stage start and end from the `CodedFrame`s, so leading and trailing lost units count in the measured `lostSamples`; measurement goldens of decoded cut fixtures change (the damaged TrueHD fixtures, `flac/built-cut-mid-frame.flac`).
    - (b) Leave it and change `SampleLocator` to add the stream's `lostSamples`.
    - Recommendation: (a), in the same pull request; the text above calls it "question 6 (a)".
7. **One name for the coded stage?** "Decoded Samples" (Opus, Vorbis) and "Coded Samples" (Speex, Musepack, and now WMA and TrueHD) name the same concept; "Decoded Samples" also collides with `StreamVerification.DecodedSamples` (JSON `decodedSamples`, the key `official-decoders.json` uses for Opus and Vorbis), which means the samples a decoder actually output, and which the analyzer does for neither format.
   - (a) "Coded Samples" everywhere, with the `official-decoders.json` key renamed.
   - (b) Keep both.
   - Recommendation: (a), as its own pull request right after this one so that this one's golden changes stay readable; this note uses "Coded Samples" meanwhile.
8. **How do the stream's and the measured lost samples relate?** The stream's is the declared samples of lost units; the measured one includes missing samples and today leaves out a lost first or last unit.
   - (a) One name, two documented bases and JSON positions (the choice made above).
   - (b) Split the measured figure into lost units and missing samples, so the shared name means one thing (a change of `measurements`).
   - (c) A distinct name for the stream's figure.
   - Recommendation: (a), with question 6 (a), so that the two agree in the common case.
9. **Is a started and unfinished WMA frame lost?** `wma.frame-unfinished` on `cut-open-3` and `cut-open-27`.
   - (a) It counts F and is lost; the two `keep` entries stay with larger differences to Windows (7,104 against 8,192, 55,808 against 57,344).
   - (b) An explicit exception: it counts nothing, because the frame's length cannot be told.
   - Recommendation: (a), the same rule as every other format.
10. **Which AAC codes make a raw data block lost?** Proposed from the goldens, for the lead to confirm:
    - lost (syntax breaks; the six codes of `corrupt-syntax.aac`, where FDK conceals six units): `aac.section-overrun`, `aac.element-past-end`, `aac.codebook-reserved`, `aac.prediction-not-allowed`, `aac.max-sfb-too-large`, `aac.escape-too-long`;
    - not lost (soft findings, the codes of `corrupt-soft.aac`, where FDK conceals three units and decodes the rest): `aac.ics-reserved-bit`, `aac.elements-mismatch`, `aac.ms-mask-reserved`, `aac.pulse-in-short-window`, `aac.scalefactor-out-of-range`, `aac.tns-order-too-high`, `aac.fill-data-invalid`, `aac.gain-control-not-allowed`, `aac.intensity-without-common-window`, `aac.crc-mismatch` (question 1).
    - Recommendation: as listed; the three soft units FDK conceals stay `noted` under rule 3 or 5 until the pull request reads which codes they carry.
11. **Do Speex packets without frames count?** An empty packet and a packet with `speex.missing-frames` count the header's frames per packet today.
    - (a) They count the header's samples and are lost (empty: decoders treat it as lost and fill in the gap, the parser's own finding).
    - (b) An empty packet declares nothing (rule 3); a packet with missing frames counts the frames present.
    - Recommendation: (a) for the empty packet, (b) for missing frames, since the packet then holds frames of its own.
12. **TrueHD units that are not supported or skipped after junk.** `truehd.unsupported` units (not damaged, not read) and `truehd.data-skipped` units (intact, skipped).
    - (a) Unsupported units count and are lost (they are units of the stream whose declaration the parser can read, but whose audio it does not); skipped units after junk count nothing (rule 3), as bytes skipped while searching for sync.
    - (b) Neither counts.
    - Recommendation: (a).
13. **The JSON form of `lostSamples`:** above 0 only (the choice made here) or always (full golden churn: a change entry in every folder).
    - Recommendation: above 0 only; the same reading as `multilingualChannels` and `bitsPerSample`.
14. **Is a cut unit lost when the parser can show its coded audio is complete?** The cut is decided by the declared length against the bytes present (rule 2). That overstates in two kinds of file: encoder-written MPEG files a byte or two short (`external-shine-joint.mp3`, 522 of 523 bytes; `external-blade080-stereo.mp3`, 405 of 418), whose missing bytes may be padding or ancillary data past the Layer III main data, which the parser can locate from the granules' part2_3_length; and three files where only the declaration is wrong, so the next unit starts at the end of the bytes present (`mpeg/damaged-bitrate.mp3`, `adts/damaged-length.aac`, `dts/fsize-too-large.dts`), where the parser cannot tell a cut from a damaged length (`cut-frame-stream-change.mp3` looks the same).
   - (a) Cut means lost, the figure's description says "cut", and it makes no claim about the audio.
   - (b) Refine per format where the parser reads far enough (the Layer III main data end, the AAC END element, FLAC subframes, an ALAC element): a cut that leaves the coded audio complete counts and is not lost. This changes six MPEG goldens (the four encoder-written files, `vbr/lame-truncated-last-frame.mp3` and `truncated-last-frame.mp3` stay lost).
   - Recommendation: (a) in this pull request, (b) as a later per-format refinement with its own goldens.
15. **The frame count and bitrate basis:** per format as today, with a test that they do not change (the choice made here), or one common basis with the extra golden changes listed. Recommendation: per format as today.
16. **How does a codec parser learn that a routed packet is cut?** The containers know (a stated size against the bytes present); `MappedBytes` has no member for it.
    - (a) Two members on `MappedBytes`, `IsCut` and `DeclaredLength`, set by the container ([What containers hand over](#what-containers-hand-over)); parsers that see the cut themselves ignore them.
    - (b) The container reports a lost packet to the host with its stated size, and the host adds the declared samples: no codec parser changes, but the host would need each codec's samples per packet.
    - (c) Do not count a cut packet of a codec whose parser cannot see the cut (MP4, Matroska, Ogg and ASF then keep leaving them out).
    - Recommendation: (a). It is the only option that gives the codec parser's declaration (a TOC, a mode, a cookie) for the cut packet; it is a change in Core.Abstractions that the lead decides.
