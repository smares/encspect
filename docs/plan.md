## Goals and non-goals

The tool tells you everything technically knowable about an audio file's structure, and every value it reports points back to the bytes it came from. It is a GUI and CLI analyzer for people who want EncSpot-level detail, across many more formats.

**Goals**

- Deep header, metadata and structure analysis: encoder detection, bitrate behavior, bit reservoir usage, VBR headers, and all tag formats.
- Every reported value carries its byte offset and length, so the GUI can show a structure tree next to a hex view and the CLI can emit text or JSON from the same data.
- Broad format coverage, with one self-contained parser per format.
- Damaged or odd files produce findings, never crashes.
- One codebase for Windows, macOS and Linux on x64 and ARM64.

**Non-goals**

- Audio playback.
- Runtime-loadable plugins. Formats are separate projects, composed at build time.
- Editing or writing tags.
- Decrypting protected content: protection is reported, no key is used and no license acquired.
- Bundling external decoders or native audio libraries.
  - The one exception is what the GUI framework itself needs: Skia and HarfBuzz, and the platform backends (ANGLE, `av_libglesv2`, on Windows; Avalonia.Native, `libAvaloniaNative`, on macOS).
  - Linux needs none beyond Skia and HarfBuzz (see [gui.md](gui.md)).
- Quality verdicts. The report says what the file shows, never whether it sounds good: no rating from an encoder's reputation or the bitrate, such as EncSpot's good, ok or bad.

### Breadth and depth

The work grows along two axes (the lead, 2026-11-08).

- **Breadth:** the formats and containers Encspect reads.
- **Depth:** how far into a format it sees, in five levels:
  1. container structure: boxes, chunks, pages, packets and frames;
  2. codec syntax: headers, side information and the frame-level fields;
  3. entropy-coded data: the Huffman, Golomb, range or run-level coded values (the entropy pass);
  4. decoded audio: verification of the checksums over it, and the measurements;
  5. interpretation: statistics, coding graphs and encoder detection.
- v1 stopped at level 2 for MPEG audio. AAC's and WMA's parsers walk their entropy-coded data already; for the other lossy formats the entropy pass comes with the decoder.

**The gap rule in detail** (the lead, 2026-11-01, #208):

- The decoder reports the frame (`decode.frame-invalid`) and outputs nothing for it; the measurements count its positions as lost samples.
- After the gap, a lossy decoder resets and withholds the output that depends on the lost state, reported as withheld samples apart from lost samples (decision 4 of [lossy-decoders.md](design/lossy-decoders.md#decisions), 2026-11-08).
- The positions of a lost first or last unit count too: the host gives the measurer the span of the units the parser handed over.
- The stream's own lost samples, from the parser without decoding, are another figure at the coded stage ([samples-definition.md](design/samples-definition.md)).
- Where an official decoder conceals the frame (FDK AAC fills it with noise substitution), its output covers positions Encspect's decoder leaves empty; the official-decoder cross-checks record such a difference as `explained`.

**The routes decision in detail** (the lead, 2026-11-07):

- A combination nobody writes (WavPack in ASF and the like) stays at container level: the track is named, and the `codec-not-analyzed` finding stays.
- The planned routes are in [0.4](#04-free-decoders-and-routes-complete), each with the muxer that writes it where one is known; `A_MS/ACM` in Matroska waits there for a real file.
- Dolby TrueHD as IEC 61937 MAT bursts in WAV was not planned at first; the lead moved it to [after 0.5](#after-05), item 9, on 2026-11-17, once the TrueHD parser existed. Until then the bursts stay named and timed.
- The table of every combination is in [formats/README.md](formats/README.md#codecs-in-containers).

### Working rules

The lead, 2026-11-08:

- Items run in parallel where that is efficient (the lead, 2026-11-19): waiting for a pipeline is not work, so agents keep working on the plan while one runs.
- A new idea goes into a later milestone, not into the current one, unless the lead says otherwise.
- Every parked item has a milestone, or sits under [Not planned](#not-planned) with the trigger that would bring it back.
- A pause the lead asks for keeps the item's place and priority, and the item resumes first.
- A format's `pending` differences from the official decoders go to the lead with the pull request that commits its `official-decoders.json`; the analyzer changes that follow are their own pull requests, each with its goldens ([official-decoder-crosschecks.md](design/official-decoder-crosschecks.md#rollout), step 8). A total that is no reference is `keep`, never `pending`.
- When a format's decoder starts, check whether the OxideAV project ([github.com/OxideAV](https://github.com/OxideAV), MIT, written from the specifications under a clean-room rule stricter than ours) has a crate for the format. Its notes' measurements against other decoders are input for the design, and its decoder can serve as one more black-box cross-check. Agreement with it is weak evidence, since it is also written with Claude and can share a misreading; a disagreement points to a passage to reread. Code that follows it closely carries its MIT notice (the lead, 2026-11-13).

## Milestones

Each milestone is something a user can do that they could not do before. Its items come in the order listed.

- **Status markers:** a marker stands in parentheses right after an item's bold title, with the origin of the item (who asked and when) before it, separated by a semicolon.
  - `(done, YYYY-MM-DD, #N)`: finished; the date is the merge date of the pull request that finished it, and several pull requests read `#a, #b and #c`, or `#a to #d` for a run.
  - `(in progress)`: started and not finished; the parts say what is done.
  - No marker: not started.
- **The gate:** a milestone ends with the independent reviews of its work ([architecture.md, "Verification"](architecture.md#verification)), their findings fixed, and the push and nightly runs green on every platform.
- **The release:** a passed gate is tagged `v0.N.0` and released ([development.md, "Releases"](development.md#releases)).
- **0.1.0** was released after Gate 2; Gate 3 and the work after it lead into 0.2 ([history.md](history.md)).
- **0.2.0** follows Gate 0.2, passed on 2026-11-10 ([history.md](history.md#gates-in-detail)); its notes are [releases/0.2.0.md](releases/0.2.0.md).
- **0.3.0** follows Gate 0.3, passed on 2026-11-16 ([history.md](history.md#gates-in-detail)); its notes are [releases/0.3.0.md](releases/0.3.0.md).

### 0.2: Lossless complete

Every lossless format with a decoder design is decoded and measured, except WMA Lossless (0.5) and DTS-HD's lossless extension (a follow-up after 0.5). Files that lossyWAV processed are recognized, and TAK and OptimFROG files are named.

**Status:** done; the gate passed on 2026-11-10 at a858ea0b, with seven independent reviews and their fixes (#254 to #260).

1. **The lossy groundwork** (done, 2026-11-08, #232): determinism (`DetMath`, the scan and its fixes), withheld output in the contract and the measurer, and the PCM comparison tools.
   - It is step 0 of the [lossy decoders' rollout](design/lossy-decoders.md#rollout).
2. **lossyWAV and lossyFLAC detection** (the lead, 2026-11-08; done, 2026-11-09, #239, #240, #242 and #243): files that lossyWAV processed are detected, with priority.
   - **Priority:** it was paused only to save tokens and was never meant to be postponed (the lead, 2026-11-08).
   - **Design:** [lossywav-detection.md](design/lossywav-detection.md), signed off by the lead on 2026-11-08 with 13 decisions, from files lossyWAV 1.4.2 wrote in CI (used only as a black-box tool).
   - **What the files show:** lossyWAV marks its WAV output with a `fact` chunk naming itself and its settings, which lossyFLAC pipelines usually drop. It zeroes low bits in blocks of 512 samples at 44.1 and 48 kHz, counted per channel unless `--linkchannels` is set.
   - **Pull requests, in order:** PR 0 (#239), the WAV parser's fixes (the misread `fact` chunk and a false `wav.truncated`); PR 1 (#240), the marker and a "Processed by" claim; PR 2 (#242), the measurement of zeroed low bits; PR 3 (#243), the wasted-bits statistic in FLAC and WavPack and the claim from behavior.
   - **Reported** as figures with their basis and as a claim with a confidence. No finding says a file is not lossless.
3. **The ALAC and TTA decoders** (the lead, 2026-11-08; done, 2026-11-09, #245 and #246): small and exact, checked bit for bit; steps L1 and L2 of [lossy-decoders.md, "Order"](design/lossy-decoders.md#order).
   - **ALAC** (done, 2026-11-09, #245): the decoder follows Apple's reference decoder and is checked sample for sample against it ([alac.md](formats/alac.md#decoding)).
   - **TTA** (done, 2026-11-09, #246): the author's public pages describe only the container, so the decoder follows a clean-room description of libtta ([tta-format.md](design/tta-format.md)) and is checked sample for sample against the reference `tta` and FFmpeg ([tta.md](formats/tta.md)).
4. **TAK and OptimFROG at header level** (done, 2026-11-10, #249 to #251): both are poorly documented, so they get only what their headers reveal ([planned.md](formats/planned.md#in-02-header-level-only)).
   - **Encoder settings:** where a header stores them (OptimFROG's mode and speedup, TAK's preset), they are read and named, as Monkey's Audio's compression level and WavPack's mode flags are; whether each header holds them is checked against a source that can be verified and against files the encoders write (the lead, 2026-11-08).
   - **Design:** [tak-optimfrog-headers.md](design/tak-optimfrog-headers.md), decided by the lead on 2026-11-09.
   - **OptimFROG** (done, 2026-11-09, #249): the parser, fixtures and cross-check ([optimfrog.md](formats/optimfrog.md)). The lead decided (2026-11-09): an `.ofc` is detected by its structure (a chain of `CORR` blocks whose CRC-32s hold) and named "OptimFROG correction data"; an `.ofs` is named "OptimFROG" with a low-confidence claim of the lossy part of a DualStream from the data blocks' reserved bytes, raised to medium when the file name ends in `.ofs`; `OFRX` and `DETA` are read as what `off` writes.
   - **TAK** (done, 2026-11-10, #250 and #251): the parser, fixtures and cross-check ([tak.md](formats/tak.md)): the metadata objects with their CRC-24s, the stream info, the encoder info and preset, the stored WAV header and the end the last frame info gives. The frames are not walked (0.4, item 3) and the codec 3 of the 2.1 betas is named without a claim (the lead, 2026-11-09).
   - **OptimFROG DualStream** (the lead, 2026-11-09; done, 2026-11-09, #249): an `.ofs` is named "OptimFROG" with a claim that it is the lossy part of a DualStream (low confidence from the data blocks' reserved bytes, medium when the file name ends in `.ofs`), and a correction file opened alone is "OptimFROG correction data". Finding the correction file beside the `.ofs` and checking the pair waits for 0.4, item 3, with WavPack's `.wvc`.
5. **Two level figures** (done, 2026-11-10, #252 and #253): the figures from step 6 of [spectral-analysis.md](design/spectral-analysis.md#rollout) that need no lossy decoder: a minimum length for the stretches that count as silence, and clipping at the extremes of the bits in use (16-bit audio padded to 24 bits tops out at 0x7FFF00, so only its negative runs count today).
   - **Design:** [Step 6: silence stretches and clipping at the bits in use](design/spectral-analysis.md#step-6-silence-stretches-and-clipping-at-the-bits-in-use), with four decisions for the lead, all taken as recommended (2026-11-10): the minimum of 10 ms applies at the start and end too, clipping counts at the bits in use from 8 bits in use, the CSV gains `Clipping Runs` and `Digital Silence Stretches`, and floats on the 16-bit and 24-bit grids are included.
   - [As built: step 6 levels](design/spectral-analysis.md#as-built-step-6-levels).

### 0.3: Lossy formats measured

The common lossy formats are decoded, so their files get the spectrum, cutoff, levels and loudness that lossless files have.

**Status:** done; the gate passed on 2026-11-16 at 752cd3f9, with five independent reviews (their fixes #310 to #314), a review of those fixes (its fixes #316) and the full nightly suite green on every platform.

1. **The decoders** (done, 2026-11-14, #267, #274, #282, #285 and #288): one pull request each, in the order of [lossy-decoders.md](design/lossy-decoders.md#order): MPEG audio (Layers I, II and III), Vorbis, Opus, AC-3, E-AC-3.
   - **Feed delay:** Opus in Matroska needs the Vorbis parser's feed delay for its DiscardPadding, and so does AAC in Matroska (item 1 of 0.5): the padding is the end trimming of the track's last block and the decoder must be told before the frames it takes samples from. The Vorbis follow-up keeps it in the Vorbis parser, since moving it into the host's session for every codec (the session holds the last frames of a track whose configuration states a padding and its block's frame count) is a design change, to be decided with the Opus decoder.
   - **Definition of done** of each: the decoder, its format's entropy-pass figures, its coding graphs and its gap wave 3 items ([lossy-decoders.md, "Gap wave 3 figures this unlocks"](design/lossy-decoders.md#gap-wave-3-figures-this-unlocks)). Coding graphs and gap wave 3 are no separate threads.
   - **MPEG audio** (done, 2026-11-11, #267): the decoder for Layers I to III. [As built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-mpeg-audio).
     - The Layer III entropy pass in every analysis with "Coded Bandwidth", and the frame CRC-16 declared as a header checksum.
     - The PCM cross-checks against minimp3, FFmpeg and ISO/IEC 13818-4's reference output.
     - **The intensity stereo bound** (the lead; done, 2026-11-13, #282 and #283): one numeric series, "Intensity Stereo Bound" in Hz on the axis of "Coded Bandwidth", the same name, unit and axis in every format with the concept, with a statistics node of the same name (lowest and highest hertz): Layers I and II (the bound subband of the mode extension, as hertz, next to the existing "Intensity Stereo Bounds" categories), Layer III (the lowest line from which a granule's right channel codes intensity positions, which the walk returns in `WalkFacts`), AAC (the lowest intensity stereo band of the frame) and Opus CELT (its intensity band, built with the Opus decoder), with their goldens ([coding-graphs.md](design/coding-graphs.md)).
   - **Vorbis** (done, 2026-11-12, #274): the decoder for floors 0 and 1 and residues 0 to 2, the floor and residue entropy pass in every analysis with "Coded Bandwidth" (statistics and series), the granule positions' trims reaching the measurer, and the PCM cross-check against libvorbis and FFmpeg. [As built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-vorbis).
     - The coded bandwidth stays residue-only (the lead, 2026-11-11).
     - FFmpeg's native decoder disagrees with libvorbis on the one stream FFmpeg's own encoder wrote, for a cause not established; libvorbis, Xiph's reference decoder, stays the reference (the lead, 2026-11-15).
   - **Opus** (done, 2026-11-12, #282): the decoder for SILK, CELT and hybrid frames, redundant frames, multistream mappings and the output gain. [As built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-opus).
     - Mode-change concealment (libopus's 5 ms cross-fade at a change between CELT and the SILK or hybrid modes, which RFC 6716 only recommends); a hybrid frame whose redundancy overruns its data is `opus.redundancy-overrun` and its packet is not decoded (libopus ignores the redundancy and conceals the CELT part; nothing is concealed here).
     - The entropy pass in every analysis with the final range coder state of every frame, CELT's "Block Sizes", "Stereo Coding" and "Intensity Stereo Bound".
     - The pre-skip and end trimming (the Matroska DiscardPadding through the feed delay, and an end trimming that reaches back over earlier packets) as trimmed ranges, which take precedence over withheld and lost spans; Opus in MP4 routed (the `Opus` sample entry and its `dOps` box, with the end trimming taken from what the track's samples hold after its one media edit).
     - The cross-checks against libopus 1.5.2 and RFC 8251's test vectors.
   - **AC-3** (done, 2026-11-13, #285): the decoder for AC-3 (bsid up to 8, Annex D's syntax included) written from A/52:2018 alone: mantissa dequantization with a fixed-seed dither, coupling, rematrixing, the transforms of 512 and 256 points with the Kaiser-Bessel derived window and the overlap; no dynamic range control or downmix. [As built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-ac-3).
     - The parser reads every mantissa code in every analysis (checks 18 to 20 of A/52 7.10.2, `ac-3.block-inconsistent`) and declares crc1 and crc2, so a stream whose frames all pass reads "verified (lossy stream)"; a lost frame is a gap, and the frame after it withholds its first block (256 samples).
     - No trims (the syntax states none and edit lists are not applied).
     - The PCM cross-check against liba52 and FFmpeg (`crosscheck_ac3_pcm.py`: every file agrees with liba52 to 2.4 x 10^-7 of full scale with the dither flags cleared; dithered files by band energy); Core Audio (the macOS job) and Dolby's decoder (on the lead's notebook, 2026-11-14 and 15) ran as for E-AC-3 below, and [development.md](development.md#e-ac-3-decoding) records what each does.
   - **E-AC-3** (done, 2026-11-14, #288): the decoder for the independent substreams of E-AC-3 (every substream ID its own program and stream) written from A/52:2018 Annex E, on AC-3's foundation. [As built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-e-ac-3).
     - The adaptive hybrid transform (vector and gain adaptive quantization with the inverse DCT across the six blocks), spectral extension with its noise blend and notch filter, enhanced coupling, standard coupling and rematrixing as in AC-3; no dynamic range control or downmix, and transient pre-noise processing with the Hanning windows A/52:2018 Annex E 3.7.2 names.
     - The dependent substreams' channels (of an E-AC-3 core and of an AC-3 core alike) are decoded and joined to the program's layout by the channel map (Annex E 2.8, 3.8), so a 7.1 program gives its eight channels.
     - **Enhanced coupling follows A/52:2018** (the lead, 2026-11-14): its angles and chaos values, which ETSI TS 102 366 V1.4.1 leaves reserved; Dolby's decoder reads it as V1.4.1 does, amplitude-only, and the choice of A/52:2018 stands.
     - The entropy pass reads every block of every E-AC-3 frame, the adaptive hybrid transform's values and enhanced coupling's parameters included, in every analysis; the one CRC is declared as the stream's checksum.
     - Hand-built files with content (`eac3-signal-aht`, `-spx`, `-ecpl`), the FFmpeg-decoded real files of `check_eac3_samples.py` and FFmpeg's decoder as a black box; Core Audio (macOS job) and Dolby's decoder (on the lead's notebook, five runs) are cross-checks too: [development.md](development.md#e-ac-3-decoding) records what each does.
   - **Follow-up to the Vorbis decoder** (the lead, 2026-11-11; done, 2026-11-12, #279): Matroska's DiscardPadding as the end trimming of a Vorbis track (a one-packet feed delay in the parser, so `Trim` reaches the decoder before the last packet) and the mid-stream "Gap Trimming" as trimmed ranges of the measurements (`IDecodeSession.TrimInner`, "inner" trimmed samples), with Matroska fixtures that carry DiscardPadding ([as built](design/lossy-decoders.md#as-built-vorbis)).
     - Gap Trimming comes from Ogg granule positions; Matroska has none.
   - **The patent question** (the lead, 2026-11-13): decision 17 of [lossy-decoders.md](design/lossy-decoders.md#decisions) is decided: the E-AC-3, HE-AAC, USAC and WMA Professional decoders are built and included in the release binaries.
   - **The official-decoder cross-checks of AC-3 and E-AC-3** (done, 2026-11-14, #285 and #288): rollout steps 5 and 7 left them open ([official-decoder-crosschecks.md](design/official-decoder-crosschecks.md)); they come with these decoders: the AC-3 variants (rules in `derive_variants.py`), the downloaded E-AC-3 samples (`eac3-samples.json`), Core Audio's independent substream 1 and dependent substreams (`substreams.eac3`), and the comparison of the PCM.
   - **Rollout step 7, AC-3 and E-AC-3 against Dolby's decoder on Windows** (the lead, 2026-11-11; done, 2026-11-15, #315, the last run): it comes with the decoders too.
     - The runner images have no Dolby decoder, but the lead's Windows 10 22H2 notebook has "Microsoft Dolby Digital Plus Decoder MFT" (`DolbyDecMFT.dll` 10.0.19041.7844, input AC-3, the AC-3 WAVE tag and E-AC-3; the probe's inventory created it).
     - The probe and the files are run there by hand, and the record states that machine's build and DLL versions in place of a runner image's.
2. **The measurements' later steps** (done, 2026-11-15, #299, #305 and #308): step 6 of [spectral-analysis.md](design/spectral-analysis.md#rollout), designed in [Step 7](design/spectral-analysis.md#step-7-spectral-bandwidth-partial-cutoff-midside-spectra-and-spectral-holes) (approved by the lead, 2026-11-14), in two changes after the decoders above.

13. **Inter-channel coherence of decoded audio** (the lead, 2026-11-14, in place of decision S10's "Not planned" for it), after the mid/side spectra of 0.3 item 2, whose pair it uses: per frequency row, the magnitude-squared coherence of the pair (front left and right, or the two channels of a stream without a layout): the averaged cross-spectrum, squared, over the product of the two power spectra, from 0 to 1.
    - **What it shows:** above an intensity stereo bound the decoder rebuilds both channels from one signal, so their coherence is about 1 there, where real stereo recordings stay well below it. The figure is where the coherence rises to near 1 and stays there, and the share of frames that show it, with a unit and a basis, never a verdict about the audio.
    - **For lossy streams** it cross-checks the parsers' "Intensity Stereo Bound"; **for decoded lossless streams** it is evidence that the audio passed through intensity stereo coding, worded as the spectral cutoff is.
    - **Cost:** one running cross-spectrum per row (512 complex accumulators) from the bins the mid/side spectra already hold, under the measurements' determinism rules.

- **An OptimFROG decoder** (the lead, 2026-11-09): the codec is closed and no open implementation exists, not even under the LGPL; its published `format.txt` covers the container only, which is what the header-level parser reads. Reverse-engineering the binaries is neither allowed by its license nor a source that can be checked. Until then OptimFROG streams are checked by their block CRC-32s, and a DualStream pair is judged on headers and checksums alone. **Trigger:** its author publishes the coding or an open implementation appears.
- **Each of the many ADPCM and PCM variants as a format of its own.** **Reason:** WAV and the containers name their codings.
- **MXF and AVI.** **Reason:** both are video containers: MXF is SMPTE's container for professional video, and AVI holds mostly video.
- **Codec and container combinations no known writer produces.** **Reason:** routing what nobody writes costs code and fixtures for no file (see the routes decision above).
